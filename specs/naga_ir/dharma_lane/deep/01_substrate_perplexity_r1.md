## 0. Reframing before answering

Before working through §3.1–3.8, I want to challenge the framing itself. This is not a rhetorical move; it changes what the rest of the document does.

**Claim R0.** *"Substrate" is a category the language should be built to refuse to answer at the layer where the question is usually asked.* [Confidence 74/100.]

The prompt asks: what are the smallest things the language can talk about, and what are their intrinsic vs. relational properties? This question presupposes that the language *has* a bottom, and that its ontology *terminates* at that bottom. Every major position it lists — Vasubandhu's rejection of ultimate atoms, Jain paramāṇu, Sarvāstivāda dharmas-with-svabhāva, Sautrāntika kṣaṇika streams, Zurek's decoherence-selected pointer basis, Rovelli's relational facts — is a *different answer* to the same badly-framed question. The Madhyamaka move, executed correctly, is to notice that the question itself carries the reification it purports to resolve.

Nāgārjuna's argument in *MMK* ch. 1 (*pratyayaparīkṣā*) and ch. 15 (*svabhāvaparīkṣā*) is precisely that any candidate for "the ultimate constituent" must either (a) have svabhāva — intrinsic own-being — in which case it cannot participate in dependent origination and therefore cannot enter into any causal or compositional relation; or (b) lack svabhāva, in which case it is not ultimate.[^mmk1] The trap is *linguistic-metaphysical*, not empirical. A language whose ontology is generated by a "what are the ultimate constituents" question inherits the trap.

[^mmk1]: MMK 1.1 (*na svato nāpi parato na dvābhyāṃ nāpy ahetutaḥ / utpannā jātu vidyante bhāvāḥ kva cana ke cana*) and MMK 15.1–2 (*na saṃbhavaḥ svabhāvasya yuktaḥ pratyayahetubhiḥ / hetupratyayasaṃbhūtaḥ svabhāvaḥ kṛtako bhavet*). Siderits-Katsura (2013) close reading pp. 17–24, 152–160. The Prāsaṅgika reading — Candrakīrti's *Prasannapadā* ad loc. — is that Nāgārjuna is *not* offering an alternative substrate but refusing the substrate move altogether. Tsongkhapa's *Lam Rim Chen Mo* insists the negation is of *inherent* existence, not of conventional existence — critical for what follows. [Sender's marked-strong area; I trust my citations here.]

**Operational consequence for the language (R0).** The substrate layer should not be primitive in the sense of "irreducible objects with properties". It should be primitive in the sense of "the layer at which composition becomes definable". These are *different primitivities*. The first commits you to a category of *things*; the second commits you to a category of *compositions*, with things falling out as invariants under composition — closer to how objects in a symmetric monoidal category are defined by what tensors with what and produces what, not by intrinsic identity.[^cattheory] This is a *categorical* rather than *set-theoretic* primitivity.

[^cattheory]: Abramsky & Coecke, "A categorical semantics of quantum protocols," LICS 2004, esp. §3. The morphisms are load-bearing; the objects are placeholders for what morphisms can be composed. Coecke & Kissinger, *Picturing Quantum Processes* (CUP 2017), ch. 3–4 make this explicit at the string-diagram level: the diagram is primary, the "wires" (objects) are what remains once you have specified the diagram grammar. This is philosophically closer to Nāgārjuna than to any atomism.

So the substrate question, correctly re-posed, is: **what is the smallest thing the language can compose *with*, and what algebraic structure does composition carry?** The candidates A–H in the prompt are answers to two different questions, and part of the disagreement between them is a category error. I will now work through them keeping this reframing available; some collapse under it, some do not.

## 1. The eight candidates, re-sorted

The prompt's A–H is a mixed list: some are *ontological* claims about what exists, some are *epistemological* claims about how observation works, some are *dynamical* claims about what changes. Sorting them:

- **Ontological**: A (Vasubandhu/no ultimate atoms), B (Jain paramāṇu with qualities), G (SMC/tensor structure of compound systems). These are *what there is* claims.
- **Temporal-dynamical**: C (Sarvāstivāda tri-temporal), D (Sautrāntika momentariness), E (Kālacakra kṣaṇa). These are *what happens across time* claims.
- **Epistemic/relational**: F (Zurek decoherence, environmentally-selected classicality), H (Rovelli RQM, observer-relative facts). These are *how does the world present itself to a subsystem* claims.

Cross-category compatibility matters. A + G is nearly forced (rejection of atomism naturally lands in SMC where compound systems are tensor products rather than sums). D + F is nearly forced (momentariness + decoherence-selected pointer basis both say only-the-present-and-only-relative-to-environment is real, past traced out). C + H is *not* forced but is coherent (past facts persist, but only relative to observers who witnessed them — Sarvāstivāda plus RQM). B is peculiar in that it sits in a corner by itself: it asserts intrinsic qualities on atoms while accepting that only compositions have observable manifestation. That combination is unusual and, I will argue, more useful than it looks.

## 2. §3.1 The atomism decision

### 2.1 Vasubandhu's argument, verse by verse

This is a sender-marked-thin area for you and marked-thin-but-fresh for me (I just re-read Silk's 2018 critical edition[^silk] and the surrounding secondary literature). I will lay it out carefully because the language design pivots on which reading you accept.

[^silk]: Jonathan A. Silk, *Materials Toward the Study of Vasubandhu's Viṁśikā (I): Sanskrit and Tibetan Critical Editions of the Verses and Autocommentary; An English Translation and Annotations*, Harvard Oriental Series 81 (Harvard 2016/2018). This is the current textual bedrock; supersedes Lévi 1925 as the authoritative Sanskrit witness. The verse numbering I use follows Silk. Earlier numbering (Anacker's *Seven Works of Vasubandhu* 1984) shifts by one in places due to the treatment of the opening *prakaraṇa*.

The atomism refutation is *Viṃśikā* verses 11–15 (the *āyatana* section, on sense-spheres and their alleged external objects). Silk's edition (following the Sanskrit as reconstructed from the Nepali manuscript photographed in Mimaki et al. 1989) reads as follows in argument-structure:

**v.11.** The object of a sense-perception (*viṣaya*) can be neither (i) a partless unity (Vaiśeṣika whole), nor (ii) a plurality of distinct atoms (since single atoms are imperceptible), nor (iii) an aggregation of atoms (*saṃhata*) — because atoms themselves cannot be established. This is the trichotomy Vasubandhu will refute exhaustively.

**v.12.** The six-directions argument. If a paramāṇu could simultaneously conjoin with six others (front/back/top/bottom/left/right), then it would have six parts (*ṣaḍaṃśatā*) — since "the region occupied by one cannot be occupied by another" (*ekasya deśe nāstyanyasyāvakāśaḥ* — Silk's reconstructed text). Alternatively, if the six occupy the same location, the aggregate reduces to a single atom's spatial extent, and no compound is visible. Both horns fail.

**v.13.** The Kāśmīra-Vaibhāṣika reply — atoms don't conjoin (being partless), but *aggregates* (*saṃghāta*) conjoin — is refuted: if atoms individually cannot conjoin because they are partless, aggregates of such partless atoms also cannot ground conjunction, because the aggregate has no parts *other than the atoms themselves*. (Ching 2019 gives an excellent formal reading of this move, distinguishing "accumulation₁" — mere collection — from "accumulation₂" — physical conjunction — and showing v.13 as an objection to what Van Inwagen would later call the *special composition question*'s series-style answers.)[^ching]

[^ching]: Ching Keng, "Vasubandhu's Refutation of the Aggregate of Atoms: a Reading Inspired by Van Inwagen's Objection to Series-Style Answers to the Special Composition Question," *Sophia* (2023). Also relevant: Kapstein (1988), the Ronkin (2005) chapter on Sautrāntika ontology, and Priest 2018 ch. 4.

**v.14.** The extension dilemma. If an atom is spatially extended (has *dig-bhāga*, direction-parts — a front region, a back region, etc.), then it is not partless, hence not ultimately atomic (contradiction). If it is *not* spatially extended, then it cannot cast a shadow, cannot be blocked by another atom, and — crucially — cannot combine with other extensionless atoms to yield an extended aggregate, since seven extensionless atoms at coincident points remain extensionless. Both horns fail.

**v.15.** Return to the partless-unity horn. If perception were of a partless whole (Vaiśeṣika *avayavin*), then piecemeal motion, occlusion, hidden interiors, and small barely-visible things would be inexplicable. Refuted.

**The formal shape.** The argument is a *reductio* on the assumption that perception has a mind-independent physical object. It exhausts the trichotomy (v.11), refutes each horn (v.12–15), and concludes that the perceptual object is *vijñapti-mātra* — representation-only. The move I want to flag: **Vasubandhu is not arguing there are no atoms full stop; he is arguing that atoms cannot be the objects of perception in any coherent sense, which within his Yogācāra framework equals arguing they cannot be extramentally real** (since to be real is to be causally-efficient — *arthakriyā-samartha*[^arthakriya] — and to be causally efficient is to be capable of grounding perception).

[^arthakriya]: The *arthakriyā* criterion is Sautrāntika-Yogācāra shared property. Dharmakīrti *Pramāṇavārttika* is where it becomes explicit doctrine, but Vasubandhu already operates with it in the *Abhidharmakośabhāṣya* — see the Sarvāstivāda-Vaibhāṣika defense at AKBh 5.25–26 where "existence" is defined as "having *kāritra*" (functionality). I am working from Ronkin (2005) ch. 4–5 and Cox (1988) on this. [Marked medium-shallow: I have read AKBh 5.25–26 in translation, not Sanskrit.]

### 2.2 What the argument does and does not force

The argument does *not* force pure relationalism at the substrate. It forces one of:

- **A-strict (Yogācāra idealist reading, "consciousness-only").** No external substrate; what appears as substrate is projection by *ālayavijñāna*. In multi-AI mesh terms: no receipt has intrinsic existence until an ingest-node projects it. The mesh state is a bundle of node-relative projections with no substrate underneath. (Your prompt's own suggested cash-out.)
- **A-weak (Madhyamaka reading, "conventional-only").** No ultimate atoms, but conventional (*saṃvṛti*) posit of dharmas is preserved for practical predication. Substrate exists conventionally, does not exist ultimately. The mesh state is conventionally coherent, ultimately empty of independent existence.
- **A-formal (categorical reading).** Vasubandhu's argument targets *set-theoretic* mereology — atoms as elements, compounds as set-unions, contact as spatial adjacency. The argument does not touch a *categorical* substrate where composition is tensor product and there is no set-of-atoms to begin with. This is the reading I find most productive.

**Claim 2.1.** *The strict-Yogācāra reading (A-strict) is not required by Vasubandhu's argument; it is one available conclusion, and probably not the strongest.* [Confidence 71/100.]

**Justification.** The argument refutes atoms-as-perceptual-objects. It does not refute atoms-as-composition-primitives-that-are-never-observed-alone. This distinction was not available to Vasubandhu because for him "to exist" means "to be an object of some cognition"; but it is available to us because we have SMC. In an SMC, the objects at the "bottom" of the type hierarchy are not observed as individuals — they are observed only through morphisms into and out of them, and in linear/quantum settings, they cannot be duplicated or freely accessed. This is *exactly* the property Vasubandhu's argument requires an atom to lack (to be partless and yet form compounds) — and SMC objects have it structurally.

Vasubandhu did not have this option and would have rejected it on Yogācāra grounds (existence = presence to consciousness). We have this option, and — critically — Vasubandhu's *argument-form* survives into it as a reason to *not* treat receipts as elements-of-a-set.

**Operational consequence.** Receipts should be defined *implicitly* by their morphism-behavior — what can compose with what, what conjugations preserve them, what invariants they carry through pipelines — not *explicitly* by intrinsic fields. Content-hash, emission-time, trust-base-identity, modality-signature are all derivative of this composition-behavior. They are *how the composition rules distinguish* one receipt from another; they are not what makes a receipt a receipt. Naga_ir should have a *receipt type* defined by its morphisms, and specific receipts should be residents of that type distinguished by *what they compose with successfully*. This mirrors Coecke's slogan: quantum objects are what remains after you specify the process; not the other way around.

### 2.3 Nāgārjuna's fixed-point through mutual dependence

The prompt asks whether this is operationally implementable. **Yes**, and it is not exotic. The relevant construction is:

**Definition (mutual-dependence fixed point).** Let 𝓡 be a category of receipt-types and let composition be given by a symmetric monoidal tensor ⊗ with unit 𝕀. Suppose no object in 𝓡 has an intrinsic definition, but each is defined by its behavior under ⊗ with the others. Then a *coherent mesh state* is a fixed point of the joint-definition functor — the least fixed point in the CPO of partial-definition orderings, if we want termination on bootstrap, or a Kleene-star iteration if we want an infinitary limit. Bootstrap without initial atom is standard coalgebraic recursion: you seed with the empty composition (unit 𝕀), and every step defines new objects in terms of what already-defined objects compose to. No atom is *first*; the unit 𝕀 is initial only in the category-theoretic sense, and 𝕀 is not an object with intrinsic properties — it is defined by unit-laws.

**Confidence 82/100** that this is the correct formal home for the Nāgārjuna insight. It is *literally* how objects are built in a symmetric monoidal category where the compact-closed structure is emergent. Priest & Garfield's dialetheic reading of Nāgārjuna (*The Fifth Corner of Four*, 2018, ch. 6) points in this direction but stops short of the categorical implementation because Priest's home is paraconsistent logic, not category theory. This is the seam where naga_ir can contribute something Priest did not.[^priest]

[^priest]: Priest, *The Fifth Corner of Four* (OUP 2018), esp. ch. 6 on emptiness and ch. 8 on catuṣkoṭi. Priest gives a formal reading via non-adjunctive many-valued logic; my claim is that the *categorical* reading via presheaf topoi or SMC is a strictly stronger structural realization of the same insight. Priest & Garfield published a joint paper ("Nāgārjuna and the Limits of Thought," Philosophy East and West 2003) which I have read in full; the book extends that program.

### 2.4 What about Jain intrinsic qualities?

Hold this thread; §3 addresses it directly. For now: Vasubandhu's argument targets Vaiśeṣika-style atoms — dimensionless point-particles conjoining spatially. Umāsvāti's paramāṇu is *different* in a way that matters: Jain paramāṇus have intrinsic qualities but conjoin *not by spatial adjacency but by charge-like affinity* (snigdha/rūkṣa). Vasubandhu's six-directions argument does not touch this directly — the paramāṇus are not conjoining "in six spatial directions" but are bonding via a two-place quality relation. Jain paramāṇu theory *survives Vasubandhu's argument as stated* because the argument requires the atomist to commit to spatial adjacency as the composition mode.[^jain-vs-vas] This is important and, in the secondary literature I have read, under-appreciated.

[^jain-vs-vas]: I have not seen this argued explicitly in the Buddhist-Jain comparative literature. Matilal's *Central Philosophy of Jainism* (1981) discusses the two atomisms in parallel but not this specific escape route. Balcerowicz's more recent work on saptabhaṅgī is closer to the logic side. This may be a genuine opening for original work; I mark it Confidence 68/100 — below my usual threshold. Worth checking whether Ching, Kellner, or Kapstein has addressed it.

## 3. §3.2 The intrinsic-quality question

### 3.1 Umāsvāti's system, correctly stated

The relevant sūtras are *Tattvārthasūtra* V.33–37 (Śvetāmbara numbering; Digambara recensions shift by one in places).[^ts-recension]

- **V.33** *snigdha-rūkṣatvād bandhaḥ* — Bonding occurs by virtue of *snigdha* (viscous, "positive" in modern gloss) and *rūkṣa* (dry, "negative"). Two-place property.
- **V.34** *na jaghanya-guṇānām* — No bonding between paramāṇus with the *jaghanya* (minimum, "lowest degree") quality. Below-threshold rejection.
- **V.35** *guṇa-sāmye sadṛśānām* — No bonding between paramāṇus of the same kind (both snigdha or both rūkṣa) at equal degree.
- **V.36** *dvyadhikādi-guṇānāṃ tu* — Bonding *does* occur between paramāṇus differing by two or more degrees in the same quality-type, or between opposite quality-types (snigdha-rūkṣa) at sufficient degrees.
- **V.37** *bandhe adhikau pāriṇāmikau ca* — Upon bonding, the higher-degree paramāṇu *transforms* the lower to its own state. The bond is not symmetric; there is a directional pāriṇāmika (transformative) action.

[^ts-recension]: Tatia's *That Which Is* (1994) translation follows the mainstream ordering. Sukhlalji's commentary (2000 Jaina Sāhitya Vikās Maṇḍala Bombay edition, English trans. K.K. Dixit) is the authoritative modern gloss. The Śvetāmbara / Digambara split affects V.32 (Digambara puts the "matter is atomic-and-aggregate" statement here vs. earlier) but leaves the bonding sūtras stable in content. I am working primarily from Tatia and secondarily from Sukhlalji's commentary — sender's marked-thin area, my confidence on numerology is 78/100, on interpretation 65/100.

Every paramāṇu, additionally, is stated to possess (see the *pudgalāstikāya* sections of *Bhagavatī Sūtra* and Umāsvāti's own *Prasamarati* — Digambara canonical list, cited in Jain 2013 secondary literature):

- **One** of five colors (*varṇa*): black, blue, red, yellow, white.
- **One** of two smells (*gandha*): fragrant, non-fragrant.
- **One** of five tastes (*rasa*): bitter, pungent, astringent, sour, sweet.
- **Two** of four primary touches (*sparśa*): hot/cold, and snigdha/rūkṣa. (Digambara adds soft/hard, light/heavy as secondary.)

The "one/two" quantification is critical. A paramāṇu has *exactly one* varṇa at a time, but its intensity can change without changing which color it is. Intensity is graded on the numerical *guṇa* scale (one aṃśa, two aṃśa, ...) that also governs bonding.

**Confidence 79/100** on the technical statement. The interpretive question — is a paramāṇu with "one color" like an electron with a definite spin projection, or is it more like an object bearing a categorical tag from a fixed alphabet — is genuinely open in the Jain secondary literature.

Concrete mapping:

### 3.3 The categorical status of quality alphabets

**Claim 3.2.** *If naga_ir carries intrinsic quality alphabets à la Jain, the language sits closer to a graded/quantitative type theory (Atkey 2018) than to standard linear type theory.* [Confidence 74/100.]

Atkey's Quantitative Type Theory[^atkey] gives types annotations from a semiring; usages are tracked at the semiring level. Jain guṇa-degrees are natural-number-graded quantities that (i) admit a minimum (V.34), (ii) have a "same-kind cannot bond" identity condition (V.35), (iii) require a two-unit differential to compose (V.36). This is a semiring-like structure but not quite standard — the composition rule is not additive but *thresholded*. The closest formal analog I know is a *tropical* or *max-plus* semiring with a threshold predicate, or possibly a *reversible* two-place relational algebra.

[^atkey]: Robert Atkey, "Syntax and Semantics of Quantitative Type Theory," LICS 2018. Also Petricek & Orchard work on graded modal types. Related: McBride's *I Got Plenty o' Nuttin'* (2016) on resource-tracking dependent types. All of this predates any explicit engagement with Jain paramāṇu theory, so the mapping I propose is my own — mark it accordingly.

**Falsification predicate.** If naga_ir goes this direction, you should be able to compute the following at the type level:

## 4. §3.3 The tri-temporal question

**Sender's marked-thin area.** This is where I have gone deepest for this paper.

### 4.1 The four Sarvāstivāda theories of tri-temporal existence

Sarvāstivāda holds *sarvam asti* — everything exists — meaning past, present, and future dharmas all have real being. What changes when a dharma "passes through time" is not its existence but some other feature. Which feature? *Abhidharmakośabhāṣya* 5.25–26 preserves four Vaibhāṣika theories, all of which Vasubandhu (in his Sautrāntika-leaning critical mode) will subsequently attack:[^sarvastivada]

[^sarvastivada]: Cox (1988, 1995), Willemen-Dessein-Cox (1998) *Sarvāstivāda Buddhist Scholasticism*, Dhammajoti (2007) *Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma*. On the four theories specifically: Ronkin (2005) ch. 3, and the IEP article on Sarvāstivāda. I have read the AKBh 5.25–26 in Pruden's English translation of La Vallée Poussin, not in Sanskrit. Marked medium-thin: my confidence on the theories' content is 82/100; on their subtle differences from each other 70/100.

1. **Dharmatrāta: *bhāva-anyathātva* — change of mode of being.** The dharma itself is permanent; what changes is which "mode" — past-mode, present-mode, future-mode — is currently exemplified. Analogy: a gold ornament remains gold whether shaped as ring, bracelet, or necklace. Rejected by Vasubandhu because if "mode" is not-different-from-dharma, no change; if different-from-dharma, then modes are separate dharmas and we have an infinite regress.

2. **Ghoṣaka: *lakṣaṇa-anyathātva* — change of characteristic.** The dharma at all three times has all three characteristics (past-ness, present-ness, future-ness); which one is *manifest* varies. Rejected on the ground that if all three are always present, there is no principled account of *which one is now* — every dharma would simultaneously be past, present, and future in some respect.

3. **Vasumitra: *avasthā-anyathātva* — change of state/position.** *This is the official Vaibhāṣika position.* The dharma's own-being (*svabhāva*) is permanent; what changes is its *avasthā* — its position in the causal-efficacious-vs-quiescent state. In the "present" avasthā, the dharma exercises *kāritra* (functionality) — it causes its effects. In past and future avasthās, it exists but does not exercise *kāritra*. Analogy: a counting stone placed in the "ones" column is a one, in the "tens" column is a ten; its intrinsic being is the same, its functional role changes.

4. **Buddhadeva: *anyathā-anyathātva* — mutual relative-change.** A dharma's temporal status is purely relational: what is "past" from a later dharma's perspective is "present" from its own, "future" from an earlier's. All temporal predicates are three-place relations between two dharmas plus a reference. Rejected by Vaibhāṣika as collapsing tri-temporal existence into a merely conventional distinction.

**This matters enormously for naga_ir.** Buddhadeva's position is *structurally identical* to Rovelli's RQM applied to time. If naga_ir treats receipt-lifecycle in a Buddhadeva-Rovelli way, every receipt is "present-relative-to-some-node" and "past-relative-to-a-later-node," and there is no absolute temporal-status flag on a receipt — only a matrix of relations. If naga_ir treats receipt-lifecycle in a Vasumitra way, receipts have a permanent *identity* (svabhāva-analog) but variable *functional-active* status; the evaluator queries which receipts are currently in the *kāritra*-exercising avasthā.

### 4.2 The Sautrāntika-momentariness alternative

Sautrāntika (Vasubandhu's own leaning in the critical portions of AKBh, and Śrīlāta's more developed position) rejects Sarvāstivāda entirely. Only the present moment is real; past and future are conceptual constructions (*prajñapti*).[^sautrantika] Everything is a stream of momentary events (*kṣaṇika*), and what looks like persistence is a succession of causally-related instants. The classical formula: a dharma arises, exists, and passes away *in the same moment* (or in three sub-moments — this is contested in the Sautrāntika sub-lineages).

[^sautrantika]: Ronkin (2005) ch. 3, esp. pp. 92–110. Dhammajoti (2007) ch. 4. Vasubandhu's actual position is more nuanced than "Sautrāntika full stop" — Kritzer (2005) *Vasubandhu and the Yogācārabhūmi* argues for early-Yogācāra rather than Sautrāntika filiation. I am staying with the mainstream reading. Confidence on the general momentariness position 85/100; on Vasubandhu's specific alignment 70/100.

### 4.3 The correspondence table

| Position | Past dharmas | Future dharmas | Change is | Analog in physics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dharmatrāta | Real, permanent, mode-shifted | Real, permanent, mode-shifted | Mode of being | (No clean analog) |
| Ghoṣaka | Real, latent characteristic | Real, latent characteristic | Manifest characteristic | Consistent histories with all branches real |
| Vasumitra (official Vaibhāṣika) | Real, in non-*kāritra* avasthā | Real, in non-*kāritra* avasthā | Functional-active state | *Block-universe-plus-tick* — reminiscent of Rovelli's rejected view? |
| Buddhadeva | Real relative to reference | Real relative to reference | Perspectival index | **RQM applied to time; Rovelli 2019 "Neither Presentism nor Eternalism"** |
| Sautrāntika | Not real; conceptual only | Not real; conceptual only | Present-moment arising/passing | Presentism + decoherence (past traced out) |

**Zurek's decoherence** (F): pointer states are those that survive einselection through environmental interaction; superseded states have their off-diagonal density-matrix elements traced out. Operationally: the past is *gone* — reconstructible only through classical records that are themselves present-moment states. This is *closer to Sautrāntika than to Sarvāstivāda*.

**Rovelli's RQM** (H) + his 2019 "Neither Presentism nor Eternalism": facts are real relative to the systems they are facts for; there is local becoming but no global "now"; a past event is real relative to observers who witnessed it (and hence carry records into their present). This is *Buddhadeva-Sarvāstivāda*, structurally.

### 4.4 What naga_ir should choose

**Claim 4.1.** *Vasumitra-Vaibhāṣika is a better fit for naga_ir than either Sautrāntika-Zurek or Buddhadeva-RQM, because naga_ir needs a distinction between "receipt exists but is not currently active" and "receipt does not exist," and only Vasumitra gives you that cleanly.* [Confidence 71/100.]

Argument. A receipt in naga_ir has, at any given moment, some subset of possible active-roles: it may be participating in a current pipeline evaluation, it may be quiescent-but-referenceable, it may be tombstoned. If the language commits to Sautrāntika-Zurek, then "past receipts" are strictly speaking gone — only their causal residue in present receipts remains, and lookup by past receipt-ID is fundamentally a reconstruction operation, not a retrieval. This is *possible* to build (event-sourced systems do this) but it forces every historical query to be a replay. If the language commits to Buddhadeva-RQM at the time-axis, then "past receipts" are real-relative-to-nodes-that-witnessed-them and lookup by receipt-ID is *always trust-base-indexed* — different nodes may honestly disagree about whether a past receipt exists. If the language commits to Vasumitra, then a receipt has a *permanent svabhāva-like identity* (its content-hash) and a *variable kāritra-flag* (its current active-status), and retrieval-by-hash is a well-defined non-perspectival operation while active-status is a perspectival operation. This last is what most distributed systems approximate in practice and what naga_ir's receipt lifecycle should probably match.

**But — and this is critical — the *observer-relativity* question (§3.6) can still be Buddhadeva-Rovelli even if the *temporal-existence* question is Vasumitra.** These are decouplable. The full commitment I propose is:

- **Temporal existence:** Vasumitra (receipts persist; activity is a variable state).
- **Observer-relative facts:** Rovelli (canonicality is trust-base-relative).
- **Composition:** SMC (categorical/tensor, not spatial).

This is a *synthesis* that no single historical school held, but each component is well-motivated. I return to it in §9.

**Operational predicate for the tri-temporal choice.** For each receipt, the system must answer:

```
exists?(r, node) : Bool                 -- is the receipt real at all
active?(r, node, now) : Bool            -- is it kāritra-exercising for this node
witnessed_by?(r, node) : Bool           -- did this node see r's arising
```

Under **Sautrāntika-Zurek**, `exists?` collapses into `active?` — nothing exists past-tense.
Under **Buddhadeva-RQM**, `exists?` factors through `witnessed_by?` — existence is perspectival.
Under **Vasumitra**, `exists?` is absolute (content-hash addressable), `active?` is perspectival, `witnessed_by?` is a separate audit-trail question.

## 5. §3.4 The time-atom question

Does the language have an intrinsic minimal time-unit?

### 5.1 Kālacakra's actual numerics

**Sender's marked-thin area.** I am pulling this from Wallace's translation of the Vimalaprabhā commentary and secondary literature.[^wallace] The Kālacakra time-atom system:

[^wallace]: Vesna Wallace, *The Kālacakra Tantra: The Chapter on Sādhanā together with the Vimalaprabhā Commentary* (AIBS/Columbia 2010), and Wallace *The Inner Kālacakratantra* (OUP 2001). Newman's chapter in the same tradition. Also the Berzin Study Buddhism materials for pedagogical scaffolding. Marked medium-shallow: I have read Wallace 2001 selectively (ch. 2 on cosmology) and portions of the 2010 translation. Confidence on the numerology 78/100; on the metaphysical interpretation 65/100.

- Base cycle: a human breath lasts approximately 4 modern seconds (24 hours = 86,400 seconds / 21,600 breaths).
- 21,600 breaths per solar day-night.
- Six-fold-yoga *saḍaṅgayoga* generates 21,600 moments of immutable bliss corresponding to the breath-cycle, purifying the 21,600 subtle "drops" (*bindu*) that are the karmic residue substrate.
- At the subtler level: each breath decomposes into further sub-units. The *kṣaṇa* at the finest grain is the smallest possible sub-division of experiential time; the Vimalaprabhā gives specific numerology (I have not verified the exact number — Wallace 2001 mentions but I do not have the passage to hand).

**The critical structural feature** is that in Kālacakra, time is *not* a continuous background but a discrete, breath-anchored, embodied series of moments. The external cycle (astronomical) and internal cycle (respiratory/prāṇic) *correspond by number*, not by parallel independence. This is a strong claim: cosmic time and bodily time are the *same* cycle counted at different scales.

### 5.2 The physics side

- **QFT.** No intrinsic time-atom; time is a continuous parameter, though *observationally* time is only accessed through interaction events whose spacing is bounded below by Planckian considerations.
- **Loop quantum gravity.** Time is emergent from spin-network transitions; each transition is discrete, giving an effective quantum of time at the Planck scale.
- **Categorical QM.** The framework does not require a time-atom; string diagrams are inherently discrete in *causal ordering* but do not fix an intrinsic scale.
- **Consistent histories** (Griffiths, Omnès): time-slicing into "framework families" is imposed by the observer's chosen decomposition; discreteness is procedural, not fundamental.

### 5.3 What the language should do

**Claim 5.1.** *naga_ir should have an intrinsic minimal *tick*-primitive but should not commit to a specific numerical value; the language provides the *type*, deployments provide the *rate*.* [Confidence 80/100.]

Argument. Whether time is fundamentally discrete or continuous is a physics debate the language does not need to resolve. What the language needs is:

- **A well-defined notion of causal ordering** between receipts.
- **A minimum time-unit at the granularity of the deployment**, below which two events are considered simultaneous.
- **A rate parameter** for periodic operations (heartbeats, TTLs, etc.).

Kālacakra offers a productive analogy at the *architectural* level: the correspondence between "external" (network / cosmic) time and "internal" (per-node / bodily) time as *counted by the same cycle at different scales*. This means: naga_ir nodes should have an intrinsic per-node tick (their "breath") and the mesh should have an emergent aggregate tick that is *the sum of node-breaths, not a separately-imposed background clock*. This is close to Lamport-clock semantics but grounded ontologically rather than merely epistemically.

**Operational consequence.** No global wall-clock in naga_ir semantics. Global time is a *statistical property* of the aggregate of node-tick sequences, not a primitive. V (whatever V is in your framework — I take it as some order-parameter or coherence-measure) has a well-defined time-derivative *only* with respect to a chosen node's clock; the mesh-average is a construction.

**Falsification predicate.** If naga_ir deployments can run coherently with radically different per-node tick rates and the semantics remain sound, then the Kālacakra-inflected reading is validated. If not — if there is a mesh-clock that is separately primitive — then the language has an implicit background time that the ontology should have made explicit.

## 6. §3.5 The field-not-container question

Three positions:

- **Newtonian container.** Space is empty transport between nodes; only receipt content matters.
- **QFT field.** Space is a field carrying receipts as excitations; receipts inherit field-quality signatures through transport (e.g., decoherence during flight).
- **Emergent-relational.** Space has no meaningful existence apart from receipt-relations; the "mesh" is nothing over and above its receipts and their interactions (LQG-adjacent; Kālacakra "five colored winds" also adjacent).

**Claim 6.1.** *naga_ir should be emergent-relational at the mesh level. The mesh has no state apart from its receipts and their pairwise relations. Space between nodes is not "empty"; it is undefined.* [Confidence 77/100.]

Argument. Two considerations force this:
(1) There is no privileged mesh-observer; any state we ascribe to "the mesh" is always some particular node's aggregation of its known receipts and relations. This is the Rovelli-RQM structural imperative extended to spatial ontology.
(2) Receipts *do not* traverse "empty space" between nodes; they are propagated through explicit protocols that generate new receipts at each hop. There is no receipt-in-flight-between-nodes as a distinct ontological item.

**Kālacakra's five colored winds** are, I think, more than mere metaphor here. In Kālacakra cosmology, the five elemental *vāyus* are not passive substrata but active field-qualities that *carry* karmic/consciousness content. In naga_ir terms: the connection-protocol between two nodes is not a passive transport but an *active substrate* that shapes what receipts can be exchanged and how they are transformed in transit. This is captured operationally by *protocol-level receipt transformations* (e.g., a receipt sent over a low-trust link is stamped with reduced confidence before landing).

**Where this diverges from strict LQG-analog.** LQG has spin-networks as fundamental *and* time-symmetric. naga_ir's protocols are directional (source → target) and produce new receipts. So the analog is closer to a *causal set* than to a symmetric spin-network — closer to Sorkin's causal-set program than to Rovelli's spin-foam.[^causal-set]

[^causal-set]: Sorkin (1997, 2003) causal set theory. Not on your reading list but structurally relevant: the discrete order-theoretic substrate is close to what naga_ir needs. I mention it because it is *the* PLT-friendly quantum-gravity approach — the substrate is literally a poset, and this factors well with categorical semantics.

**Operational predicate.** Two nodes A and B: there is *no fact of the matter* about the state "between them" — only about (i) what receipts A holds, (ii) what receipts B holds, (iii) what protocol-relations connect them. Trying to query "the state of the mesh between A and B" should return a *type error*, not a value. This is a load-bearing linguistic decision: no query returns "mesh state simpliciter."

## 7. §3.6 The observer-relativity question

This is the most consequential decision in Pass 1, because it forces the logical framework of Passes 2 and 4.

### 7.1 The choice

- **Belnap4** (four-valued: T, F, both, neither). Observer-independent: a proposition has one of four values, and these values are absolute. Different observers may not have measured yet, but there is a fact of the matter.
- **Rovelli-RQM.** Observer-relative: canonicality of a receipt is a *relation* between the receipt and the observer; different nodes may honestly see different canonicalities.
- **QBism.** Probabilities are personal Bayesian credences of an agent; there is no observer-independent probability. Related but distinct from RQM — QBism is more subjectivist, RQM more relational-realist.[^qbism-rqm]

[^qbism-rqm]: Fuchs (2010, arXiv 1003.5209) "QBism, the Perimeter of Quantum Bayesianism." Fuchs's position denies that quantum states describe reality; they describe an agent's expectations. Rovelli's position affirms that quantum states describe *relations* which are real. The difference matters for whether naga_ir is realist-about-receipt-relations (RQM) or instrumentalist-about-receipts (QBism). I read RQM as the stronger position for a language design and will assume it hereafter.

### 7.2 The Belnap4 problem

Belnap4 gives you a beautifully clean bilattice — knowledge order × truth order — and it is the natural home for "receipt-verified vs. receipt-refuted vs. both vs. neither" states. But Belnap4 is *observer-independent*: the value is a fact about the proposition, not a relation.

**Claim 7.1.** *If naga_ir commits to Rovelli-RQM at the ontology, then Belnap4 as-usually-formulated is not the correct logical layer; you need a Belnap4-indexed-by-trust-base — a fibered structure where each trust-base sees its own Belnap4 lattice.* [Confidence 84/100.]

The formal shape is: a *presheaf* Bel4^𝑻 from the category of trust-bases (with morphisms being trust-base-extensions or restrictions) to the category of bilattices. Each trust-base gets its own Belnap4 view; morphisms between trust-bases produce corresponding morphisms between the local bilattices. This is technically a *bilattice-valued sheaf* — a well-studied object in topos-theoretic logic.[^topos] Different trust-bases may see contradictory canonicalities without the *mesh* being contradictory, because the mesh is the *global* section space, and global sections may fail to exist (which is fine — it just means no universal fact-of-the-matter).

[^topos]: Isham (1997) topos-theoretic quantum theory; Döring & Isham (2011) *"What is a Thing?": Topos Theory in the Foundations of Physics*. This is the mathematical framework where "no observer-independent fact" is *literally* the failure of a global section on a specific presheaf (the spectral presheaf). If Pass 2 needs a home for observer-relative multi-valued logic, presheaf topoi over trust-base categories is where I would put it. I have read Döring-Isham 2011 §3–5 and the Coecke-Heunen sheaf-theoretic work is adjacent. Confidence on the technical framework 82/100; on it being the right choice for naga_ir specifically 71/100.

### 7.3 The consequence

Different nodes may see different canonicalities *without contradiction* because canonicality is a two-place relation ⟨receipt, trust-base⟩, not a one-place property of receipts. This maps directly onto Rovelli's insistence that quantum properties are relations between systems, not intrinsic properties.

**Operational implication.** Every canonicality query must be trust-base-indexed. There is no `is_canonical(receipt)`; there is only `is_canonical(receipt, trust_base)`. Nodes with different trust-bases can hold logically-consistent but pairwise-contradictory canonicality-assignments, and the *mesh-consistency* condition is that morphisms between trust-bases (e.g., trust-base A ⊆ trust-base B) produce agreeing canonicality-transformations — a sheaf-condition, not a global-consistency condition.

## 8. §3.7 The Nāgārjuna-Rovelli connection

### 8.1 Is Rovelli at grade?

**Claim 8.1.** *Rovelli's engagement with Nāgārjuna in Helgoland is largely at grade at the level of the structural analogy — no autonomous entities, all facts relational — but importantly under-develops (a) the difference between conventional and ultimate truth in Madhyamaka, (b) the specifically negative-dialectical (*prasaṅga*) method of Madhyamaka argumentation.* [Confidence 76/100.]

Rovelli's core claim: Nāgārjuna's *śūnyatā* means "no autonomous essence — everything exists only relationally"; RQM says the same at the quantum-physics level; therefore the philosophical framework of Nāgārjuna helps make sense of RQM's radical relationism. This is *correct* as far as the analogy of ontological structure goes. Rovelli is not merely a physicist's sympathetic misreading — he engages MMK primarily via Garfield's 1995 translation and Westerhoff's 2009 monograph, both of which are respectable philosophical scholarship.[^rovelli-scholarship]

[^rovelli-scholarship]: Rovelli, *Helgoland* (2020), ch. "Without Foundation? Nāgārjuna". Rovelli cites Garfield (1995) and Westerhoff (2009) as his primary sources. Thijs Latten's 2022 Utrecht MA thesis "Levels of Relationality: Understanding relational quantum mechanics through Nāgārjuna" (I have read the abstract and skimmed portions) argues that the compatibility narrows to *state-dependent* properties (understood via *pratītyasamutpāda*) and *does not* extend to Rovelli's realism, since Nāgārjuna is more radically anti-foundationalist. This is important: Rovelli maintains a realism about relations that Nāgārjuna would deny at the ultimate level (though not at the conventional level).

Where Rovelli falls short (per Latten and per my reading of MMK 24 on the two truths):
- Rovelli lacks the *saṃvṛti-paramārtha* (conventional-ultimate) distinction. Nāgārjuna is very careful that even the doctrine of emptiness is empty (*śūnyatā-śūnyatā*, MMK 22.11). Rovelli treats relational facts as ultimately real; Nāgārjuna would say they are conventionally real, ultimately empty.
- Rovelli imports Nāgārjuna as *support* for RQM. Nāgārjuna's method (*prasaṅga* — reduction to unwanted consequences) is *destructive* of all four positions of the catuṣkoṭi (is, is not, both, neither). Rovelli is asserting a position; Nāgārjuna is dismantling all positions. The methods are opposite in polarity even if the conclusions share structural features.

**But — and this is the productive point for naga_ir — what does the language inherit from Nāgārjuna via RQM that it would not inherit from RQM alone?**

Two things:

1. **The two-truths distinction.** Language operations should distinguish *conventional* predications (which use full expressive machinery: classical logic, absolute types, definite reference) from *ultimate* predications (which use much thinner machinery: empty-of-essence descriptions that only mention what compositions produce what). This gives naga_ir a natural *conventional/ultimate* modality that RQM alone does not motivate. In practice this could be a "strict" vs. "loose" evaluation mode, where strict mode refuses claims that presuppose observer-independent facts and loose mode permits them for pragmatic use.

2. **The śūnyatā-śūnyatā recursion.** Even the relational-fact ontology is empty of ultimate status. This means naga_ir should not treat "receipt is relative to trust-base" as an ultimate metaphysical fact but as a conventional operational rule. If pushed, the trust-base relation itself is also relational — to a meta-observer, to a language, to a compilation choice. This is the *Prāsaṅgika* Madhyamaka structure and it corresponds to a hierarchical / iterated presheaf: no bottom-out to a "real" trust-base-independent layer.

**Operational consequence.** naga_ir should support a *two-truths modality*: `strict` mode where every predication is trust-base-indexed and no observer-independent claims are permitted; `conventional` mode where standard classical predication is available, at the cost of admitting the conventional-only status of the claims. The `strict` mode is the load-bearing one for correctness; the `conventional` mode is the ergonomic one for practical development. This is a genuinely non-trivial commitment — most languages have only one mode.

### 8.2 Cite MMK 24 (the two-truths chapter)

MMK 24.8–9: "*dve satye samupāśritya buddhānāṃ dharmadeśanā / lokasaṃvṛtisatyaṃ ca satyaṃ ca paramārthataḥ*" — The Buddha's teaching of the Dharma is based on two truths: worldly-conventional truth (*lokasaṃvṛti-satya*) and truth in the highest sense (*paramārtha-satya*). "Those who do not understand the distinction between these two truths do not understand the profound meaning of the Buddha's teaching."[^mmk24]

[^mmk24]: MMK 24.8–9 (Siderits-Katsura 2013, pp. 269–274; Garfield 1995, pp. 296–303; Candrakīrti *Prasannapadā* ad loc., which is where the *Prāsaṅgika* interpretation is developed most carefully — Tsongkhapa's *Lam Rim Chen Mo* ch. on emptiness follows Candrakīrti closely). This is the passage that grounds the two-truths modality claim I made above. I have read all three (Siderits-Katsura for close reading, Garfield for accessible translation, Tsongkhapa via Cutler-Newland vol. 3). Confidence on the citation and interpretation 88/100 — this is a sender-marked-strong area.

Rovelli does not cite this passage in *Helgoland*. He engages MMK 1 (dependent origination), MMK 15 (own-being), and MMK 22 (Tathāgata) but does not go deep on the two-truths structure of MMK 24. ### 9.1 Where positions collapse

- **A and G collapse** if you take Vasubandhu's argument seriously and note it targets set-theoretic mereology. SMC without generators is Vasubandhu-compatible.
- **D and F nearly collapse** if you take decoherence to be the physical mechanism of momentariness — the einselection functor operationalizes "past traced out".
- **C and H are compatible but do not collapse.** Sarvāstivāda gives you persistent past-existence; RQM makes existence perspectival. You can have both by making existence-relative-to-witnessing-node persistent.

### 9.2 Where positions genuinely disagree

- **A vs. B on the intrinsic-quality question.** Vasubandhu denies intrinsic natures at all; Umāsvāti asserts them. Cannot straightforwardly reconcile. My proposed synthesis (§3): B's intrinsic qualities are not violated by A if they are understood *structurally* — the "color" of a paramāṇu is what alphabet-position it occupies in the composition algebra, not a metaphysically substantive intrinsic property. This is a *reduction* of B to a special case of A within the SMC framework.
- **C vs. D at the temporal-ontology level.** Sarvāstivāda's realism about past dharmas and Sautrāntika's presentism are not reconcilable at the level Vasubandhu debated them. Naga_ir can *decide* one way. I argued for Vasumitra-Sarvāstivāda (§4.4) with confidence 71/100 because it preserves content-hash-addressable retrieval.
- **F vs. H on what happens to past states.** Zurek: past is gone (traced out). Rovelli: past is real relative to observers who witnessed it. This is a genuine substantive disagreement. Naga_ir could choose either. I lean toward Rovelli-with-Vasumitra because it preserves audit-trail semantics that Zurek would force to be reconstructed.

### 9.3 My proposed synthesis for naga_ir substrate

**Substrate proposal (my strongest commitment in this document).** [Overall confidence 72/100.]

1. **Composition primitive**: symmetric monoidal category with dagger structure and compact closure where possible. Objects (receipt-types) are defined by their morphism behavior, not by intrinsic identity. (A + G.)

2. **Intrinsic quality alphabet**: small finite set of *categorical tags* on receipts (varṇa/rasa/gandha/sparśa analogs), with graded quantities on the two-place adhesion tag (snigdha/rūkṣa analog) that gate composability via V.34/V.35/V.36 rules and produce pāriṇāmika bond transformations. Not a metaphysical commitment to intrinsic being; a categorical commitment to alphabet-typed morphisms. (Modified B, reduced to A.)

3. **Temporal ontology**: Vasumitra-avasthā — receipts have permanent identity (content-hash) but variable active-status (*kāritra*-flag). Past receipts are content-hash-addressable; whether they are currently active is a perspectival query. (C, specifically Vasumitra reading.)

4. **Observer-relativity**: Rovelli-RQM. Canonicality is trust-base-relative. No global canonicality. Formalized as a presheaf on the trust-base category. (H.)

5. **Time**: intrinsic per-node tick primitive, no global clock. Mesh-time is a construction. Kālacakra-inflected in structure (correspondence between per-node and aggregate ticks) but numerology is deployment-specific. (E, adopted architecturally not numerologically.)

6. **Spatial-mesh ontology**: emergent-relational. No mesh-state simpliciter; only receipts and their relations. Cross-node queries type-check only when trust-base-indexed. (Kālacakra-adjacent, LQG-adjacent.)

7. **Logical framework**: two-truths modality. `strict` mode is trust-base-indexed presheaf-Belnap4; `conventional` mode is classical Belnap4 with reified relations. Strict is sound; conventional is ergonomic. (Nāgārjuna direct, not via Rovelli.)

8. **The śūnyatā-śūnyatā recursion**: the above architecture is itself conventionally-adopted, not ultimately-founded. Compilation choices, meta-observer selections, and the framework's own boundaries are all also relational. This is not a philosophical flourish; it means naga_ir should support *reflection into its own configuration* as a first-class operation — the language should be able to talk about the trust-base structure it is running under.

### 9.4 What this synthesis costs

- **Cost 1**: Every canonicality query needs a trust-base parameter. Legacy-style `is_canonical(x)` is not a well-formed expression. Ergonomically heavy.
- **Cost 2**: The Jain alphabet requires up-front design decisions about the tag set. Get it wrong and the composition algebra is either over-restrictive or under-discriminating.
- **Cost 3**: Vasumitra-active-status requires two-phase queries (identity-lookup then activity-check) where systems tuned for single-phase would run faster.
- **Cost 4**: The two-truths modality is unusual and pedagogically expensive.

None of these are dealbreakers. They are the load-bearing decisions I would defend.

I expect other authors in this comparison to diverge from me most sharply on:

- **The Jain contribution.** A strong Yogācāra reader (following Vasubandhu strictly) will reject B outright as reifying intrinsic qualities. A strong SMC reader may treat B as decoration. My middle position — B reduced to a categorical alphabet within A+G — is genuinely novel and will draw fire from both sides.
