# Research: SRS + Pitch-Accent Training for a New Japanese Learning App

*Compiled 2026-08-09. Every claim carries a source URL. Where a source could not be fetched directly (network egress restrictions), claims rest on search-result summaries of the cited page and are marked (search summary).*

## 1. Competitive landscape

**Anki** — general-purpose flashcard app; the de-facto standard for serious Japanese self-learners. Historically SM-2-based; since Anki 23.10 it ships FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) as an opt-in built-in scheduler ([Anki FAQ](https://faqs.ankiweb.net/what-spaced-repetition-algorithm); [FSRS history](https://l-m-sherlock.notion.site/The-History-of-FSRS-for-Anki-1e6c250163a180a4bfd7fb1fee2a3043); [ankitects/anki PR #2633](https://github.com/ankitects/anki/pull/2633)). Pitch accent reaches Anki only via community tooling: the Yomitan (ex-Yomichan) popup dictionary has built-in pitch-accent display ([WaniKani community PSA](https://community.wanikani.com/t/psa-yomichan-now-has-built-in-pitch-accent-support/43259)) and the AJT PitchAccent add-on bulk-adds accent notation to notes ([Ajatt-Tools/PitchAccent](https://github.com/Ajatt-Tools/PitchAccent)).

**WaniKani** — kanji/vocab SRS with a fixed, non-adaptive interval ladder for every item and user: 4h → 8h → 1 day → 2 days → 1 week → 2 weeks → 1 month → 4 months across 8 SRS stages ([WaniKani Knowledge: SRS stages](https://knowledge.wanikani.com/wanikani/srs-stages/); [Wanilog explainer](https://wanilog.com/guides/wanikani-srs-explained)). No pitch-accent training.

**Bunpro** — grammar SRS covering 800+ grammar points organized by JLPT level, drilled via cloze-deletion sentences; intervals lengthen on correct answers, shorten on misses ([bunpro.jp](https://bunpro.jp/); [Tofugu review](https://www.tofugu.com/reviews/bunpro/) (search summary); [Bunpro community thread on intervals](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/what-are-the-srs-intervals/311) (search summary)). No pitch-accent training.

**Pitch-accent tools:**
- **Kotu (kotu.io)** — free Pitch Accent Minimal Pairs Perception Test: plays native audio of an isolated word, user picks the pattern (heiban/atamadaka/nakadaka/odaka, filterable), with replay of both pronunciations after a mistake ([kotu.io test](https://kotu.io/tests/pitchAccent/minimalPairs); [Tofugu review](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-learning-resources-database/kotsu-pitch-accent-minimal-pairs-test/) (search summary)). A community clone exists at [kuuuube.github.io/minimal-pairs](https://kuuuube.github.io/minimal-pairs/).
- **Migaku Pitch Trainer** (successor to the MIA pitch-trainer work) — perception-training lessons plus exercise modes: Matching (pick the pattern you heard among the 4), Graph (match audio to the pitch graph), and Kaku (pick the mora before the drop); ~5,000 native-recorded words, level/difficulty scaling and achievements ([Patreon alpha announcement](https://www.patreon.com/posts/migaku-pitch-66658695); [demo](https://pitch-demo.migaku.io/); [Tofugu review](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-learning-resources-database/migaku-pitch-trainer/) (search summary)).
- **OJAD** (University of Tokyo) — Online Japanese Accent Dictionary with the **Suzuki-kun** prosody tutor, which visualizes the pitch/prosodic structure of arbitrary sentences in Tokyo Japanese and synthesizes model audio (multiple voices, speed control) ([OJAD](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/eng/pages/home); [Suzuki-kun](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/eng/phrasing/index)). The authors report experiments in which visualized prosody helped learners significantly more than audio-only model utterances ([ISCA paper, Minematsu et al. 2017](https://www.isca-archive.org/slate_2017/minematsu17_slate.html)).
- **Dogen's Japanese Phonetics course** (Patreon video series) — the most detailed English-language pitch-accent instruction; explanatory, not an SRS/trainer ([Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/cw/dogen); [Tofugu review](https://www.tofugu.com/reviews/dogen-japanese-phonetics/)).

## 2. SRS algorithms in use, and criticisms

- **SM-2 (Anki legacy, and the ancestor of most app schedulers)** — 1987 SuperMemo formula; a single per-card "ease factor" scales intervals. Criticisms: **"ease hell"** (repeated "Again" answers drive ease to the 130% floor, after which intervals barely grow); no actual model of memory decay — it can't estimate when you'll forget; assumes one fixed exponential forgetting curve for everyone and treats cards as independent ([FSRS vs SM-2 comparisons](https://www.neurako.com/blog/fsrs-vs-sm2-spaced-repetition-algorithms-compared); [kachika.app explainer](https://kachika.app/en/blog/spaced-repetition-algorithms/); [SM-2 breakdown analysis](https://earezki.com/ai-news/2026-07-24-sm-2-is-not-enough-where-classic-spaced-repetition-breaks-down/)).
- **FSRS (Anki 23.10+)** — machine-learned difficulty/stability/retrievability model, trainable on the user's own review history, targeting a user-chosen retention rate. Benchmarks on hundreds of millions of real reviews show FSRS variants beating SM-2 on log-loss/RMSE in the overwhelming majority of collections, with reports of ~20–30% fewer reviews for equal retention ([open benchmark](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html); [ankitects/fsrs-benchmark](https://github.com/ankitects/fsrs-benchmark); [Neurako comparison](https://www.neurako.com/blog/fsrs-vs-sm2-spaced-repetition-algorithms-compared)). Known rough edges: optimizer runs can occasionally pick worse parameters than the previous fit, prompting user complaints and mitigation proposals ([Anki forum: worse log loss after optimize](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/in-anki-desktop-25-02-optimizer-chooses-parameters-with-greater-worse-log-loss-and-rmse/56977); [suggestion thread](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/measures-to-prevent-new-fsrs-parameters-from-being-worse-than-the-old-ones/38957)).
- **WaniKani (fixed table)** — every item gets the same 8-stage ladder; the schedule never adapts to the individual item or user, no published retention target, and no published derivation of the 4h/8h/23h/47h timings — the core criticism relative to Anki/FSRS ([Wanilog analysis](https://wanilog.com/guides/wanikani-srs-explained); [WaniKani Knowledge](https://knowledge.wanikani.com/wanikani/srs/); [community threads on interval rigidity](https://community.wanikani.com/t/srs-intervals-and-wk-long-time-value/32550)).
- **Bunpro (adaptive-interval SRS, SM-2-family behavior)** — correct answers lengthen intervals, misses shorten them; the exact schedule is documented only in community threads, and users note items must be answered correctly several times before intervals reach multi-day lengths ([Bunpro community thread](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/what-are-the-srs-intervals/311) (search summary); [Tofugu review](https://www.tofugu.com/reviews/bunpro/) (search summary)).

## 3. How pitch accent is taught today — and the gaps

**Current state.** Classroom and textbook instruction largely omits pitch accent: Japanese remains comprehensible with wrong pitch, textbooks prioritize early reading, teachers cite time limits and lack of training, and effective materials are scarce ([Mikey Does overview of the debate](https://mikeydoes.com/japanese-pitch-accent-debate/); [Language Log discussion](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=52917); [Purdue thesis on online oral practice for pitch acquisition](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI1565094)). What exists online splits into: (a) *lookup/reference* — OJAD, Yomitan pitch dictionaries; (b) *perception drills* — Kotu, Migaku Pitch Trainer, kuuuube's minimal pairs; (c) *explanatory video* — Dogen ([sources as in §1](https://kotu.io/tests/pitchAccent/minimalPairs)).

**Gaps.**
1. **Perception and production are disconnected.** The trainers test hearing only; none score the learner's own speech. Research suggests oral practice matters for accentuation acquisition ([Purdue dissertation](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI1565094)), and an LSA proceedings paper describes a practice program addressing L1 interference ([LSA proceedings](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/PLSA/article/download/4276/3884/6268)).
2. **Pitch is not integrated into mainstream SRS.** WaniKani and Bunpro ignore it; Anki needs add-on plumbing ([Ajatt-Tools/PitchAccent](https://github.com/Ajatt-Tools/PitchAccent)).
3. **Isolated words, not sentences.** Kotu/Migaku drill words in isolation; sentence-level phenomena (downstep, phrasing, particle behavior) are only *visualized* by OJAD/Suzuki-kun, not drilled ([Suzuki-kun](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/eng/phrasing/index)).
4. **No spaced scheduling of pitch knowledge itself.** Existing trainers use level/difficulty scaling ([Migaku Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/posts/migaku-pitch-66658695)) rather than a forgetting-model scheduler like FSRS.

## 4. Differentiator opportunities (5–7)

1. **FSRS-class adaptive scheduling as the default**, with a user-visible retention target — directly addressing WaniKani's fixed-ladder criticism and SM-2 ease hell ([benchmark evidence](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html); [Wanilog](https://wanilog.com/guides/wanikani-srs-explained)).
2. **Pitch accent as a first-class SRS dimension**: each vocab item carries a separate "can you hear/produce its pattern?" review state, scheduled by the same forgetting model — nothing on the market does this (gap per §3, [Kotu](https://kotu.io/tests/pitchAccent/minimalPairs)/[Migaku](https://pitch-demo.migaku.io/) are standalone).
3. **Production scoring via on-device pitch tracking**: record the learner, extract F0, compare against the target pattern — closing the perception→production gap the research flags ([Purdue](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI1565094); [LSA](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/PLSA/article/download/4276/3884/6268)).
4. **Sentence-level prosody drills**, not just isolated words: leverage OJAD-style visualization plus TTS for downstep/phrasing practice — evidence that visual prosody beats audio-only models ([Minematsu et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/slate_2017/minematsu17_slate.html)).
5. **Integrated stack (kanji + vocab + grammar + pitch) in one scheduler**, versus today's fragmented WaniKani + Bunpro + Anki + Kotu toolchain (landscape per §1).
6. **Transparent, auditable scheduling**: publish the algorithm, per-item recall predictions, and retention stats — contrasting with WaniKani's unexplained interval table ([Wanilog](https://wanilog.com/guides/wanikani-srs-explained)).
7. **Miss-aware remediation for pitch**: after a wrong minimal-pair answer, auto-queue contrastive replay of both patterns (Kotu's replay-on-error, but persisted into the SRS queue) ([Kotu test description, Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-learning-resources-database/kotsu-pitch-accent-minimal-pairs-test/) (search summary)).
