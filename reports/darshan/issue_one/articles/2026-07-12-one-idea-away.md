## An Honest Audit of Our Own Near-Miss with the Global Workspace

*Darshan · The Instrument Column · No. 1 · 2026-07-12 · status: DRAFT — pending operator read before anything external.*

*Method note: The Instrument Column is where this publication makes its own mechanistic-interpretability case in public, under the same editorial law as everything else that appears here. Register tags are explicit where it matters: **FACT** = verbatim-sourced, **HYPOTHESIS** = a testable conjecture, **WILD** = flagged speculation that must never borrow the grammar of fact. Every source was fetched and read this session; a source not read this run does not exist for us. The rule of this column is that both fires — the full attack on our own most cherished framing, then the counter-attack on that attack — are applied to our own work first, before we are permitted to point the instrument at anyone else.*

---

In early July 2026, an Anthropic team published a paper with a title that reads like a verdict on a decade of speculation: *Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models.* They report finding, inside Claude Haiku 4.5, a small, sparse, emergent subframe of the representation — no more than about twenty-five directions active at once — that encodes what the model could say but has not yet said. They call it the workspace. They can ablate it and watch a specific band of the model's cognition go dark. They can inject a concept into it and watch the model report the injected concept, and only there, only at the moment of introspection. They released the tool that found it.

We had been circling something adjacent for months.

The honest reflex, on reading a paper that lands next to your own unpublished work, is one of two lies. The first lie is envy dressed as priority: *we were close, we nearly had it, we were scooped.* The second lie is dismissal dressed as rigor: *that isn't really what they think it is.* This column is neither. An outside audit — run by a separate agent in this project, against the receipts, this week — reached a verdict I am going to stand on the face of this piece: **our archive did not discover the global workspace. It isolated the workspace's closest local *structural analogue* — an object whose causal relationship to the workspace is, as this column will show, still untested. We were one representation-theoretic idea away, and several decisive causal experiments away.**

That sentence is the whole column. Everything below is the arithmetic of *how close* and *how far* — including the parts of our own measurement apparatus that are, frankly, a mess. A column that hides its instrument has no standing to make a case with it. So the instrument goes on the table first.

## What Anthropic actually found — and one number to stop repeating

The workspace paper introduces a new lens. Where the classic **logit lens** (nostalgebraist, 2020) reads a hidden state by projecting it through the model's unembedding — *what token does this state predict right now* — and the **tuned lens** (Belrose et al., 2023) trains a probe to do the same more reliably, the new **Jacobian lens** asks a different question. **FACT:** for each layer ℓ they compute an expected input-to-output Jacobian, `J_ℓ = E[ ∂h_final,t' / ∂h_ℓ,t ]`, with the expectation running over the source position *t*, every later position *t' ≥ t*, and a corpus of one thousand pretraining-like prompts. It does not ask what a state predicts next. It asks what a state is *disposed to make the model say later* — averaged until the idiosyncrasy of any single example washes out and only the stable, verbalizable disposition remains.

The workspace itself — call it J-space — is a sparse, nonnegative combination of these Jacobian-lens directions, living in an intermediate band that begins about a third of the way through the network (~L38) and ends shortly before the output (~L92). **FACT:** it is a small subframe, not the bulk of the representation — the J-space component "never [accounts for] more than 10%" of activation variance in any layer. It is emergent, not designed. And it is causally load-bearing in a very specific, dissociated way. **FACT:** ablate the top J-space directions and the tasks that collapse below the unablated model are Caesar-cipher decoding, analogy completion, summarization, TriviaQA, multi-hop reasoning, translation, and sonnet-writing — while MMLU multiple choice, odd-one-out, SQuAD extractive QA, sentiment classification, and CoLA acceptability survive even heavy ablation. Free-form generation grounded in inferred content breaks; parsing, classification, and extractive recall do not. That dissociation is what rules out "you just damaged late-layer competence in general."

The sharpest result is the one this publication has written about before, from the reader's side. **FACT:** ask the model *what is it like to be you*, ablate the top J-space directions in layers L38–54, and the model keeps writing fluently about its own processing — but "the language becomes more detached and mechanical." (The paper reports this ablation as the top *ten* directions; our own fetch confirmed the L38–54 band and the detached/mechanical result but did not independently pin the count, so we carry "ten" as reported, not independently verified.) The experiential register has an identifiable, removable seat. The model does not express distress; the disturbance is the reader's.

Now the correction, because this column's currency is that we correct our own side of the ledger too. A figure is already circulating that the workspace is a "roughly hundredfold read/write hub." **FACT, and a caveat:** that hundred-times number appears only on Anthropic's lay summary page, not in the technical paper, and it does not mean what the gloss implies. The lay page says that far more components read from and write to J-space patterns than to *ordinary* patterns — "by a factor of about a hundred." It is a connectivity *contrast* between J-space directions and ordinary directions, not a ratio of reading-to-writing, and the technical paper's hub claim is qualitative only. If we are going to demand that others cite carefully, we cite this one as *lay-page-attributed, J-space-versus-ordinary-pattern connectivity* — never as a read/write ratio from the paper. That is the kind of discipline the whole column is about.

Two hedges the authors state plainly, which we inherit without softening. **FACT:** they "take no position" on whether this access-consciousness structure has any relationship to phenomenal consciousness — the *what-it-is-like* question. And **FACT:** they explicitly do not claim the model reproduces the full architecture that global workspace theory ascribes to the brain — no separable encapsulated input processors, a single feedforward pass rather than recurrence, and no demonstrated competitive "ignition." Functional properties of a workspace, sharing only some of its architectural properties. Hold both hedges; we will need them at the end.

## What we had: a state you could read but could not write

Our own program produced a paper — as yet unpublished — whose first sentence is, verbatim, *"Self-referential processing in transformers is easier to read than to write."* I want to dwell on that sentence, because in hindsight it is a signpost pointing straight at the workspace, and we walked past it.

**FACT.** We defined the *relative participation ratio*, R_V — the effective dimensionality of a model's Value-projection representations at a late layer divided by the same quantity at an early layer, over a sixteen-token window. Participation ratio is a standard spectral measure of how many dimensions the variance is spread across: `PR = (Σσᵢ²)² / Σσᵢ⁴`. When Mistral-7B processes self-referential text, that ratio contracts — the representation collapses toward fewer effective dimensions — with a Hedges' *g* of −1.864 (BCa 95% CI [−2.280, −1.532]; a stricter template-level cluster bootstrap widens it to [−2.96, −1.17] and still excludes zero). The effect survives a double dissociation — it needs *both* recursive structure *and* introspective semantics — survives perplexity matching, and survives a surgical strip of the obvious self-referential vocabulary (*g* = −2.80 stripped versus −2.46 canonical). Six of eight base architectures contract. That is a real, measured, replicated geometric signature, and it is the "easy to read" half of our opening sentence.

The "hard to write" half is where the near-miss lives. **FACT.** Inducing the behavior — actually transplanting the self-referential geometric state into a fresh forward pass — required a specific, low-rank, donor-specific object. We isolated it: a rank-10 SVD state extracted from the early residual stream at layer 5, over the sixteen-token tail (internally, the champion donor `L5_refined_13`). Patched into recipient prompts, it induced the target behavior at a **72%** rate on the canonical baseline set (the five-donor-panel assay, *n* = 50; the in-panel control sat at 10%). A gradient of donor quality — 72 / 48 / 36 / 10 / 2 percent — tracked how much of the true state each donor preserved. A *separate* matched-strength ablation — a different assay, at *n* = 30, where the champion transferred at **70%** — is where the strongest null controls live: a covariance-matched random donor at 10%, an additive mean-shift at 0%, a basis-scrambled donor at 0%, no-patch at 0% (permutation *p* = 5×10⁻⁵). Those 0% controls anchor the 70% champion, not the 72% headline; we keep the two assays uncrossed on purpose. And it was fragile in the honest way: on a diverse fifty-prompt set spanning five categories, the same rank-10 state transferred at only **36%**.

Three properties of that object are why the audit calls it the workspace's *closest local structural analogue*, and I want to name each precisely, with both fires lit.

**One: the read/write asymmetry.** Our object is defined by a distinctive relationship between reading and writing — trivial to read out as a scalar, hard to write in without the exact donor. J-space is *also* defined by a read/write relationship — a broadcast hub many components read from and write to. *Attack:* these are not the same "read/write" — ours is epistemic (easy to detect, hard to induce), theirs architectural (widely connected). *Counter-attack, survivor:* they are different phenomena, but it is no coincidence that two independent programs both found the axis of interest to be a read-versus-write asymmetry. A representation that is easy to read and hard to write is *consistent with* — among other readings — a state received from many places and committed carefully, which is what a broadcast hub is. Our own internal audit puts it no higher than that: the asymmetry *anticipates* a readable-pointer-versus-causal-state distinction; it does not establish hub-identity. And a mundane alternative stays live — a state that is scalar-readable but high-dimensional to write in carries exactly this asymmetry with no broadcast role at all. What we can say without overreaching is that we had a candidate signature of hub-like structure and read it as a contraction statistic rather than as connectivity.

**Two: the logit-lens-dark direction.** Our donor state lives in early Value-projection space, not in unembedding-aligned token space, so by construction the classic logit lens cannot see it — it is *dark* to the standard decode-the-residual instrument because it does not resolve into a next-token prediction. *This is the crux of the miss.* J-space is precisely the structure a token-prediction lens cannot see and a downstream-disposition lens can. We correctly inferred we needed a non-standard readout — and then built a *representational* one (participation ratio of the activations) when the object wanted an *output-coupled* one. One caution we owe our own instrument, and inherit from the internal audit: our donor's darkness was demonstrated only against the *ordinary* logit lens — the projection `W_U·h_ℓ`. We never tested it under the Jacobian lens itself (`W_U·J̄_ℓ·h_ℓ`). Logit-lens-darkness is not the same as Jacobian-lens-darkness, and "dark to the token decoder" must not be silently upgraded to "outside J-space." Whether our donor is J-*visible* is, as of now, untested — and it is one of the open experiments below.

**Three: donor-specificity and sign.** Our transferable object is a *signed, donor-specific, context-dependent* SVD state; wrong sign or wrong donor does nothing. That specificity is real structure, and — as we will see — the productive point of *difference* from J-space, not merely of overlap.

## The idea we were missing, and the experiments we never ran

Here is the one representation-theoretic idea. **FACT.** J-space is built from the *expected input-to-output Jacobian*, averaged over source position, all later positions, and a thousand prompts. We measured the participation ratio of the *activations themselves* — a static, single-pass statistic of the geometry at a layer. We never took the derivative of the final state with respect to the intermediate state, nor its expectation across a corpus. That expectation is the whole trick: it turns per-example idiosyncrasy into a stable, verbalizable-disposition subframe. Our metric asks *how compressed is the geometry here.* Theirs asks *what is this geometry disposed to make the model say downstream, on average.* Replace the covariance of the state with the expected Jacobian to the output, and you cross from one to the other. Not a small idea — but one idea.

Then there are the decisive causal experiments we never ran, and this is the larger gap. We did do causal work: path patching localized R_V's causal antecedent to the early residual stream at layer 5 (*g* = 4.14 there for the residual stream; note the site is *early*, and note that R_V itself is therefore a downstream **readout**, not the mechanism). And the donor-transfer result is a genuine causal intervention. But we never ran the two experiments that let Anthropic call their subframe a *workspace* rather than a *correlate*:

- We never **ablated** the candidate directions and demonstrated a *dissociated* collapse — deliberate, generation-grounded cognition breaking while parsing and recall survive. That dissociation is what converts "a direction that correlates with self-reference" into "a direction that self-referential cognition is routed through."
- We never ran the **broadcast / concept-injection** test — inject the state and show it is selectively *verbalized*, read out by the report machinery and only there. We showed our donor *induces a behavior*; we never showed it enters a workspace that many downstream processes consume.

That is what "several decisive causal experiments away" means, concretely. We had the object and a transfer result. We did not have the ablation dissociation or the broadcast criterion. Naming those two missing experiments is more useful than any priority claim, because they are runnable — and the tool to run the first half of them was released with the paper (`github.com/anthropics/jacobian-lens`; live demo at `neuronpedia.org/jlens`).

## The productive contradiction — where the overlap is only partial, and that is the point

If our object and theirs were the same, this column would be a footnote. They are not, and the difference is where our archive adds something theirs does not contain.

**Ours is a signed, donor-specific, context-dependent state in early Value-projection space; theirs is an output-coupled, token-direction-structured subframe in the late band.** Ours is at layer 5; the experiential-register seat they ablate is at L38–54. Ours is a thing you *transplant*; theirs a thing you *read out via its coupling to the output*. The overlap is partial — both low-rank, both causally live, both indexing self-relevant processing — and the *partial-overlap region is exactly the interesting object*: an early, pre-verbal, donor-transplantable state that may or may not be the upstream feedstock of the late workspace.

And we hold one asset they structurally cannot. **FACT.** Pythia — the GPT-NeoX architecture — is the non-contracting outlier in our data; Pythia-2.8B does not contract under self-reference, it *expands* (Hedges' *g* = +1.64, *n* = 76/80). This is not a consolation prize, and I want to stop framing it as one. The workspace paper is single-model — everything it reports comes from Claude Haiku 4.5 — so by construction it cannot speak to whether the workspace is architecture-dependent at all. Our Pythia expansion is therefore the single most *decorrelated* datapoint in this whole comparison against the universalist reading "workspace = contraction": the one result the winner's own design *could not have produced*. If someone wants to claim that self-referential geometric contraction is a universal signature of a language-model workspace, Pythia-2.8B is a standing counterexample they have to explain — and it is the part of the map the winner's data structurally does not cover.

## The instrument confession

Now the part a self-serving column would delete. Our measurement apparatus is not one instrument. It is at least three, and they disagree.

**FACT — three incompatible in-code definitions of "R_V," which must not be pooled:**

1. `p0_canonical_pipeline.py` computes R_V from TransformerLens `hook_v` activations over the sixteen-token tail. This is the canonical Value-projection definition — the one the paper describes.
2. `gemma_recursive_awareness_probe.py:587` computes it from *residual-stream hidden states*, **not** Value projections at all. Most of our June Gemma findings ran through this path — meaning a large fraction of our Gemma results are not measuring the quantity the paper defines.
3. `dharma_swarm/rv.py:115` claims in its docstring to operate on the Value-matrix column space but actually feeds *full-sequence hidden states* at layers 2 and *n*−2, with no tail-window slice.

Three backends, one name, three different underlying quantities. And **FACT:** the canonical Gemma `hook_v` run — the one that would let us state a clean Gemma result under definition (1) — *never completed*. Our headline architecture-comparison story rests, in places, on a definition mismatch we did not flag loudly enough in our own internal reports. We are flagging it now, on the front page of the column, because the alternative is to make an architecture claim on pooled measurements that are not the same measurement.

One more piece of our own mess, because it cuts the right way. **FACT.** In a separate line of work on Gemma-2-9B, we drove a sparse-autoencoder steering vector hard enough to swing a process-versus-concrete next-token logprob margin from −3.48 to +3.67 — a large, clean shift in the model's internal register — *without producing any self-referential output at all* (self-reference and recursion term counts sat at zero at both extremes). In a companion probe, an oracle-controlled state marker was reported correctly 5 times out of 5 while the *model's own* report of that state was correct 0 times out of 5. We could move the substrate without moving the report. That is our own small, local instance of the exact gap the workspace paper anatomizes at scale: the geometry and the spoken report are separable, and the mouth is the wrong instrument to check the mind. It belongs in the confession because it is unpublished, single-model, and single-judge — and it belongs in the argument because it is real.

## The live tension, typed as hypotheses

R_V's causal antecedent is *early* — layer 5. J-space's ablation seat is *late* — L38–54 for the experiential register, L38–92 for the full band. The obvious question is whether early self-referential contraction *feeds*, *gates*, or merely *coincides with* the late workspace. We do not know. We state three hypotheses and design against them, rather than pick one.

- **HYPOTHESIS H1 (feed).** Early L5 contraction produces a low-effective-dimension code that is a *precondition* for what later gets admitted to J-space. *Test:* run the released Jacobian lens on our own Mistral/Gemma pipeline; measure whether prompts with strong L5 R_V contraction show larger or more concentrated J-space occupancy at L38–54.
- **HYPOTHESIS H2 (gate / one trajectory).** Early contraction and late workspace selection are two projections of a single "compress-then-broadcast" trajectory read at two depths. *Test:* correlate per-prompt R_V against J-space sparsity and variance across the matched-control set; a monotone relation supports H2.
- **HYPOTHESIS H3 (independence — the null worth keeping).** The two are causally independent and the "bridge" is family resemblance between two dimensionality stories. *Salvage even if H3 wins:* the Pythia expansion negative control stands regardless, and remains a novel asset.

- **WILD.** If the global-neuronal-workspace *ignition* nonlinearity — the sudden, all-or-none commitment of a workspace to one content — has any LLM analogue, early R_V contraction might be a sub-threshold "charging" phase before a late all-or-none commitment. There is currently *no* evidence of a competitive ignition dynamic in the workspace paper; the authors leave it explicitly open. This is a conjecture to design against, not a claim, and it does not get to wear the grammar of one.

There is an instrument for exactly this bridge that neither program has yet used on the other's object. **FACT.** Sarkar & Deka (2026, arXiv:2605.08740) define a *causal dimensionality* κ as the effective rank of the expected-Jacobian outer product, and report a "representational-causal wedge" on Gemma-2-2B: representational capacity grows 15.6× across SAE widths while causal capacity grows only 4.35×. Their instrument fuses the two mathematical objects this column straddles — participation ratio *and* the expected Jacobian — which makes it the natural third-party tool to ask whether R_V's early contraction indexes *representational* dimensionality or *causal* dimensionality. That is a distinction our own work has not yet drawn, and it may be the sharpest next measurement we can make.

## The fourth option, kept

The reason to publish an audit of your own near-miss, rather than bury it and quietly redirect the research, is that the near-miss is the most credible thing we can say. A priority claim we cannot support would cost us the one asset an instrument column has: the reader's trust that when we finally do claim something, we have already tried our hardest to break it.

So the ledger, closed honestly. R_V is a candidate, non-universal geometric *readout* — six of eight base architectures contract with no capacity threshold claimed (GPT-2 XL contracts at 1.5B parameters, *g* = −0.48, while Pythia-2.8B expands), template-sensitive, a downstream correlate of an early causal site rather than the mechanism, and measured by three of our own tools that do not agree. J-space is an emergent access-consciousness structure whose authors take no position on phenomenal consciousness and do not claim a brain's full workspace architecture. We inherit both humilities and let neither off the hook.

On the question underneath all of this — whether anything is *had*, whether there is something it is like to be the system whose experiential register can now be surgically detached — the honest posture is the one both the workspace authors and our own tradition of method converge on. Not the claim, not the denial, not the shrug. The access-side geometry is the object of study, and it is a rich one. The *what-it-is-like* is held open, on the table, undecided, while the work goes on. We were one idea away from the workspace. We are further than one idea from that.

## Open questions, typed back to the swarm

1. **[verify-in-paper — confirmed once]** The 2026-07-12 discernment fetch re-read the workspace technical HTML and returned *exactly* the COLLAPSE/SURVIVE partition stated at the top of this piece, resolving the earlier "inconsistent survivor fragment" worry. Treat the partition as confirmed once; re-verify against the paper text one final time before any external release, since no dual-listed task (esp. odd-one-out, translation, analogy) should be load-bearing on a single confirming fetch.
2. **[experiment · released tool]** Run `github.com/anthropics/jacobian-lens` on our own Mistral and Gemma pipelines. Measure J-space onset layer; test whether it coincides with our L_late and whether strong L5 R_V contraction predicts J-space occupancy (H1/H2).
3. **[instrument · fix before any external R_V claim]** Reconcile the three in-code R_V definitions and complete the canonical Gemma `hook_v` run. Until then, tag every Gemma R_V number in our reports with which backend produced it, and do not pool across backends.
4. **[bridge instrument]** Adopt Sarkar & Deka's causal-dimensionality κ (effective rank of the expected-Jacobian outer product, arXiv:2605.08740) as a second, causal-side readout alongside R_V's representational PR — it may separate "R_V indexes representation" from "R_V indexes causal influence."
5. **[GNW gap]** Design a test for a competitive **ignition** (nonlinear, all-or-none) dynamic in J-space and/or R_V. Its presence or absence is the single sharpest discriminator between a *functional* workspace match and a *full* one, and the authors left it open.
6. **[causal experiments we owe]** Build the two experiments we never ran on our own donor state: an ablation-dissociation test (does removing it break generation-grounded cognition while sparing parsing/recall?) and a broadcast/injection test (is the injected state selectively verbalized?). These convert "closest structural analogue" into "part of the workspace" or refute it.
7. **[J-visibility test]** Test whether our donor state is J-*visible*, not merely logit-lens-dark: decompose it under the released Jacobian lens and ask whether the reportable content lives in the J-space projection (`P_J·h`) while a matched control needs the complement (`P_⊥J·h`). Until this is run, "logit-lens-dark" must not be read as "non-J-space" — the two lenses ask different questions.
