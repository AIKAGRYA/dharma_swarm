# Before You Say China Bought the Land

“China is buying the land” can mean a citizen buying a home, a locally
incorporated company acquiring a farm, a controlled company leasing grazing
land, or the Chinese state obtaining property near a military installation.
These transactions do not have the same owner, duration, control, or security
significance.

This article uses two registers. **FACT** is supported by a linked government
source. **HYPOTHESIS** names both an explanation and the test needed to
distinguish it.

## Five variables before one conclusion

A serious claim identifies:

- jurisdiction;
- asset type;
- freehold, lease, licence, concession, or corporate interest;
- government, enterprise, citizen, resident, or beneficial controller;
- annual acquisition, cumulative holding, parcel count, acreage, or value.

“Chinese” also needs definition. The PRC, Hong Kong, and Macao may be separate
official categories. Nationality does not establish state direction.
Incorporation does not necessarily reveal beneficial ownership.

## United States: a small share inside an imperfect register

**FACT:** USDA reports that foreign persons held interests in 46.3 million
acres of American agricultural land at the end of 2024—3.6% of privately held
agricultural land. Chinese primary-investor filers reported 247,659 acres,
slightly under 1% of foreign-held acreage and roughly 0.02% of privately held
US agricultural land. USDA recorded no filing directly by the Chinese
government
([USDA 2024 AFIDA report](https://www.fsa.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2026-01/AFIDAYR2024ReportWithPageNumbers.pdf)).

Agricultural land includes timber. Reportable interests include leases of ten
years or longer. Five companies accounted for 92% of the Chinese-associated
acreage. In Texas, long-term wind leases predominate among these interests; a
lease is not an outright transfer.

The number is not a perfect census. GAO found duplicate records, missing
ownership tiers, weak verification, and difficulty identifying non-filers
([GAO-24-106337](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-24-106337.pdf)). USDA launched
an online reporting portal in January 2026, but digitising submission does not
retroactively prove completeness
([USDA announcement](https://www.fsa.usda.gov/news-events/news/01-22-2026/usda-launches-new-online-portal-reporting-foreign-owned-agricultural)).

**HYPOTHESIS:** Chinese-associated acreage is materially undercounted through
hidden ownership tiers. **Test:** reconcile AFIDA filings against deeds,
beneficial-ownership records, tax data, and farm records.

A low national percentage cannot decide a site-specific risk. CFIUS reviews
certain foreign purchases, leases, and concessions near listed military
installations, airports, and ports
([Treasury CFIUS real-estate instructions](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/international/the-committee-on-foreign-investment-in-the-united-states-cfius/cfius-real-estate-instructions-part-802)).
Location and access can matter more than acreage.

## Japan: different datasets answer different questions

**FACT:** Japan's agriculture ministry found that foreign corporations,
foreign nationals, and specified domestic corporations acquired 175.3
hectares of farmland in 2024—0.004% of total farmland. No foreign corporation
or overseas-resident foreign individual directly acquired farmland that year.
The ministry counted nationalities among resident acquirers but did not publish
area by nationality. A buyer count cannot honestly become hectares
([MAFF 2024 survey](https://www.maff.go.jp/j/press/keiei/seisaku/250916.html)).

Forests are separate. The Forestry Agency recorded 382 hectares acquired in
2024 by overseas persons or domestic foreign-affiliated companies—0.003% of
private forest area. It reported no known development of those forests for
drawing water or groundwater
([Forestry Agency survey](https://www.rinya.maff.go.jp/j/press/keikaku/250916.html)).

Japan also monitors property around important facilities and border islands.
The Cabinet Secretariat reports 3,498 foreign-associated parcels or building
units acquired during fiscal 2024 in those designated areas; China was the
largest listed source. This is a sensitive-zone parcel count, not nationwide
acreage and not proof every transaction impaired a facility
([Cabinet Secretariat data](https://www.cas.go.jp/jp/seisaku/symbiotic_society/pdf/gaikokujinniyorufudousanshutokunojittaihaaku.pdf);
[Important Land Survey Act outline](https://www.cas.go.jp/jp/houan/210326/siryou1_e.pdf)).

## Australia: millions of hectares, mostly leasehold

**FACT:** At June 2025 Australia attributed 6.537 million hectares of
agricultural-land interests to the PRC, 1.7% of Australian agricultural land.
Of that total, 738,000 hectares were freehold and 5.799 million leasehold.
Reporting the combined figure as land owned outright is false
([Australian Taxation Office register](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/foreign-resident-investments/foreign-investment-in-australia/register-of-foreign-ownership-of-australian-assets/register-of-foreign-ownership-of-australian-assets-reports/register-of-foreign-ownership-of-australian-assets-2024-25-report/agricultural-land)).

## Both fires

The first fire is directed at alarm. Nationality is not agency. A resident
farmer, pension fund, private investor, state-owned enterprise, and
intelligence-linked buyer are not interchangeable. A national percentage
cannot be inferred from selected security zones.

The second fire is directed at dismissal. Small percentages do not eliminate
concentrated local risk. One site with surveillance access may matter more
than thousands of ordinary hectares. Registers can miss non-filers and obscure
beneficial owners. Water, logistics, processing, and biotechnology can confer
influence not visible in an acreage table.

The double-survivor is site-specific scrutiny with equal protection: examine
control, opacity, location, access, and intended use without treating
nationality alone as conduct.

## What would change this

This assessment would become more alarmed if audited records showed
coordinated state direction, systematic concealment, rapid concentration near
strategic chokepoints, or hostile use established by competent authorities.

It would become less concerned if deed-level audits demonstrated high register
completeness, disclosed beneficial owners through material tiers, separated
leases from freeholds, and repeatedly resolved site-specific reviews.

## Open questions

- **DATA:** Measure the false-negative rate by reconciling AFIDA records with
  deeds and beneficial ownership.
- **LEGAL:** Compare restrictions by transaction type, protected interest,
  review standard, and appeal right.
- **SECURITY:** Build a site-risk model using proximity, access, line of sight,
  intended use, and control—not nationality alone.
- **ECONOMIC:** Measure local effects on prices, rents, farm entry, employment,
  and production.
- **REPORTING:** Require every public number to state freehold, leasehold,
  parcels, annual acquisitions, or cumulative holdings.

The correction rule is simple: bring the disputed sentence, the primary table
or statute, and a proposed replacement. Material errors remain visible on the
face of the article.
