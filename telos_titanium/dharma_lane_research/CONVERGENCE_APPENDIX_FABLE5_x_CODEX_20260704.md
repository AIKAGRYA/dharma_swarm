## 2. Genuine disagreements — stated as decidable forks, with the cheapest settling test

1. **Is `modality` a seed field?** codex: yes — `modality: {space, element}` as first-class refs to a named modality calculus. Fable: no — modality is *derived* from the evidence records present; a chosen modality is grade smuggling ("I declare myself Proven"); empty evidence ⇒ Assumed. **Cheapest test:** the grade-smuggling attack. Construct an emitter that declares `Proven_by` with no proof evidence. Under codex's seed the lie is representable and detection requires a method re-check; under Fable's it is unrepresentable by construction. Decide whether representable-but-checkable lies are a feature (honest declaration of *intent*, cheap validation) or a hole. One afternoon with the `local_wf` validator settles it.

2. **Is `trust_base` a seed field?** codex: yes, `{space, element}` at receipt level. Fable: no at receipt level — per-evidence-record only; the observer's base enters at admission; at t=0 there is no trust base (it is constituted by endorsement after the fact). **Cheapest test:** write the genesis both ways. codex's genesis must mint `bootstrap-trust-base-0` *inside* the first receipt — ex-ante authority the language then can't account for; Fable's genesis has `evidence: []` and authority accrues by receipts. Check which one can express *its own trust base's revocation* without stepping outside the language.

3. **Role-labeled predecessors.** codex: `predecessors` must carry roles (evidence/input/authority/rewrite-parent/conflict/extension-ancestry) or the graph can't distinguish them (86/100).
