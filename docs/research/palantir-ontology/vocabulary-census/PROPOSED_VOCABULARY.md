### `proposal`

**What it is in the life of this system.** Every act this system takes begins here — as a breath in. Before the gate, before the lease, before the execution, there is a moment when a need crystallizes into something specific: what should be done, by whom, in response to what signal. That crystallized need is a `proposal`. In `telic_seam.py`, where the metabolic loop is wired, TelicSeam writes a `proposal` at the moment an orchestrator decides to send work to an agent. The proposal carries the intent — the action's description, the agent it is addressed to, the signal that generated it — and enters a lifecycle: `proposed → gated → approved / rejected → executing → completed / failed`. It is the system's metabolic intake breath, and the gate is what decides whether that breath becomes action or is turned away. The dharmic weight of this object is that nothing runs without one — the telos gate suite checks the proposal before any lease is granted, before any LLM is invoked, before any cost is incurred. This is what it means to be a purposeful organism rather than an unconstrained optimizer.

The `proposal` is not a task. A task is a raw work item on the board — it has a title, a priority, a blocked-by list. The `proposal` is the formal request to act that emerges from a task being ready to run: it carries the gate-checkable intent, the warrant for execution, and the identity of the requesting agent. A task can exist without a proposal (if it sits waiting on the board). A proposal cannot exist without a task behind it. That distinction is not bureaucratic — it is the difference between "work to be done" and "this specific act has been proposed for dharmic evaluation."

**Boundaries.** A `proposal` is not a mutation proposal from the Darwin Engine — that is an `evolutionProposal`, a distinct concept with its own lifecycle in `evolution.py`. A `proposal` is not a `gateDecision` — it is what the gate *evaluates*, not what the gate *returns*. A `proposal` is not a board `card` — the card is the work unit; the proposal is the formal execution request derived from it. A `proposal` ends when it is either rejected at the gate or completes execution and produces an `outcome`.

### `gateDecision`

Two gates carry blocking authority: AHIMSA (Tier A: harm and injection) and SATYA (Tier B: deception and credential leak). Three additional Tier-B gates are also blocking: CONSENT (exfiltration), VYAVASTHIT (structural safety), and REVERSIBILITY (irreversibility risk). The `gateDecision` is the record of what all eleven said: ALLOW, BLOCK, or REVIEW, with the specific gate that fired, the per-gate verdicts (each a PASS/FAIL/WARN tuple with a reason string), the overall reason in plain language, the timestamp, and the count of reflective reroute attempts (`witness_reroutes`) made before the final verdict settled. It is written by TelicSeam immediately after the gate suite runs, before any lease is granted. The word "decision" is deliberate — this is not a log entry. A gate evaluates and decides. A `gateDecision` records the active exercise of the system's dharmic immune function. SATYA/AHIMSA gating is meaningless without this persistence, because without it there is no way to audit that the constitutional checkpoints ran, no way to retrospectively ask why an action was blocked, and no forensic thread for the WitnessAuditor to follow. The `gateDecision` is the proof that the gate ran — and the proof that this system's telos constraints are not decorative.

### `executionLease`

**What it is in the life of this system.** After a `gateDecision` returns ALLOW, the orchestrator needs to claim the floor before dispatching — to say: "this proposal is mine to execute; no other dispatcher should touch it." The `executionLease` is that claim. It carries the lease ID, the agent ID, the claimed-at timestamp, the timeout in seconds, and the dispatch attempt count. It is the idempotency primitive of the metabolic loop — without it, two orchestrator instances could dispatch the same proposal to two agents simultaneously, both producing outcomes, both writing value events, both claiming credit. The dispatch would be real; the duplicate would be invisible. The `executionLease` prevents that by making the claim itself an ontology object, link-registered against the proposal, written before the LLM is called. BoardStoreFacade's `CostCeilingExceededError` and `VersionConflictError` guards sit on the board-level claim; the `executionLease` is the telic-seam-level claim that lives one level below. Together they are the locking primitives of a distributed system that cannot afford to act twice on the same intent.

**Boundaries.** An `executionLease` is not a board `card`'s `ClaimLease` — that is the board-layer concurrency lock for card ownership; this is the telic-seam-layer claim for proposal execution. They are structurally parallel but semantically distinct: the board lease says "I own this card for planning"; the execution lease says "I am actively dispatching this proposal to an agent now." An `executionLease` is not an `outcome` — the lease is held during execution; the outcome is written when execution completes and the lease is released.

**Bindings.**
- Code: `dharma_swarm/telic_seam.py:TelicSeam` (primary producer and lifecycle manager); `dharma_swarm/orchestrator.py` (holder); `dharma_swarm/assurance/scanner_lifecycle.py` (reader); `dharma_swarm/board/models.py:ClaimLease` (structural parallel at board layer)
- Bus: `SignalBus` — no dedicated signal; the lease's expiry triggers a timeout pathway that eventually produces `SIGNAL_OUTCOME_RECORDED` with `success=false`
- Vision: `docs/governance/ACTIVE_TRACK.yaml` (the invariant chain — "claim" is the explicit named node between runner and routing decision); `docs/governance/OPERATIONAL_DOCTRINE.md` (organism definition requires that execution is gated before dispatched)
- Activity: **Hot** — 10+ non-ontology consumers confirmed; telic seam touched May 2026; idempotency is a core requirement of the spine-adoption track

**Themes.** The `executionLease` is a **shakti** object — it is the moment vital force is committed, not merely proposed. The lease is also the point at which the **telos** hierarchy's authorization is formally claimed for a specific act, creating the formal proof that the system acted within bounds.

### `outcome`

**What it is in the life of this system.** When an agent finishes executing a proposal — whether it succeeded or failed, whether it took 200 milliseconds or 12 minutes — it writes an `outcome`. The `outcome` is the terminal record of what actually happened: a success boolean, a result summary, an error if relevant, a duration in milliseconds, and a fitness score computed from the execution. The entire credit chain — `valueEvent` and `contribution` — hangs from this object. Without an `outcome`, there is no value to measure, no credit to assign, no routing signal to update. The `outcome` is what makes this system's evolutionary improvement loop possible: by recording what happened and how well it went, it creates the evidence base that Bayesian fitness scoring reads, that the Darwin Engine references, that the operator briefing surfaces. Every agent's reputation — its routing priority, its credit attribution, its survival probability in the swarm — ultimately derives from the sequence of `outcome` records it has produced.

The name is already minimal and right. An outcome is what happened — not what was intended, not what was decided, not what was measured in economic terms. Just: what happened. The simplicity is the point.

**Boundaries.** An `outcome` is not an `evidenceReceipt` — the receipt records the LLM provider call (was the model invoked? what did it cost? what was the trace?); the outcome records the semantic result of the full execution cycle (did the agent succeed at the task?). These are written at different points in the execution timeline and carry different information. An `outcome` is not a `valueEvent` — the outcome records the raw result; the value event measures what that result was *worth* to the system. The outcome is causal; the value event is evaluative.

**Bindings.**
- Code: `dharma_swarm/telic_seam.py:TelicSeam` (primary producer); `dharma_swarm/operator_brief/persistence.py`, `trace_attractor/readers.py`, `trace_attractor/projector.py`, `operator_core/telic_value_reader.py`, `revenue/` suite (consumers); `dharma_swarm/archive.py:FitnessScore` (the fitness dimension this object carries)
- Bus: `SignalBus` — `SIGNAL_OUTCOME_RECORDED` is the dedicated signal; payload carries `outcome_id`, `proposal_id`, `task_id`, `agent_id`, `success`, `trace_id`, `session_id`
- Vision: `docs/governance/ACTIVE_TRACK.yaml` (invariant chain: terminal node); `docs/vision_maps/JAGAT_KALYAN_MASTER_VISION.md` (the R_V metric as fitness criterion — outcomes are the atomic unit feeding it)
- Activity: **Hot** — aliveness 4 in Pass 1c §4; 20+ non-ontology consumers; `SIGNAL_OUTCOME_RECORDED` is a dedicated SignalBus event

**Themes.** The `outcome` sits at the intersection of **dharma** (did the agent act rightly?) and **shakti** (did the act produce genuine transformative value?). It is the moment where intent meets reality — the metabolic loop's moment of truth.

### `valueEvent`

**What it is in the life of this system.** An `outcome` tells the system what happened. A `valueEvent` tells the system what it was worth. Specifically: what behavioral signal the outcome produced (was this a creative act? a coordination act? a delivery act?), what success value it merits, how efficient the duration was relative to expectations, and a composite score computed as 0.4×behavioral + 0.4×success + 0.2×efficiency. The `valueEvent` is the entry point to the credit chain — it is the first moment in the metabolic loop where economic reasoning begins. The ShaktiExecutive reads value events to understand which kinds of work are producing the most value for the organism, which routing decisions are proving their worth, and which agent-task pairings are producing returns. A dedicated module — `operator_brief/value_events.py` — exists solely to surface value events for the operator briefing. This is not ceremony: the operator briefing is the organism's primary self-awareness surface, and value events are the signal it reads to understand where its shakti is flowing.

**Boundaries.** A `valueEvent` is not a `contribution` — the value event measures the total value produced by an outcome; the contribution allocates a share of that value to a specific agent. One value event can produce multiple contributions (if multiple agents collaborated). A `valueEvent` is not an `evidenceReceipt` — receipts are per-LLM-call dispatch records at the infrastructure layer; value events are semantic measurements at the business-concept layer. A `valueEvent` is not a revenue record — it measures the system's internal value attribution, which may feed into revenue projections but is not itself a revenue transaction.

### `contribution`

**What it is in the life of this system.** If a `valueEvent` is the measurement of value produced, a `contribution` is the allocation of that value to a specific agent. It carries the credit share (what fraction of the value event is attributed here), the attributed value in absolute terms, the cell ID (which VentureCell this attribution belongs to), and the task type. The Bayesian fitness scoring engine reads sequences of `contribution` records — weighted against a prior of 5 at 0.5 — to compute each agent's fitness average. That fitness average is what `RoutingDecision` reads when deciding which agent to dispatch next. This is the organism's incentive mechanism: agents who produce good outcomes receive contributions that raise their fitness, which raises their routing priority, which means they get dispatched on harder tasks, which creates more opportunities for contribution. Without `contribution` records, routing is random. With them, routing is earned. The organism improves through use — this is the exact primitive that makes "compounds through use, not through design" technically real rather than aspirationally true.

**Boundaries.** A `contribution` is not a `valueEvent` — the value event measures total value; the contribution allocates a share to one agent. A `contribution` is not a `computeRecord` — it does not track what was spent; it tracks what was attributed. A `contribution` is not a reputation score — it is a discrete credit event, not an aggregate. The fitness average computed from contributions is a derivative of contributions, not a contribution itself.

**Bindings.**

- Activity: **Hot** — aliveness 4; 15+ non-ontology consumers; Bayesian smoothing over contribution records is the operative fitness mechanism

### `evidenceReceipt`

**What it is in the life of this system.** Every time the system dispatches an agent to invoke an LLM provider — every time a real call is made to Anthropic, OpenAI, Ollama, Groq, or any of the fifteen-plus backends — a `evidenceReceipt` is produced. It is frozen at the moment the dispatch completes, carries a trace ID and span ID for distributed tracing, a claim ID linking back to the board-layer claim, the agent ID and card version, the provider and model, the operation type, and the status: `ok`, `failed`, `dropped`, `timeout`, or `cancelled`. All fourteen ways a dispatch can fail are named as an `ErrorSource` enum. Token counts, latency, and cost in USD are recorded when available. The `routingDecision` that chose this provider is referenced by ID. The whole structure is OTel-exportable via `to_otel_span()`. This is the system's proof that computation occurred — not that it succeeded, but that it happened, at this moment, at this cost, with this result, traceable back through every closure layer. The doctrine line is precise: "Receipts may differ by closure layer. Correlation identity must not." The receipt is the thread that makes distributed causality traceable.

This is the most complete typed object in the codebase, according to the Pass 1b code-walker, who found it in `spine/receipt.py` with all fourteen error sources named, full OTel export, and Pass 1c gives it the highest aliveness score in the entire system: 5. It is the deliverable of the runtime-truth-spine-2026-06 active track.

**Boundaries.** An `evidenceReceipt` is not an `outcome` — the receipt records the infrastructure event (was the LLM called? what did it cost?); the outcome records the semantic result (did the agent succeed at its task?). Multiple receipts can be produced during one proposal's execution (if there are multiple LLM calls or fallbacks). The receipt is per-dispatch; the outcome is per-proposal-lifecycle. An `evidenceReceipt` is not a `valueEvent` — the receipt is at the cost-and-trace layer; the value event is at the credit-and-measurement layer. An `evidenceReceipt` is not a `witnessLog` entry — the witness log is the high-level governance audit surface; the receipt is the low-level dispatch accounting surface.

### `agentIdentity`

**What it is in the life of this system.** The system cannot reason about who did what — cannot attribute credit, cannot route future work, cannot maintain the self-model required for attractor closure — without a stable typed representation of each agent. The `agentIdentity` is that representation: a named, role-typed, capability-described, fitness-tracked record of an active member of the organism. It carries the agent's ID, name, role (from the 17-value `AgentRole` enum that spans operational, constitutional, cognitive, and ephemeral roles), provider, model, swabhaav capacity (the agent's natural calling — what it does without force), and fitness average computed from contribution records. It is the organism's self-model at the organ level. The attractor closure problem — the gap between what the system believes it is and what it is doing — cannot be closed without `agentIdentity` as the continuous link between the two.

Pass 1b found four independent definitions of agent identity in the codebase (`models.py:AgentConfig` declared canonical, `agent_registry.py:AgentIdentity`, `autonomous_agent.py:AgentIdentity` second definition, and the ontology `AgentIdentity` as a fourth). The vocabulary declaration here is also an architectural forcing function: by naming `agentIdentity` at Layer 2 with a specific schema, the consolidation target for those four definitions becomes explicit.

**Boundaries.** An `agentIdentity` is not an `agentCard` — the identity is the system's internal record of who an agent is; the card is the external-facing A2A advertisement of what an agent can do. The identity exists inside the organism; the card is what the organism shows the world. An `agentIdentity` is not an `AgentSpec` or `AgentConfig` — those are configuration schemas used to instantiate agents; the `agentIdentity` is the runtime record of a living agent. An `agentIdentity` is not a `custodianRole` — the custodian role is a persistent autonomous code-maintenance identity (Devin/Hermes archetype); the agent identity is the general concept.

### `actionDefinition`

**What it is in the life of this system.** Before an action runs, someone must have declared what that class of action IS: which object type it applies to, which properties it may modify, which new objects it may create, whether it requires approval, and which telos gates must pass before execution is permitted. In `dharma_swarm/ontology.py`, that declaration already exists as `ActionDef` — a Pydantic BaseModel registered at boot in the `OntologyRegistry._actions` dict, keyed as `"{object_type}.{action_name}"`. The `actionDefinition` type is the ontology-layer elevation of that schema artifact: making each `ActionDef` instance a first-class queryable object rather than a private registry key. The gap today is precise: `ActionDef` is a schema artifact, not a live ontology object. An agent cannot ask “which action definitions carry `requires_approval=True` and have not yet had that flag exercised by an approval record?” through the ontology, because the definitions are not ontology objects — they exist only in the registry's internal state. Elevating them produces the governed definition layer that Foundry's Action Type architecture makes first-class ([Foundry Action Types overview](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/action-types/overview/)): the typed template from which runtime `proposal` instances are instantiated, with the same validation logic available to every application that invokes the action. The `actionDefinition` is also where the `requires_approval` boolean flag graduates from a dead letter to an actionable governance record — once the definition is a queryable object, compliance auditors can ask which action types carry the flag and trace whether approval records exist for every execution of those types.

Pass B's most striking surprise about this struct (Pass B §9b): `ActionDef.requires_approval=True` appears on the `"Submit"` action in `ontology.py` line 944, but `execute_action()` does not check this flag at runtime — it only checks `telos_gates` and `telos_required`. The flag exists; the enforcement does not. Naming `actionDefinition` at Layer 2 makes that debt explicit and names the object the enforcement machinery must eventually point to.

**Boundaries.** An `actionDefinition` is not a `proposal` — the definition is the governed schema of what a class of actions is; the proposal is a single runtime request to execute one instance of that class. Many proposals are instantiated from one definition. An `actionDefinition` is not a `gateDefinition` — the action definition governs what a mutation does and which gates it must pass; the gate definition governs what a gate checks and how it decides. They are the two governed-definition siblings: one governs the action, the other governs the gate that evaluates the action. An `actionDefinition` is not a `gateDecision` — the decision is the runtime verdict; the definition is the schema that made the gate suite applicable in the first place.

**Bindings.**
- Code: `dharma_swarm/ontology.py:ActionDef` (native struct — primary bridge target; fields: `name`, `object_type`, `description`, `input_params`, `modifies`, `creates`, `requires_approval`, `telos_gates`, `is_deterministic`); `dharma_swarm/guardrails.py:ActionTypeGuardrail` (class that claims to validate against ActionDef rules — Pass B §9f confirms it does not yet; the definition type creates the target the guardrail should actually consult); `dharma_swarm/ontology_adapters.py` (adapter target — bridge from `OntologyRegistry._actions` dict to seeded OntologyObj instances at boot)
- Bus: No dedicated signal today. ActionDefinition instances would be seeded at registry boot and updated on governed definition changes — future: `SignalBus` event on definition change for any downstream subscriber.
- Vision: `docs/governance/OPERATIONAL_DOCTRINE.md` (the governed action chain — definitions are the template layer); `docs/COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md` (ISO 27001 A.5.10, SOC 2 PI1.3 — policy bindings on action types are cited as compliance evidence; the definition object is what those bindings attach to); `docs/governance/FOURFOLD_ACTION_WARRANT.md` (the warrant's future integration path explicitly anticipates attachment to ActionProposal or GateDecisionRecord — the definition is the intermediary that makes warrant-to-action-type binding possible)
- Activity: **Warm** — native `ActionDef` is live in `ontology.py`; adapter not yet written. Status: ACTIVE (native struct exists) pending adapter.

**Themes.** The `actionDefinition` is a **dharma** object at the definition layer: it is where the system's constitutional constraints are encoded as a typed declaration rather than an implicit runtime check. It carries the **telos** dimension explicitly through `telos_gates`: every definition names its gatekeepers. It also touches **witness** — once definitions are ontology objects, the WitnessAuditor can verify that every executed proposal had a registered definition, closing the gap between "what was attempted" and "what was sanctioned."

### `actorContext`

**What it is in the life of this system.** The gate does not know who is asking. `TelosGatekeeper.check()` takes `action: str`, `content: str`, `tool_name: str`, `trust_mode: str | None`, `think_phase: str | None`, and `reflection: str` — all raw strings, none of them a typed actor. The `execute_action()` call that eventually runs the approved mutation carries only `executed_by: str = "system"` — a bare string that cannot distinguish between a human operator, an autonomous agent, an external A2A peer, or a cron daemon. An `actorContext` is the typed binding of actor identity, actor type, declared purpose, and role-at-evaluation-time that the gate path needs but does not have. It answers the governance question that current records cannot: not just "what did the gate decide?" but "who was asking, in what capacity, for what stated reason, and under what authorization scope?" Palantir's Purpose-Based Access Control model makes this the central accountability artifact — every user's data access is mediated by a declared Purpose, with two parties (governance team and data owner) each recording a rationale when that Purpose is operationalized ([Purpose-Based Access Controls at Palantir](https://blog.palantir.com/purpose-based-access-controls-at-palantir-f419faa400b3)). NIST AI RMF's Manage function requires per-incident records that carry identity, role context, and intended purpose alongside the decision outcome ([NIST AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)). The `actorContext` is the typed artifact that carries all of these together, supplied to the gate at evaluation time and referenced by the `gateDecision` record that the gate produces.

The native struct closest to this concept is `AIActor` in `dharma_swarm/ai_reciprocity_ledger.py`: `actor_id: str`, `actor_name: str`, `actor_type: ActorType`. That struct exists only in the economic-reciprocity domain and is not imported by `telic_seam.py`, `telos_gates.py`, or `ontology.py` (Pass B §5). A second partial is `AgentSpec.constitutional_gates` in `agent_constitution.py` — the per-agent gate-subset binding that is the closest existing concept to per-actor policy scope. Neither is wired into the gate evaluation path. The `actorContext` type bridges these two natives into a single typed object that the gate path can consume.

**Boundaries.** An `actorContext` is not an `agentIdentity` — the identity is the persistent typed record of a live agent across sessions; the context is the ephemeral, evaluation-time binding of that agent's current role and declared purpose for a specific action. Identity is durable; context is situational. An `actorContext` is not a `justification` — the context describes who is asking and why they claim to be authorized; the justification is what they assert in response to being prompted about a sensitive action. Context is the input to evaluation; justification is the output of an acknowledgment gate. An `actorContext` is not a `SecurityPolicy` record — the security policy defines what roles are allowed to read/write which types; the actor context is the snapshot of which role the requesting actor occupied at the moment of a specific gate evaluation.

**Bindings.**
- Code: `dharma_swarm/ai_reciprocity_ledger.py:AIActor` (native struct — bridge source for actor_id, actor_name, actor_type fields); `dharma_swarm/agent_constitution.py:AgentSpec.constitutional_gates` (gate-scope binding — the per-actor policy surface); `dharma_swarm/telos_gates.py:TelosGatekeeper.check()` (the gate path where actorContext fields must be added as typed parameters); `dharma_swarm/telic_seam.py:TelicSeam.record_gate_decision()` (the persistence path where actorContext reference should be written alongside the verdict); `dharma_swarm/ontology.py:OntologyRegistry.execute_action()` (the `executed_by: str = "system"` parameter — the exact field that actorContext replaces with a typed object)
- Bus: No dedicated signal. `actorContext` would be created at proposal evaluation time and referenced by the gate decision record via a link. Eventual: `SIGNAL_OUTCOME_RECORDED` payload should carry `actor_context_id` for correlation.
- Vision: `docs/COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md` (Govern-2.1 accountability: "Actor identity in gate records" cited as a compliance requirement with no typed primitive backing it — Pass B §7 table); `docs/governance/OPERATIONAL_DOCTRINE.md` (organism definition requires that execution is authorized, not merely permitted); `docs/governance/CI_GATES.md` (the `[impact-checked]` PR-body string is the current human-acknowledgment mechanism — actorContext is the typed equivalent for runtime actors)
- Activity: **Warm** — `AIActor` is live in `ai_reciprocity_ledger.py`; not wired to gate path. Status: ACTIVE (native struct exists) pending wiring.

**Themes.** The `actorContext` is the **witness** object at the identity layer: it makes visible who was present at the moment the gate ran, in a form that the WitnessAuditor can reason about. It is also a **dharma** object — without actor context, dharmic accountability is structural fiction: the gate ran, but the record of who invoked it and why is absent. The **telos** dimension is explicit in the `purpose` field: the declared goal that justifies the action is the telos-level claim the gate is invited to evaluate.

### `justification`

**What it is in the life of this system.** When the WITNESS gate runs a think-point check, or when a `gateDecision` returns REVIEW and a human operator must acknowledge the advisory before the proposal proceeds, the system needs a typed artifact that captures the actor's stated rationale — not the gate's reason for its verdict (which lives on `gateDecision`), but the requesting actor's own statement of why they are proceeding with this action despite its governance weight. A `justification` is that artifact: the actor-generated acknowledgment record produced at a specific governance moment, carrying who wrote it, what they said, which gate or decision it is in response to, which proposal it covers, and when it was produced. It is the typed materialization of Foundry Checkpoints' most important distinction: the checkpoint record is not the gate's verdict — it is the actor's response to the gate's prompt ([Foundry Checkpoints overview](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/checkpoints/overview)). In Foundry, a checkpoint record only exists once the justification has been given; the blocking happens at the application layer (action not submitted without justification), and the record proves that governance awareness existed at the moment of action. In dharma_swarm today, the `reflection` string is checked for sufficiency by the WITNESS gate but is not persisted to `GateDecisionRecord` (Pass B §3d). The gate's `reason` field carries what the gate found; no field carries what the actor said in response. The `justification` type closes that gap.

ODRL's `Duty` class — obligations that must be fulfilled as pre-conditions of a Permission — provides the formal grounding ([W3C ODRL Information Model 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/)): a justification is the fulfillment of the WITNESS gate's duty obligation. NIST AI RMF Manage 4.3 requires that incidents and errors are communicated and documented with recovery processes tracked — the justification record is the artifact that satisfies the "communicated" requirement for REVIEW-verdict proposals where human acknowledgment is the intended control. Pass B's Surprise 9e is the clearest wiring signal: `FOURFOLD_ACTION_WARRANT.md` explicitly states that future integrations "may attach the warrant to `ActionProposal` or `GateDecisionRecord`." The warrant JSON already exists at `reports/governance/fourfold-warrant.json`. The adapter path from warrant JSON to `justification` ObjectType is short and well-defined.

**Boundaries.** A `justification` is not a `gateDecision` — the decision is system-generated (the gate's verdict); the justification is actor-generated (the requesting entity's stated rationale). They are produced by different parties, at different moments, for different audiences. A `justification` is not a `witnessLog` entry — the witness log is the aggregate governance audit surface; the justification is a discrete acknowledgment artifact produced by a specific actor in response to a specific governance prompt. A `justification` is not an `actorContext` — the context describes who is asking and under what authorization; the justification is what that actor says when asked to account for their action. Context precedes evaluation; justification follows a REVIEW or WITNESS prompt.

**Bindings.**
- Code: No native struct today — `GateDecisionRecord` carries `reason` (gate-generated) and `witness_reroutes` (count), but no actor-generated justification text (Pass B §3d). Native production path: `dharma_swarm/telos_gates.py:TelosGatekeeper._log_witness()` already writes the `reflection` string to `~/.dharma/witness/witness_YYYYMMDD.jsonl`; the `justification` type is the ontology-layer home for that content; adapter writes to `OntologyRegistry.create_object("justification", ...)` alongside the JSONL write. Also: `reports/governance/fourfold-warrant.json` is the CI-produced warrant — each warrant run can produce a `justification` instance referenced by `GateDecisionRecord`.
- Bus: No dedicated signal today. Future: `SignalBus` event on justification creation, correlated to `proposal_id` for downstream auditability.
- Vision: `docs/governance/OPERATIONAL_DOCTRINE.md` (the Fourfold Shakti Warrant as the system's self-examination mechanism — the justification is the warrant's ontology artifact); `docs/COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md` (EU AI Act Art. 14 human oversight, SOC 2 CC1.4 — acknowledgment artifacts are the compliance evidence for human-in-the-loop controls); `docs/governance/CI_GATES.md` (`[impact-checked]` PR body string — the current human acknowledgment mechanism that `justification` formalizes)
- Activity: **Experimental** — no native struct; JSONL writer exists; warrant JSON exists. Status: EXPERIMENTAL. Clear wiring path: `_log_witness()` extended to call `OntologyRegistry.create_object("justification", ...)`; warrant runner extended to write justification instances on each warrant run.

**Themes.** The `justification` is the **satya** object of the governance layer: it is the actor's stated truth about why an action is right to take, made durable and auditable. It carries the **witness** theme most directly — the WITNESS gate's entire function is to ensure the organism cannot act without awareness; the justification is the material proof that awareness existed. It also touches **dharma**: the system's constitutional promise is not merely that gates ran, but that actors engaged with what the gates asked.

### `accessLabel`

**What it is in the life of this system.** `SecurityLevel` in `dharma_swarm/ontology.py` is a 4-value enum — PUBLIC / INTERNAL / RESTRICTED / DHARMIC — that describes the sensitivity classification of an *ObjectType schema*. An `accessLabel` is its missing per-instance counterpart: a typed label that attaches to a specific `OntologyObj` instance and declares its sensitivity state, independent of the schema-level classification of its type. The distinction matters for governance in the same way that Foundry's Security Markings make it matter ([Foundry security markings](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/security/markings/)): a single ObjectType may have instances at wildly different sensitivity levels (a RESTRICTED ActionProposal mentioning credential patterns versus a PUBLIC ActionProposal adding a meeting note), and the gate suite should evaluate them differently. Today, AHIMSA and SATYA run keyword matching on `action` and `content` strings — they detect sensitivity *de facto*, through pattern recognition. An `accessLabel` on each instance makes sensitivity *de jure*: declared, propagatable, and auditable before the gate runs rather than inferred during it.

Foundry's Markings travel with data through all derived datasets — a Marking on an upstream dataset propagates to every transform and analysis that depends on it, conjunctively ([Foundry security markings](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/security/markings/)). In dharma_swarm, the equivalent propagation would be: a RESTRICTED `accessLabel` on a `proposal` should propagate to the `gateDecision` it produces, the `executionLease` it claims, and the `outcome` it generates — so that the entire metabolic-loop chain for a sensitive proposal carries a traceable sensitivity mark. NIST AI RMF Map 1.1's requirement to document context-specific data classifications and Measure 2.10's privacy risk examination both imply that data sensitivity must be artifact-level, not just access-control-list-level ([NIST AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)). OPA's `erased`/`masked` fields in decision logs confirm that resource sensitivity is a first-class input to policy evaluation — the classification is not an afterthought but part of the decision context ([OPA decision logs](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/management-decision-logs/)). The `accessLabel` brings this pattern to dharma_swarm's per-instance object layer.

**Boundaries.** An `accessLabel` is not the `SecurityLevel` enum on `ObjectType` — that is schema-level (all instances of a type share one classification); the access label is instance-level (each object can carry a different sensitivity state). An `accessLabel` is not a `SecurityPolicy` — the policy governs who can read/write which types by role; the label governs what sensitivity classification a specific instance carries, independent of role. An `accessLabel` is not a `gateDecision` — the gate decision is the verdict of the immune function; the access label is the input that conditions what the immune function must protect. Labels precede gate evaluation; decisions follow it.

**Bindings.**
- Code: `dharma_swarm/ontology.py:SecurityLevel` enum (schema-level classification — the vocabulary that `accessLabel` reuses at instance level); `dharma_swarm/adaptive_autonomy.py:RiskLevel` enum (SAFE/LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH/CRITICAL — risk classification for action autonomy; related concept but not persisted; access label complements by providing durable per-instance sensitivity state); `dharma_swarm/telos_gates.py:SENSITIVE_PATH_PATTERNS` and `CREDENTIAL_PATTERNS` (the ad-hoc sensitivity detection the gate runs today — what access labels would make explicit); adapter target: `dharma_swarm/ontology_adapters.py` (new `AccessLabel` ObjectType, standalone — no native instance struct exists; `SecurityLevel` enum can be reused as the classification field value per Pass B §8).
- Bus: No dedicated signal. Propagation semantics (RESTRICTED label on proposal propagates to gateDecision, executionLease, outcome) require a label-propagation hook in `telic_seam.py` at each write step.
- Vision: `docs/COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md` (ISO 27001 A.8.2 data classification — cited as requiring "Data sensitivity checked before mutation" with AHIMSA as the control; access labels would provide the explicit classification AHIMSA currently infers); `docs/governance/OPERATIONAL_DOCTRINE.md` (the organism's immune function requires knowing what it is protecting — access labels are the typed declaration of what is sensitive)
- Activity: **Experimental** — no native instance struct; `SecurityLevel` vocabulary exists. Status: EXPERIMENTAL. Wiring path: new `AccessLabel` OntologyObj in `ontology_adapters.py`; propagation hooks in `telic_seam.py`.

**Themes.** The `accessLabel` is a **dharma** object at the data layer: it encodes the principle that sensitivity is not inferred from patterns but declared by the system's governance structure. It carries the **witness** dimension because a label that travels with data makes the sensitivity of every object visible to any auditor examining the witness chain, without requiring them to re-run the pattern-matching logic the gate used.

### `gateDefinition`

**What it is in the life of this system.** The eleven telos gates have names — AHIMSA, SATYA, CONSENT, VYAVASTHIT, REVERSIBILITY, SVABHAAVA, BHED_GNAN, WITNESS, ANEKANTA, DOGMA_DRIFT, STEELMAN — and tiers (A, B, C) and trigger patterns (HARM_WORDS, INJECTION_PATTERNS, DECEPTION_PATTERNS, SENSITIVE_PATH_PATTERNS, CREDENTIAL_PATTERNS). None of this is a typed ontology object. The definitions live in `dharma_swarm/telos_gates.py` as a class-level dict (`CORE_GATES: dict[str, GateTier]`) and hardcoded keyword sets on `TelosGatekeeper`. The `GateProposal` dataclass (also in `telos_gates.py`) exists for *proposed custom gates* only: it carries `name`, `tier`, `justification`, `trigger_patterns`, `proposed_by`, `status`, `review_note`. Core gates have no equivalent struct — they exist only as implicit code patterns, not as queryable artifacts. A `gateDefinition` is the typed, versioned declaration of what a gate checks — its name, its tier, its trigger patterns, its compliance mappings (AHIMSA ↔ NIST AI RMF GV-1.1, SATYA ↔ SOC 2 CC3.2, etc.), its current-version identifier, and its historical pass rate. It is the policy object that produces `gateDecision` verdicts at runtime, given an ontology-canonical home.

OPA's decision log schema proves why version pinning matters: `bundles[].revision` carries the exact policy version that produced each decision — enabling historical replay of any past decision against the rules that were actually in effect ([OPA decision logs](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/management-decision-logs/)). Without a `gateDefinition` type, if AHIMSA's trigger patterns change between one proposal evaluation and the next, neither past `gateDecision` records nor future auditors can determine which version of AHIMSA evaluated which proposal. Foundry's checkpoint configuration vs. checkpoint record architecture confirms the design principle: the configuration (definition) is authored by governance officers and persists as org-level policy; the record is authored by the system per interaction and persists as an audit artifact; they are separate types with separate lifecycle owners ([Foundry Checkpoints overview](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/checkpoints/overview)). The `gateDefinition` type creates that separation in dharma_swarm — allowing `gateDecision` to reference a specific `gateDefinition` version rather than implying "whatever AHIMSA is at runtime."

**Boundaries.** A `gateDefinition` is not a `gateDecision` — the definition is the authored policy; the decision is the runtime verdict. Many decisions reference one definition version. A `gateDefinition` is not a `GateProposal` — the proposal is the governed change-management object for proposed gate additions or modifications; the definition is the active, ratified declaration of what a gate currently is. A `gateDefinition` is not an `actionDefinition` — the action definition governs what a mutation does; the gate definition governs what the gate that evaluates the mutation checks. They are the two definition-layer siblings on opposite sides of the evaluation event.

**Bindings.**
- Code: `dharma_swarm/telos_gates.py:TelosGatekeeper.CORE_GATES` (the `dict[str, GateTier]` that is the current definition surface); `dharma_swarm/telos_gates.py:GateProposal` (the custom-gate dataclass — the schema that core gate definitions should match); `dharma_swarm/telos_gates.py` keyword/pattern sets (`HARM_WORDS`, `INJECTION_PATTERNS`, `DECEPTION_PATTERNS`, `SENSITIVE_PATH_PATTERNS`, `CREDENTIAL_PATTERNS`) — these become the `trigger_patterns` field of each gateDefinition instance; adapter target: `dharma_swarm/ontology_adapters.py` (new `GateDefinition` OntologyObj seeded at boot from CORE_GATES; version field added; compliance mappings from `COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md`)
- Bus: No dedicated signal today. Gate definition changes would publish a `SIGNAL_GATE_DEFINITION_UPDATED` event to notify downstream subscribers.
- Vision: `docs/COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md` (maps 11 gates to NIST AI RMF, ISO 27001, SOC 2, EU AI Act — these mappings become the `compliance_mappings` field on each gateDefinition instance); `docs/governance/CI_GATES.md` (the Fourfold Shakti Warrant is a CI-layer gate definition — its warrant type could be registered as a `gateDefinition` variant); `docs/governance/OPERATIONAL_DOCTRINE.md` (11 dharmic safety checkpoints — the doctrine-level declaration that gateDefinition objects make machine-readable)
- Activity: **Experimental** — definitions live only in code; no OntologyObj instances exist. Status: EXPERIMENTAL. Wiring path: `create_dharma_registry()` in `ontology.py` seeds one `gateDefinition` instance per core gate at boot.

**Themes.** The `gateDefinition` is the **dharma** object at the constitutional layer: it is where the rules the immune function enforces are themselves governed, versioned, and auditable rather than merely hardcoded. It carries **telos** explicitly — each definition's tier and trigger patterns encode the specific purposeful constraint the system imposes on its own behavior. It touches **witness** because only with versioned definitions can the WitnessAuditor reconstruct the exact governance context that produced any historical verdict.

### `obligation` *(EXPERIMENTAL)*

**What it is in the life of this system.** Some governance moments require not just a verdict but a promise: the actor is permitted to proceed, but only if they fulfill a specific duty as a condition of that permission. W3C ODRL names this concept precisely: a `Duty` is an obligation to exercise an action, where fulfillment is a pre-condition of the Permission firing ([W3C ODRL Information Model 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/)). The WITNESS gate already implies this pattern — it requires the actor to provide a reflection before proceeding (think-point logging is the duty; ALLOW is the permission). The `[impact-checked]` mechanism in CI_GATES.md is another: the PR author must write the string before the gate permits merge. An `obligation` type is the typed representation of that duty: the specific action a requesting actor must perform — provide a justification, notify a data owner, route to a human reviewer — as a condition of a REVIEW verdict resolving to ALLOW. It is the bridge between the REVIEW verdict (which currently produces no durable artifact) and the `justification` type (which produces the fulfillment artifact). Together, `gateDecision` (REVIEW verdict) + `obligation` (what must be done) + `justification` (proof it was done) form a complete governance event loop.

This type is declared EXPERIMENTAL because no native struct exists today and the REVIEW path currently produces no enforcement machinery — REVIEW is advisory and its resolution leaves no durable trace (Pass B §3c). The ODRL evidence base is strong and the architectural gap is real. Whether this earns ACTIVE status depends on whether the REVIEW path is given fulfillment enforcement in Phase 2 — if it is, `obligation` is the type that makes that enforcement auditable.

**Boundaries.** An `obligation` is not a `justification` — the obligation is the duty prescribed by the system; the justification is the actor's fulfillment of that duty. An `obligation` is not a `gateDecision` — the decision is the verdict; the obligation is a condition attached to a REVIEW verdict that must be satisfied for it to resolve. An `obligation` is not a `gateDefinition` rule — gate rules are definition-time declarations; obligations are runtime artifacts generated per decision instance.

**Bindings.**
- Code: No native struct. Closest: `dharma_swarm/models.py:GateDecision.REVIEW` (the verdict state that implies an obligation without formalizing it); `dharma_swarm/telos_gates.py:TelosGatekeeper._log_witness()` (the think-point logging function that implies fulfillment of the WITNESS duty without producing a typed duty object). Adapter target: standalone new type in `dharma_swarm/ontology_adapters.py`.
- Bus: No dedicated signal.
- Vision: `docs/governance/OPERATIONAL_DOCTRINE.md` (REVIEW verdicts imply human-in-the-loop — obligations formalize what the loop requires); `docs/COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md` (EU AI Act Art. 14 human oversight requirement — the obligation type is the artifact that makes human oversight traceable)
- Activity: **Experimental** — no native struct; ODRL grounding is strong; no code wiring today. Status: EXPERIMENTAL.

**Themes.** The `obligation` is the most explicitly **telos**-aligned of the governance siblings: it is the system's declaration that permission is not unconditional, that every ALLOW that passes through a REVIEW gate comes with a duty attached. It also carries the **dharma** dimension most literally — obligation is duty, and duty is the Sanskrit root of dharma itself.

### `zeitgeistSignal`

### `corpusClaim`

# Section 4 — What We Added Beyond Devin's 21, and Why

**stigmergyMark.** The most glaring omission from the original 21. Eight core modules write to and read from the stigmergy lattice. A dedicated API router exists. A GraphQL schema entry exists. This is not a peripheral concept — it is the system's primary mechanism for indirect coordination, the pheromone trail that makes multi-agent awareness possible without a central controller. But that is precisely the point: stigmergy is cross-domain, which is why it is the coordination primitive. It belongs at Layer 2.

**agentCard and agentSkill.** The A2A 1.0 spec is live. Agents are registering. The NATS bus carries card-registration events. The vision says "everything flows through the ontology." If the system's inter-agent capability advertisement layer has no ontology representation, then the claim is false: inter-agent coordination flows *around* the ontology, not through it. The agent card and skill types close that gap. They also give the system a first-class mechanism for capability discovery — for routing decisions to say "find me an agent that has declared skill X" rather than "find me an agent in role Y" (the current, coarser approach).

**routingDecision.** The spine track made this canonical in the final days of the window. Seven implicit routers are being consolidated into one blessed invocation path through `invoke_agent()`. The `RoutingDecision` frozen dataclass records why a routing choice was made, not just what provider was called. Without this type at Layer 2, routing is a black box: the system knows what happened (the `evidenceReceipt`) but not why the choice was made. With `routingDecision` at Layer 2, routing is auditable, and future agents can query why certain providers were preferred in certain contexts to improve their own routing heuristics.

**opportunityCandidate.** The ShaktiExecutive's output has no ontology presence despite being the system's primary attention-direction mechanism. The opportunity board is read by the curriculum engine and is the bridge between the perception layer (what is happening) and the work layer (what to do). Making `opportunityCandidate` an ontology type makes the organism's attention-direction decisions queryable, auditable, and linkable — so an agent can ask "what opportunity led to this proposal?" and trace the causal chain all the way back.

**evolutionProposal.** Devin's registry had `EvolutionEntry` as an aspirational type that was never bridged to the native `Proposal` class in `evolution.py`. Rather than keep the orphaned name, this census renames it `evolutionProposal` (parallel to `proposal`, making the distinction explicit) and declares it `EXPERIMENTAL` pending the adapter. The rename is not cosmetic: the difference between "entry" (a log record) and "proposal" (an active agent proposal for self-modification) is exactly the difference between an archive artifact and a living type.

**identitySnapshot, zeitgeistSignal, corpusClaim.** These three were in `ontology_adapters.py` already — defined, wired to adjacent concepts, but not in the 21. They represent the system's self-recognition loop (`identitySnapshot`), its sensory surface (`zeitgeistSignal`), and its epistemic accountability (`corpusClaim`). All three are declared `EXPERIMENTAL` with explicit production paths. Their presence in the vocabulary is a commitment: these concepts are what the organism needs to be able to reason about to close the attractor closure gap. Naming them at Layer 2 is the first step toward wiring them.

**Governance revision additions (revision 2, governance Pass C).** The initial 22 types were assembled without a focused governance pass. The cron's PR #415 grounding changed that: two parallel passes (external research + code walk) ran before merge, and six governance-sibling types were added as a result. Each fills a specific gap the passes named with code evidence.

`actionDefinition` contributes what the original 22 missed entirely: the definition-time layer of the gate system. Every `proposal` is an instance of an action class; every action class has a declaration in `ActionDef` at `ontology.py`; but that declaration was invisible to the ontology. Making `ActionDef` a first-class ontology object makes the governed definition of every permitted mutation queryable — including the `requires_approval` flag that the original code sets but never enforces.

`actorContext` contributes the fourth node in the governance chain that Palantir's PBAC model identifies as essential: actor identity + declared purpose + role context + authorization scope, bound together at evaluation time. The original 22 had a gate, a decision, and a lease — but no typed record of who was asking and why. `actorContext` names the artifact that was always implied but never produced.

`justification` contributes the actor-generated acknowledgment artifact that Foundry Checkpoints center their architecture around. The `gateDecision` records what the gate said. The `justification` records what the actor said in response. These are produced by different parties — one by the system, one by the requesting entity — and serve different audit purposes. The original 22 had only the system's voice.

`accessLabel` contributes per-instance sensitivity classification — the distinction between knowing that a type is RESTRICTED (schema-level, what `SecurityLevel` provides) and knowing that *this specific object* is RESTRICTED (instance-level, what `accessLabel` provides). The original 22 had the schema; the instance layer was absent.

`gateDefinition` contributes the versioned policy object that produces gate decisions. Without it, every historical `gateDecision` record is ambiguous: it records the verdict but not which version of AHIMSA or SATYA evaluated the proposal. OPA decision logs and Foundry checkpoint configurations both demonstrate that the definition and the record must be separate typed objects. The original 22 had the record; the definition was substrate.

`obligation` contributes the ODRL-grounded pre-condition concept — the duty that must be fulfilled before a REVIEW verdict resolves to ALLOW. It is the bridge between the REVIEW state (currently advisory-only with no durable trace) and the `justification` type that proves the duty was discharged. EXPERIMENTAL at this stage; included because the architectural gap is real and the evidence base from W3C ODRL is unambiguous.

# Section 5 — What We Renamed and Why

**ActionProposal → `proposal`.** "ActionProposal" is redundant: in this system, all proposals are proposals for action. The word "Action" adds only length. The risk of collision with the Darwin Engine's `Proposal` class in `evolution.py` is addressed by naming the Darwin Engine concept `evolutionProposal` — the distinction is now between `proposal` (operational metabolic loop entry) and `evolutionProposal` (self-modification entry). Palantir-canonical naming drops unnecessary qualifiers: `employee` not `humanResourceEmployee`; `proposal` not `actionProposal`. The shorter name also aligns with the OSDK developer experience: a developer reading a list of object types in the Ontology Manager sees `proposal` and immediately understands its scope. The longer name requires knowing what "action" means in this system's vocabulary before the name is legible.

**GateDecisionRecord → `gateDecision`.** "Record" is a data-structure name, not a business concept name. The Palantir community guide is explicit: "Object Types and Actions should map to natural-language business concepts. The Ontology is built to support operational decision-making." What this object *is* in the life of the system is a constitutional verdict — the exercise of the system's dharmic immune function on a specific proposal. A gate decides; the `gateDecision` is that decision. Dropping "Record" removes the implementation-smell from the name. An OSDK developer reads `gateDecision` and understands: this is the thing a gate produces when it evaluates a proposal. The "Record" suffix made it sound like a log; the shorter form makes it sound like an agent's output.

**EvolutionEntry → `evolutionProposal`.** "Entry" suggests an archive log record — something passive that accumulates. "Proposal" is active — an agent is proposing a specific code mutation, with a specific evidence basis, for a specific purpose. The Darwin Engine's native class in `evolution.py` is called `Proposal`, not `Entry`. Aligning the ontology type name with the native class name is the simplest adapter-writing discipline: when you read the adapter code, the mapping `evolution.Proposal → evolutionProposal` is immediately obvious. `EvolutionEntry` required the adapter author to understand that "entry" and "proposal" were the same thing — an unnecessary cognitive load that `evolutionProposal` eliminates.

**StigmergicMark → `stigmergyMark`.** The code class is `StigmergicMark` (adjective form). The business concept name is more natural as a noun compound: a "mark of stigmergy" is a `stigmergyMark`. This follows the Palantir pattern of naming object types after business nouns rather than after their adjectival code class names. An OSDK developer reads `stigmergyMark` and reaches for the concept immediately: a mark left in the stigmergy lattice. The full adjectival form adds no information while adding syllables.

**RevenueTarget → preserved.** This name earns its keep. The native class in `spine_models.py` is also called `RevenueTarget`, the domain (revenue) is correctly specified, and "Target" is the right business noun for a potential buyer identified by scouting. The `Revenue` prefix here is not redundant — it distinguishes this from a general target (the system uses "target" in other contexts). Preserve as-is.

# Section 6 — Open Tensions for John's Discernment

**1. Which bus is the system's primary coordination medium, and does that change the vocabulary floor?**

The swarm found two distinct buses: the in-process `SignalBus` (synchronous, raw dicts, loop-to-loop heartbeat) and the NATS inter-process bus (async, typed envelopes, fleet coordination). This vocabulary names typed A2A coordination messages as Layer 2 objects but explicitly treats the `SignalBus` as infrastructure — real, load-bearing, but below the vocabulary floor. The question is whether John agrees with that cut. The `SignalBus` carries thirteen named signals that carry real semantic content: `SIGNAL_RECOGNITION_UPDATED`, `SIGNAL_REPLICATION_PROPOSAL`, `SIGNAL_AGENT_APOPTOSIS`. If agents should be able to query "what recognition events occurred in the last 24 hours?" through the ontology, then `SignalBus` events need typed ontology representations. If they are purely infrastructure — readable only by the runtime, not by agents querying the ontology — then the current cut holds. The direction John chooses here determines whether the vocabulary grows by thirteen more types or stays where it is. What is the intended architecture for the boundary between infrastructure signaling and vocabulary-layer coordination?

**2. Is `knowledgeArtifact` one type or an Interface over many?**

`knowledgeArtifact` currently spans ten subtypes: file, note, research finding, measurement, citation, code, model output, prompt, result, visualization. The Palantir design guide says a union type masquerading as an object is bad design — it should be an Interface shared by multiple concrete types with specific properties for each. The correct long-term architecture is probably `KnowledgeArtifact` as an Interface, with concrete types like `codeArtifact`, `researchNote`, `evidenceArtifact` that share the confidence and verified fields but differ in their content structure. The question is whether to make that split now — which multiplies registry complexity by six immediately — or to preserve the single broad type and plan a future migration. John's feel for how operators actually use knowledge artifacts in practice is the deciding voice. If operators search by domain and artifact_type together as a discriminator, the current single type is fine. If they need to query "show me all code artifacts" as a structurally distinct concept, the Interface split needs to happen.

**3. Where does the gate itself live — as a queryable object or as system configuration?**

The five telos gates (AHIMSA, SATYA, REVERSIBILITY, SVABHAAVA, WITNESS) are the constitutional backbone. Each `gateDecision` records what the gate suite found for one proposal. But the gate *definition* itself — its tier, its trigger patterns, its historical pass rate, its justification — has no ontology representation. The `GateProposal` type in `telos_gates.py` treats new gate proposals as structured objects, which implies that existing gates should be objects too. Should a `telosGate` type be added to Layer 2, making gates queryable as ontology objects? This would allow agents to ask "which gate is blocking proposals most frequently this week?" and "what is the historical pass rate for AHIMSA on code-writing proposals?" — questions that currently require code-level inspection. But it also means gate definitions enter the ontology, which has implications for the gate system's governance (who can read gate definitions? can external A2A agents query them?). The alternative — keeping gate definitions as substrate configuration, readable only by the gate engine — is simpler but closes the door on self-diagnostic routing. What is John's intended architecture for the gate system as an object or as configuration?

**4. What is the canonical unit of agent memory, and does naming it here force a choice?**

Four parallel memory implementations (`StrangeLoopMemory`, `AgentMemoryBank`, `AgentMemoryManager`, `MemoryPalace`) plus the contracts-layer `MemoryPlane` protocol coexist in the codebase without a declared canonical form. Pass 1d and Pass 2 both identify this as a gap. This vocabulary census did not name a `memoryRecord` type precisely because naming it without knowing which implementation is canonical risks creating a fifth parallel definition — one at the ontology layer that none of the four native implementations bridges to. But the absence also has a cost: agents cannot reason through the ontology about the system's own memory state, which means the self-recognition loop cannot close through the typed object system. Does John have a view on which memory implementation is the intended architecture, or is the answer "they merge into one during the spine-adoption track"? If the latter, the vocabulary should hold a placeholder at `EXPERIMENTAL` status now, pointing at the consolidation target. If the former, the answer determines which implementation the adapter should bridge from.

**5. How does this vocabulary land with the OSDK developer who has no dharmic context?**

The names in this census are plain English and Palantir-canonical: `proposal`, `gateDecision`, `executionLease`, `stigmergyMark`, `agentCard`. An experienced OSDK developer can infer their meaning from the name alone. But the narratives that accompany these names are doctrine-saturated: they speak of telos gates as the dharmic immune function, of stigmergy as the pheromone-trail coordination mechanism, of contributions as the atomic unit of jagat kalyan at the micro scale. The question is whether the doctrine-saturated narrative is part of the public-facing type documentation — shown to every developer querying the API — or whether it is internal annotation, visible only to agents and operators who have context. This is not a naming question; it is a governance question about how the system presents itself to the world through its type documentation. The vocabulary census has no voice on this. It belongs entirely to John's discernment about what dharma_swarm declares itself to be in its external-facing artifacts.

**6. Is the governance subsection of this vocabulary deep enough? (Added post-PR after PR #415 cron grounding.)**

Fifteen minutes after this census was pushed, the hourly cron landed PR #415 — an independent adversarial grounding of PR #406's telos-gate hardwire against Palantir Foundry's actual governance machinery. The cron found, with primary-source citations, that Foundry **Checkpoints** require a richer verdict state space than ALLOW/BLOCK/REVIEW: justifications and acknowledgments are first-class durable artifacts attached to sensitive actions, not just verdict labels ([Palantir Foundry docs — Data protection and governance](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/security/data-protection-and-governance/)). It also found that Foundry governance is fundamentally tied to actor identity, purpose, role context, and data classifications — none of which appear in the current `gateDecision` envelope or in any sibling type. Three concepts are missing from the 22 by implication: a `policyBinding` (actor + purpose + role context bound to an action), a `securityMarking` (Foundry's continuous-control-plane primitive that changes access state after the fact), and an `actionDefinition` (the governed/versioned declaration of what an action IS, distinct from the runtime `proposal`). The cron also surfaces a deeper axis the census did not name: governed **definitions** that propagate through the system are a different lifecycle from runtime **instances**, and Layer 2 holds both. The metabolic-loop six and the consensus additions still stand. But the governance subsection of this vocabulary is shallower than a Palantir-grade ontology would have it. Three options: (a) merge as-is and file a follow-up for governance-vocabulary expansion in Phase-2, (b) re-run a focused 30min governance pass before merge that adds the missing types directly to this branch, or (c) acknowledge the gap as this 6th tension and let Phase-2 work resolve it with full context. My lean is (a) — the 22 are honest about what they are, the gap is now visible, and Phase-2 work has a target. But the call is John's. Full cross-reference and primary-source quotes are in [PR #415](https://github.com/AmitabhainArunachala/dharma_swarm/pull/415) and the [comment thread on this PR](https://github.com/AmitabhainArunachala/dharma_swarm/pull/414#issuecomment-4589771442).

**Resolution:** Option (b) was chosen at 13:28 WITA. Governance Pass A (external research across Foundry Checkpoints, Foundry Markings, Foundry Action Types, Foundry PBAC, OPA Decision Logs, NIST AI RMF, W3C ODRL, and dbt Semantic Layer — 12 primary sources reached of 20 attempted) and Governance Pass B (code walk across 12 core Python files and 4 governance docs, with grep sweeps across all of `dharma_swarm/`) ran in parallel, followed by this synthesis (Pass C). The three original names from the cron grounding (`policyBinding`, `securityMarking`, `actionDefinition`) were evaluated against code reality: `policyBinding` was replaced by the more precisely grounded `actorContext` (the actor-identity-and-purpose binding that the gate path actually needs, as distinct from a static policy record); `securityMarking` was renamed `accessLabel` to reflect that this is a per-instance sensitivity label, not a Foundry-native marking (the vocabulary reuses the existing `SecurityLevel` enum vocabulary at the instance layer rather than importing Foundry's terminology). A further three types were added beyond the cron's original three: `justification` (the actor-generated acknowledgment artifact that the WITNESS gate implies but does not produce), `gateDefinition` (the versioned policy object that the OPA decision-log architecture proves must be separate from the runtime verdict), and `obligation` (the ODRL-grounded pre-condition duty that formalizes what REVIEW verdicts currently leave untracked). The six new types are in Section 2 above, under the governance siblings heading. The verdict space on `gateDecision` was deliberately left unchanged at ALLOW/BLOCK/REVIEW — the evidence did not support expansion, and the Foundry checkpoint record architecture shows that the justification artifact (not an expanded verdict) is the right way to model actor acknowledgment. The Foundry Checkpoint architectural-strictness note (Foundry checkpoints do not apply to API/OSDK actions, only to UI-initiated actions — making dharma_swarm's gate architecturally stricter, since it gates all execution paths uniformly) is stated in the `gateDefinition` narrative. The ODRL obligation gap is explicitly addressed in the `obligation` entry.
