## 2. How Parallel Institutions Actually Win — The Evidence Base

Dual power "done right" is not Lenin's transitional confrontation. The parallel institutions that won share five mechanics; the ones that died violate at least one. (Historical record; canonical sources named.)

### 2.1 The Dáil Courts (Ireland, 1919–1922) — the same-law principle
The First Dáil's parish and district courts spread through most counties by 1920 not because litigants converted to republicanism, but because: (a) the British petty-sessions system was collapsing (magistrates resigning, RIC withdrawing); (b) the Dáil courts were *faster, cheaper, locally enforced* (IRA police executed decrees); and (c) crucially, they applied **substantially the existing law** — common law and equity litigants and even solicitors already understood. Unionist farmers used them for land disputes. When the Free State formed, the parallel courts were absorbed into the new judiciary. (Source: Mary Kotsonouris, *Retreat from Revolution: The Dáil Courts, 1920–24*.)
**Lesson**: A parallel lane wins by service quality and *continuity of substance*, not novelty of doctrine. Run on the host's own recognized legal materials so the switching cost is near zero — and so absorption, when it comes, happens on your terms.

### 2.2 Solidarity / the self-limiting revolution (Poland, 1976–1989)
Michnik's "New Evolutionism" (1976) argued for building society rather than seizing the state. KOR's mutual aid to workers' families seeded it; Solidarity reached ~10M members; after martial law (1981) the movement persisted as an underground society — independent presses, flying universities, parallel culture — that maintained *society's capacity to coordinate* while the state hollowed. When the state needed a legitimate negotiating partner (1989 Round Table), only the parallel institution was standing. (Term "self-limiting revolution": Jadwiga Staniszkis.)
**Lesson**: Self-limitation is not weakness — it denies the host's immune system a target. Write the non-goals into the constitution: no territorial claim, no rival currency, no citizenship-of-the-world enrollment drive.

### 2.3 M-Pesa (Kenya, 2007–) — attach to existing flows, make the regulator a beneficiary
M-Pesa did not fight banks. It rode infrastructure everyone already had (SMS), solved a flow that already had to happen ("send money home"), and the Central Bank of Kenya under Njuguna Ndung'u chose to observe with a letter of no objection rather than ban. Within a decade transaction volumes commonly cited near half of Kenyan GDP flowed through it; the banking system integrated *with it*.
**Lesson**: Never require behavior change first. Attach receipts to flows that already must exist — compliance logs, MRV, grant reporting — and design so regulators gain visibility rather than lose control.

### 2.4 COVID mutual aid (2020–21) — dormancy economics
Thousands of hyperlocal groups (4,000+ registered in the UK alone) activated in weeks on WhatsApp and spreadsheets; most dissolved within 18 months. What persisted was not organizations but *relationships and playbooks*, and the durable nodes were anchored to pre-existing institutions (churches, unions, councils).
**Lesson**: Crisis-activation is a latent-network property. Design for dormancy: nearly free to keep alive, fast to activate, anchored to institutions that exist for other reasons. The fediverse proves the cost floor — a governance node must be Raspberry-Pi cheap or the lane is theater (https://fediview.com/articles/fediverse-in-numbers-mastodon-stats-2026/).

### 2.5 Amateur radio ARES/RACES — the auxiliary charter
The one governance form that is *explicitly crisis-activatable and beloved by states*: amateur radio emergency services. RACES exists inside FCC regulation (47 CFR Part 97.407); ARES drills in public through boring service events; both activate under declared emergencies *in service of* civil authority, then stand down. Nobody calls ham radio operators secessionists.
**Lesson**: The path to being load-bearing in crisis without reading as a threat is the **auxiliary charter**: formal MOUs with municipalities and NGO federations, public annual drills, activation only under declared conditions, automatic stand-down. The US interstate EMAC compact (P.L. 104-321, 1996) and the EU Civil Protection Mechanism are the legal genre to imitate.

## 3. Architecture (Mechanisms)

### 3.1 The two-lane protocol and witnessed substitution events
The parallel lane is not a separate stack. It is **one artifact in two modes**:
- **Lane 1 — Interop mode (default, ~99% of life)**: CARs attach to existing institutional flows as their evidence layer. A restoration project's MRV, a municipality's grant disbursement, an agent's delegated purchase — each emits a receipt into a transparency log. The institution remains the system of record; the Commons is the system of *proof*.
- **Lane 2 — Load-bearing mode (declared, bounded, reversible)**: when a primary system fails (registry offline, institution collapsed, jurisdiction contested), the receipt graph *is already* a complete, independently witnessed record of who may act, on whose authority, with what obligations — so coordination continues on the lane itself.

The switch is a **substitution event**: itself a signed, witnessed receipt, declared against pre-agreed conditions in the auxiliary charter, with an automatic reversion review. Design ancestor: the Roman dictatorship's six-month term (constitutional emergency power that expires by default); anti-pattern: Weimar Article 48 (emergency power without effective sunset). Every activation carries its own stand-down clock. This single mechanism answers the operator's "if/when traditional structures give way" without a single sentence of collapse-porn: the lane's crisis behavior is a *chartered, receipted, sunset-limited public service*, indistinguishable in genre from a backup generator test.

**Readiness is measured, not asserted**: annual load-bearing drills (one municipality or NGO federation runs one real process on the lane for one week per year), each drill proven by its own receipts, aggregated into a public per-node **substitution-readiness index**. The Commons audits its own crisis-activatability with the same primitive it sells. No prior parallel institution could prove its readiness; this one can, natively.
