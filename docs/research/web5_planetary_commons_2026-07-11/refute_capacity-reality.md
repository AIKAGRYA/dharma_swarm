### V2. "Incumbents structurally cannot own witnessed challenge-and-reversal; only a commons can — so a solo operator can beat them to the constitutional ground floor"
**Claim** (arch_constitutional.md:244-246 boldest claim: "a Visa-owned dispute layer adjudicating Visa's agents is Verra adjudicating Verra's credits"; arch_dharmic.md:41: "A payments network cannot make reversal ordinary, because its economics run on settlement finality"; echoed in arch_protocol.md:161 "the dispute organ nobody in the payment-protocol war owns").

**Attack.** The card networks have run **reversal-as-ordinary at planetary scale for ~50 years**. The chargeback/dispute system processed an estimated **261 million disputes worth ~$33.8B in 2025**, projected to 324-359M disputes by 2028-29 — per Mastercard's own State of Chargebacks research with Datos Insights (https://www.chargebackgurus.com/blog/chargeback-stats-and-insights-from-mastercards-state-of-chargebacks-report; https://www.chargeback.io/blog/chargeback-statistics; https://www.mastercard.com/global/en/news-and-trends/Insights/2025/what-s-the-true-cost-of-a-chargeback-in-2025.html). Every element the essays claim incumbents "structurally cannot" supply is present: independent-ish adjudication (issuer vs. acquirer, network rules as arbiter), bounded windows, fee schedules that do **not** scale with approvals (dispute fees are charged regardless of outcome — the anti-Verra invariant already exists at Visa), and consumer-side reversal as a *routine right*. The Verra analogy fails on its face: Verra's pathology was developer-pays issuance revenue, not actor-adjacency; card networks earn interchange whichever way a dispute resolves. The specific sentence "a payments network cannot make reversal ordinary" is **factually false today**, and it is load-bearing under the "only a commons can own it" monopoly claim and the derived 12-24-month-window confidence. If anything, the window logic inverts: the entities with the deepest demonstrated dispute-layer competence on earth are the same ones (Visa TAP, FIDO/AP2) the essays name as identity/payments winners — extending chargeback mechanics to agent actions is *adjacent* to their core competence, not structurally foreclosed.

**Verdict: REFUTED** (as stated — the structural-impossibility form and the "only a commons" monopoly form).
**Nearby survivor:** the narrower claim survives and is still valuable: incumbents adjudicate disputes *between their users on their own rails*; none submits **its own conduct** to an external, non-backdateable challenge log, and none offers **cross-rail** challenge semantics (a chargeback cannot contest a Visa policy, and doesn't span MCP/A2A/x402). "Self-binding confession + cross-platform challenge grammar" remains an uncontested niche a commons can credibly hold — as a *differentiator to be defended*, not a law of nature that guarantees the ground floor to whoever signs first.
