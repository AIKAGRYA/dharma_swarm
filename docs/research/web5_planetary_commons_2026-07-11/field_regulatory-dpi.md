## 0. Executive summary

The mid-2026 regulatory landscape is, with almost eerie precision, manufacturing demand for the atomic unit of the operator's design — the causal action receipt — while leaving the *cross-domain schema that binds its stages* entirely unowned. Four regulatory instruments each mandate ONE fragment of the receipt: EU AI Act Article 12 mandates tamper-relevant automatic **logging** (now landing Dec 2027 for most high-risk systems after the June 2026 omnibus deferral); AI Act Article 50 mandates machine-readable **provenance marking** of AI content from 2026-08-02; eIDAS 2.0 creates **qualified electronic ledgers** — a legal trust service granting records a *presumption of integrity and accurate chronological ordering*; and the revised Product Liability Directive (transposition due 2026-12-09) makes software/AI **strictly liable** with evidence-disclosure duties, making exculpatory evidence trails commercially existential. Meanwhile the agentic protocol stack (MCP, A2A, AP2) solved communication, tool use, and payment mandates — and nothing else. No protocol answers authority, material burden, telos, witnessed outcome, or challenge/reversal.

On the DPI side: the movement is institutionalized (50-in-5 at 30+ countries, Gates $200M, UNDP safeguards framework, WSIS+20 making DPI a permanent UN agenda item), the DPG registration path is cheap and solo-feasible (243 DPGs, free application, "Standard" is an eligible DPG type), and the three canonical precedents (MOSIP, DHIS2, Beckn) prove tiny teams can author national infrastructure — but every one of them had an institutional anchor, $5–30M of patient philanthropy, and one committed first-deployer government. ONDC's 2025 stumble is the cautionary mirror: protocol + state backing + subsidies still lost to platform UX. The strategic consequence for the Commons is developed in §9.

## 1. EU AI Act — the precise mid-2026 state

### 1.1 What actually binds on 2026-08-02

The AI Act's general applicability date remains 2 August 2026, but the June 2026 "Digital Omnibus on AI" carved out the high-risk core (see §1.2). What survives on the original date:

- **Article 50 transparency obligations** — disclosure when humans interact with AI systems; machine-readable marking of synthetic content; deepfake labeling — apply from 2 August 2026 on the original schedule ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)). Penalties for Article 50 violations reach €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover ([C2PA Viewer / EU AI Act analysis](https://c2paviewer.com/articles/eu-ai-act-content-credentials)).
- **GPAI enforcement begins.** GPAI model obligations (technical documentation, copyright policy, training-content summary) have applied since 2 August 2025 ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-gpai-model-obligations-in-force-and-final-gpai-code-of-practice-in-place)); on 2 August 2026 the Commission's enforcement powers enter into application, including Article 101 fines of up to 3% of global annual turnover or €15M, whichever is higher ([AI Act Service Desk, Art. 101](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-101); [MediaLaws](https://www.medialaws.eu/eu-ai-obligations-for-gpai-providers-compliance-enforcement-deadlines-2025-2027/)). GPAI models placed on the market before 2 Aug 2025 have until 2 Aug 2027 to comply (per Commission GPAI guidelines — [digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-gpai-providers)).
- **The GPAI Code of Practice** (final version published 10 July 2025) is the voluntary compliance vehicle; signatories as of spring 2026 include Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Mistral, Anthropic and dozens of smaller EU firms; the AI Office treats signatories as acting in good faith but "will fully enforce the GPAI requirements from 2 August 2026" ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/08/eu-ai-act-european-commission-publishes-generalpurpose-ai-code-of-practice); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-gpai-model-obligations-in-force-and-final-gpai-code-of-practice-in-place)).

### 1.2 The Digital Omnibus — the dates just moved (June 2026)

This is the single most important correction to any pre-May-2026 briefing:

- The Commission proposed the Digital Omnibus on AI on 19 November 2025; a provisional Council–Parliament–Commission agreement was reached on 6–7 May 2026 (sources differ by one day: [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/) says 6 May; [VerifyWise](https://verifywise.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-what-changed) says 7 May).
- **High-risk obligations for stand-alone Annex III systems are deferred from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 December 2027**; Annex I (AI embedded in regulated products) to **2 August 2028** ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act); [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/eu-agrees-to-delay-key-ai-act-compliance-deadlines/)).
- Parliament formally endorsed 16 June 2026; the **Council gave final green light 29 June 2026**; publication in the Official Journal expected before 2 Aug 2026 ([Consilium press release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/) — page returned 403 on direct fetch; content corroborated via [Inside Privacy/Covington](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions/) and [ComplianceHub](https://compliancehub.wiki/eu-digital-omnibus-ai-act-deadline-deferral-annex-iii-2027/)).
- A **grace period for synthetic-content marking**: for generative systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026, the provider marking obligation slips to **2 December 2026** ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Inside Global Tech](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2026/05/28/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions/)).
- New Article 5 prohibitions added (AI-generated NCII/CSAM "nudifiers") ([Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions/)).
- The AI Office gains "exclusive competence" over AI systems built on GPAI models where model and system share a provider, with reinforced investigation/inspection powers ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)).

**Read**: the EU blinked on dates but not direction. The deferral is a 16-month gift of preparation time for anyone building evidence/logging infrastructure — and a warning against building a venture on any single compliance date.

### 1.3 Logging & traceability duties (the receipt-shaped hole in the Act)

Article 12 (Record-keeping) requires high-risk AI systems to **technically allow automatic recording of events (logs) over the system's lifetime** — manual records don't count ([artificialintelligenceact.eu, Art. 12](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/12/); [AI Act Service Desk](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-12)). Logging must enable identification of risk situations, post-market monitoring (Art. 72), and deployer operation-monitoring (Art. 26(5)); it must support **post-hoc reconstruction of individual AI-assisted decisions**, i.e., decision traceability, not mere activity capture ([truescreen.io](https://truescreen.io/insights/ai-act-record-keeping-requirements/); [aigovernancedesk.com](https://aigovernancedesk.com/eu-ai-act-articles-12-13-decision-traceability/)). Logs must be retained at least 6 months (Art. 19 via [practical-ai-act.eu](https://practical-ai-act.eu/latest/conformity/record-keeping/)). Responsibility is split across the value chain: providers design the capability, deployers maintain and use it ([isms.online](https://www.isms.online/iso-42001/eu-ai-act/article-12/)).

**Crucial structural fact**: Article 12 logs are *private, per-provider, non-interoperable artifacts*. The Act mandates that evidence exist; it specifies no shared schema, no cross-provider verification, no third-party witnessing, no challenge mechanism. That is exactly the causal-action-receipt gap.

### 1.4 Agents specifically

There is **no dedicated EU agent law** as of mid-2026. Agents are governed by classification: an agent qualifying as high-risk falls under Chapter III (now mostly Dec 2027); agents interacting with humans or generating content fall under Article 50 from Aug 2026 ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/steptechtoe-blog/ai-agents-navigating-emerging-risks-and-compliance-challenges-under-eu-law.html); [ReedSmith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102mp93/regulators-turn-their-attention-to-agentic-ai/)). The AI Office's 2026 guidance agenda covers high-risk classification, value-chain responsibility, substantial modification, and Art. 50 transparency ([Zylos Research](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-05-01-ai-agent-governance-compliance-2026/)). The most advanced agent-specific governance instrument globally is **Singapore IMDA's Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI (January 2026)** — the first comprehensive framework requiring each agent to carry a **verifiable digital identity and an audit trail of which agent acted under whose authorization** ([Zylos Research](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-05-01-ai-agent-governance-compliance-2026/)). Academic architecture work ("AI Agents Under EU Law", [arXiv:2604.04604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04604)) confirms the accountability-chain problem (orchestrator → sub-agent → API → database) is recognized and unsolved in law.

## 2. AI liability — direction of travel

- The proposed **AI Liability Directive was withdrawn**: the Commission signaled withdrawal in its Feb 2025 work programme and the file was formally withdrawn in 2025 (October 2025 per [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/ai-liability-directive-what-it-was-and-why-it-was-withdrawn/); see also [EP Legislative Train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-a-europe-fit-for-the-digital-age/file-ai-liability-directive) and [regulations.ai](https://regulations.ai/regulations/european-union-2025-2-ai-liability-directive)).
- What remains is a **two-pillar regime**: the AI Act (public-law compliance) + the **revised Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853** — adopted 23 Oct 2024, in force 8 Dec 2024, **national transposition due 9 December 2026** ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/publication-listing/the-new-eu-product-liability-reform-addressing-the-digital-age); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/01/di-new-product-liability-directive)).
- The new PLD **redefines "product" to include software and AI systems** and digital manufacturing files; **strict liability** (no negligence proof needed — only defect, damage, causation) now reaches software itself ([basilpuglisi.com analysis](https://basilpuglisi.com/eu-ai-liability-product-directive/); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/france/ai-liability-in-light-of-the-new-2024-pld-expanded-liability-challenging-defences-and-new-evidentiar)).
- **Evidence-disclosure duty**: where a claimant shows plausibility, the manufacturer must disclose necessary evidence (subject to trade-secret protection), with presumptions of defectiveness for non-disclosure ([Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/france/ai-liability-in-light-of-the-new-2024-pld-expanded-liability-challenging-defences-and-new-evidentiar)).

**Read**: liability law is converging on *evidence as the battleground*. Any deployer of agentic software in the EU after Dec 2026 has a direct commercial interest in non-backdateable records of what its systems did, under whose authority, with what result — because those records are its only defense under strict liability. This is demand for receipts created by the courts, independent of AI Act dates.

## 3. eIDAS 2.0 / EUDI wallet — identity + the ledger surprise

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 (in force 20 May 2024) obliges **all 27 member states to offer at least one EUDI wallet to citizens by December 2026** — the operative deadline widely cited as **24 December 2026**, 24 months after the Nov/Dec 2024 implementing acts ([EC Digital Building Blocks](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/spaces/EUDIGITALIDENTITYWALLET/pages/915931811/The+European+Digital+Identity+Regulation); [Signicat](https://www.signicat.com/blog/eudi-wallets-only-one-year-to-launch); [deepidv](https://www.deepidv.com/media/news/eudi-wallet-deadline-late-2026)).
- **What will be live at launch**: Person Identification Data (PID) everywhere; then a spread of attestations — mobile driving licences, diplomas, professional qualifications — via **Qualified Electronic Attestations of Attributes (QEAA)** from qualified trust service providers and **PubEAAs** from public authorities, per the Architecture and Reference Framework ([ARF, eudi.dev](https://eudi.dev/2.4.0/architecture-and-reference-framework-main/); [walt.id](https://walt.id/eidas2/eudi-wallet)). Readiness varies sharply: France, Italy, Poland lead; Germany targets 2 January 2027; several states will launch PID-only ([Namirial status check](https://www.namirial.com/en/blog/stories/status-check-eudi-wallet/)).
- **Mandatory acceptance** by regulated relying parties (banks, large platforms) follows ~one year after rollout, i.e., late 2027 ([Zyphe](https://www.zyphe.com/resources/blog/eidas-2-eu-digital-identity-wallet-kyc-compliance-guide)).
- **The under-reported prize — electronic ledgers as a trust service.** eIDAS 2.0 introduces "recording of electronic data in an electronic ledger" as a NEW trust service: services offering "sequential chronological ordering of data records, guaranteeing the integrity and accuracy of both the records themselves and their chronological order," deliberately technology-neutral (centralized or distributed) ([EC Q&A on Trust Services](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/questions-answers-trust-services-under-european-digital-identity-regulation)). **Data records in a QUALIFIED electronic ledger enjoy a legal presumption of their unique and accurate sequential chronological ordering and of their integrity** ([regulation text via european-digital-identity-regulation.com](https://www.european-digital-identity-regulation.com/Article_45_(Regulation_EU_2024_1183).html); article numbering ~45k–45l, UNVERIFIED against EUR-Lex directly). Qualified ledgers must be run by qualified trust service providers, establish record origin, and make any subsequent change immediately detectable. eIDAS 2.0 also adds **qualified electronic archiving** (receipt, storage, retrieval of documents preserving integrity over retention periods) ([EC Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/questions-answers-trust-services-under-european-digital-identity-regulation)).

**Read**: the EU has already built the *legal container* for a receipt spine — a court-recognized presumption of integrity and chronology for ledger records — without specifying what goes in it. A causal action receipt anchored in a qualified electronic ledger would carry statutory evidentiary weight across all 27 member states. This is the single strongest legal hook found in this entire survey.

## 4. C2PA and content provenance in regulation

- C2PA Content Credentials — cryptographically signed, tamper-evident provenance manifests — is the de facto standard; current spec family is **v2.4** ([spec.c2pa.org](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html)), with broad adoption (Adobe, Microsoft, Google, OpenAI, BBC, AP) ([eyesift adoption status](https://www.eyesift.com/faq/c2pa-content-credentials-2026-cryptographic-provenance-adoption/)).
- **The AI Act does not name C2PA** in operative text — Article 50(2) requires marking "in a machine-readable format... effective, interoperable, robust and reliable" — but the **Commission's draft Code of Practice on Transparency names C2PA Content Credentials by example** and prescribes a multilayer approach (visible disclosure + signed metadata + invisible watermarking + fingerprinting) ([c2pa.ai compliance guide](https://c2pa.ai/eu-ai-act-compliance); [Pebblous analysis](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/eu-ai-content-labeling-article-50-provenance/en/)).
- C2PA is being fast-tracked through **ISO/IEC JTC 1**; one source asserts it is "formally standardized as ISO/IEC 21694" ([C2PA Viewer](https://c2paviewer.com/articles/eu-ai-act-content-credentials)) — **UNVERIFIED**: a second search could not confirm a finalized ISO number as of July 2026; treat "ISO standardization in progress" as the safe claim ([eyesift](https://www.eyesift.com/faq/c2pa-content-credentials-2026-cryptographic-provenance-adoption/)).
- Timing: Article 50 marking binds 2 Aug 2026; systems placed before that date get until 2 Dec 2026 (omnibus grace, §1.2).

**Read**: C2PA proves the template the Commons needs — an industry coalition's open spec becoming the referenced compliance mechanism for a functional legal requirement. But C2PA only answers "where did this MEDIA come from." Nobody has done the same for ACTIONS.

## 5. UN layer — Global Digital Compact follow-through

The GDC (adopted September 2024 in the Pact for the Future) has produced real machinery, faster than most UN processes:

- **Independent International Scientific Panel on AI**: established by UNGA resolution A/RES/79/325 on 26 Aug 2025; 40 members appointed 12 Feb 2026; **Yoshua Bengio and Maria Ressa elected co-chairs** 3 Mar 2026; >2,600 applications from 140+ countries ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_International_Scientific_Panel_on_AI); [UN FAQ](https://www.un.org/independent-international-scientific-panel-ai/en/faq)). Its **preliminary report launched 1 July 2026**, warning AI is "outpacing both scientific understanding and governments' ability to adapt" ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319647/20260703/un-ai-governance-summit-opens-monday-science-panel-says-control-not-guaranteed.htm)); full report due May 2027 ([boesl.org analysis](https://boesl.org/en/un-independent-scientific-panel-ai-preliminary-report/)).
- **Global Dialogue on AI Governance**: launched 25 Sept 2025; **first substantive session Geneva 6–7 July 2026** (i.e., this week), fed by >1,500 written submissions and structured consultations since Jan 2026 ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/un-global-dialogue-opens-urgent-call-safe-and-inclusive-ai-benefits-all); [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1167873)).
- **WSIS+20 outcome (adopted by consensus 17 Dec 2025)**: makes the **IGF a permanent UN forum**, establishes a **joint implementation roadmap aligning WSIS with the GDC**, reaffirms multistakeholder governance and human rights anchoring, and **explicitly reaffirms investment in DPI** ([Global Partners Digital](https://www.gp-digital.org/wsis20-what-the-final-outcome-delivers-and-what-it-leaves-unresolved/); [Internet Society](https://www.internetsociety.org/blog/2025/12/wsis20-reaffirms-multistakeholder-governance-and-a-lasting-igf/); [EC welcome statement](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/eu-welcomes-wsis20-outcome-un-general-assembly-commits-inclusive-digital-future-and)).
- **India AI Impact Summit (16–21 Feb 2026, New Delhi)**: the **New Delhi Declaration on AI Impact** adopted 19 Feb 2026, endorsed by 89 countries/organizations; launched a **"Global AI Impact Commons"** (a voluntary registry of 80+ AI-for-good impact stories across 30+ countries) ([MEA press release](https://www.mea.gov.in/press-releases.htm?dtl%2F40810%2FAI_Impact_Summit_2026_Concludes_with_Adoption_of_New_Delhi_Declaration=); [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234343&reg=3&lang=1)). Note the naming collision with the operator's "Planetary Intelligence Commons" concept — the term "Commons" is now UN-adjacent vocabulary, which cuts both ways (legitimacy + dilution).

**Read**: multilateral AI governance is in its institution-building year. The channels a Commons would submit into (Global Dialogue written submissions, IGF, WSIS-GDC roadmap consultations) are open, explicitly multistakeholder, and hungry for civil-society technical proposals — participation costs words, not money.

## 6. The DPI movement — mid-2026 ground truth

### 6.1 India Stack at scale, and its honest counterpoint

- **UPI**: 23.2B transactions in May 2026 (~₹29.9T); 22B+ in June 2026 (+23% YoY); ~49% of global real-time payment volume; acceptance in 22 international markets, ~45M cross-border transactions/month (Apr 2026); Aadhaar ~1.38B enrolled ([coinlaw UPI statistics](https://coinlaw.io/upi-statistics/); [IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/news/upi-transactions-rise-23-to-over-22-billion-in-june); [The Platinum Capital](https://www.theplatinumcapital.com/article/india-upi-network-crosses-20bn-monthly-transactions-real-time-payment-adoption-global-record)).
- **ONDC — the cautionary mirror**: nominally 800+ cities and 370k+ sellers ([IBM overview](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ondc); [Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/open-network-for-digital-commerce-ondc)), but retail orders FELL from 6.5M (Oct 2024) to 4.6M (Feb 2025) as subsidies were cut ~88% per buyer app; three senior executives departed since Dec 2024 (chair R.S. Sharma, CBO Shireesh Joshi, CEO T. Koshy); flagship partner PhonePe Pincode exited and shut consumer operations by Dec 2025; governance and dispute resolution remain fragmented with "no clear rules if a network partner defaults" ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/ondc-ecommerce-failure-struggles-india-125050501101_1.html); [Finshots](https://finshots.in/archive/has-the-ondc-experiment-failed-nrai-rapido-t-koshy-swiggy-zomato-food-delivery/); [The Ken](https://the-ken.com/the-nutgraf/ondc-is-losing/)). **Lesson: an open protocol with state backing and subsidies still loses when it demands users change behavior against superior platform UX — and when the challenge/dispute layer is an afterthought.** (Note: the dispute layer is precisely what the receipt design leads with.)

### 6.2 Beckn — the protocol that outgrew its network

- Founded 2019 by **Nandan Nilekani, Pramod Varma, and Sujith Nair**; stewarded by **FIDE (Foundation for Interoperability in Digital Economy)**, a donations-funded nonprofit employing only "a small number of software architects, community managers, and program managers" plus volunteers ([FIDE](https://fide.org/founders/sujith-nair/); [arXiv:2504.18602 "Beyond Platforms"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18602)).
- **Beckn Onix + Google Cloud partnership (3 Nov 2025)**: open-source cloud-native deployment kit cutting open-network setup from 4–5 months to ~45 minutes; live deployments include Uttar Pradesh's Open Network for Agriculture (12,800+ farmers), ONEST (livelihoods), VISTAAR (agriculture), DEG/UEI (energy); target sectors: agriculture, health, skilling, energy, tourism, mobility. Google Cloud's framing: **"Open networks are the digital rails for the next generation of AI"** ([Google Cloud press release](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2025-11-03-Beckn-Labs-and-Google-Cloud-Partner-to-Accelerate-the-Adoption-of-Open-Networks-Worldwide-with-Beckn-Onix)).
- International spread: Indonesia is adopting an open digital commerce approach drawing on ONDC's model ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/indonesia-png-join-50-in-5-as-digital-public-infrastructure-push-expands); [nasscom](https://community.nasscom.in/communities/application/what-beckn-protocol-backbone-ondc)).
- **Lesson: FIDE's leverage came from authoring the SPEC and reference implementation, not operating networks.** Networks (ONDC, UP-ONA, DEG) are others' problems; the tiny nonprofit owns the grammar. This is the replicable move for a solo operator.

### 6.3 The global DPI institutional stack

- **50-in-5 campaign** (launched Nov 2023 by 11 first-mover countries incl. Estonia, Singapore, Norway, Sri Lanka, Togo): **30 countries joined to date**; Indonesia and Papua New Guinea joined June 2026; first 50-in-5 Awards announced for April 2026; target: 50 countries with at least one DPI component by end-2028 ([50in5.net](https://50in5.net/); [UNDP launch release](https://www.undp.org/news/11-first-mover-countries-launch-50-5-campaign-accelerate-digital-public-infrastructure-adoption-around-world); [Biometric Update June 2026](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/indonesia-png-join-50-in-5-as-digital-public-infrastructure-push-expands)).
- **Gates Foundation**: US$200M five-year DPI commitment (announced UNGA 77), covering payments, digital ID, data-sharing, civil registries ([Gates Foundation DPI program](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/our-work/programs/global-growth-and-opportunity/digital-public-infrastructure); [mobileidworld](https://mobileidworld.com/gates-foundation-backs-un-campaign-to-accelerate-digital-infrastructure/)).
- **UNDP** is the coordinating partner across 50-in-5, DPGA, GovStack, IDB, UNICEF ([UNDP DPI](https://www.undp.org/digital/digital-public-infrastructure)).
- **Universal DPI Safeguards Framework** (UN ODET + UNDP, released 2024, updated 2025): 18 foundational/operational principles, 13 lifecycle risks, 300+ recommendations; a **DPI Safeguards Accelerator** launched Sept 2025 at UNGA-80 to move countries from commitment to implementation ([dpi-safeguards.org](https://www.dpi-safeguards.org/framework); [UNDP press release](https://www.undp.org/press-releases/un-releases-universal-dpi-safeguards-framework-promote-safe-and-inclusive-digital-public-infrastructure)).
- **G20 and WSIS+20** have both formally recognized DPI as an SDG accelerator ([UNDP/G20](https://www.undp.org/press-releases/g20-digital-ministers-recognize-digital-public-infrastructure-accelerator-global-goals); [GPD on WSIS+20](https://www.gp-digital.org/wsis20-what-the-final-outcome-delivers-and-what-it-leaves-unresolved/)).

## 7. The DPGA path — concrete registration mechanics

- **The DPG Standard** (v1.1.6, published 4 Sep 2024; maintained by DPGA Secretariat, CC-BY-SA) has **9 indicators**: (1) SDG Relevance, (2) Open Licensing, (3) **Clear Ownership** [a legal entity must own the project], (4) Platform Independence, (5) Documentation, (6) Mechanism for Extracting Non-PII Data, (7) Privacy & Applicable Laws, (8) Open Standards & Best Practices, (9) Do No Harm by Design (9A Data Privacy & Security, 9B Inappropriate/Illegal Content, 9C Protection from Harassment) ([digitalpublicgoods.net/standard](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/standard); [DPG-Standard GitHub](https://github.com/DPGAlliance/DPG-Standard)). 2026 revisions tightened indicators 7 and 9A toward demonstrated practice (data minimization, consent, retention rules) ([UNDP blog](https://www.undp.org/digital/blog/how-dpg-standard-and-universal-dpi-safeguards-framework-are-charting-safe-and-inclusive-digital-future)).
- **Registry state (fetched from DPGA FAQ, July 2026): 243 recognized DPGs; 46 new certifications in 2025 (+25% YoY); 120 applications currently under review; 35 origin countries. Application is free**; entry points are an eligibility self-test, then nomination through the submission portal; two-stage review; DPG status is valid one year, then reassessed ([DPGA FAQ](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/frequently-asked-questions); [UNICEF accelerator guide](https://unicef.github.io/publicgoods-accelerator-guide/about-dpgs/what-is-a-dpg/)). Review duration: not published (UNVERIFIED; 120-deep queue suggests months).
- **Eligible DPG types include "Standard"** — not just software: the registry filters by Software, AI Model, Content, Data, **Standard** ([DPG Registry](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/registry)). A receipt SCHEMA + reference implementation can register as a DPG in its own right.
- Benefits: globally trusted credential, registry visibility to governments/funders, access to the DPG product community ([DPGA FAQ](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/frequently-asked-questions)).

## 8. The agentic protocol stack — what exists and what doesn't (mid-2026)

- **MCP** (Anthropic) — agent↔tool connection. **A2A** — agent↔agent, donated to the **Linux Foundation**, joint interoperability effort with MCP ([dev.to state-of-standards](https://dev.to/alexmercedcoder/the-state-of-agentic-ai-standards-in-2026-mcp-a2a-webmcp-osi-and-the-protocol-stack-taking-3o2l); [Zylos](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-03-26-agent-interoperability-protocols-mcp-a2a-acp-convergence/)).
- **AP2** (Google, open-licensed 16 Sept 2025) — chains three cryptographically signed mandates (Intent → Cart → Payment) forming "a non-repudiable audit trail of who authorized what, within what limits" — **for payments only** ([eco.com AP2 explainer](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15192002-ap2-protocol-explained-google-s-agentic-commerce-standard-2026)). **ACP** (OpenAI + Stripe) competes in the same purchase lane ([GitHub ACP](https://github.com/agentic-commerce-protocol/agentic-commerce-protocol)). Agentic retail spend projected ~$20.9B in 2026 ([Ekamoira/eMarketer](https://www.ekamoira.com/blog/how-ai-agents-are-changing-e-commerce-in-2026-open-protocols-explained-complete-guide)).
- **Confirmed absence**: nothing in this stack answers under whose *community* authority an agent acts, what material/ecological burden it creates, which telos it serves, what independent evidence proves the outcome, or who can challenge/reverse it. AP2's mandate chain is the closest existing artifact to a causal action receipt — and it stops at "payment authorized." The mission's premise survives adversarial checking.

Adjacent: the **EU Carbon Removals Certification Framework** (first vertical candidate for outcome receipts): first permanent-removal certification methodologies (Delegated Act EU 2026/285) entered force 7 May 2026; carbon-farming methodologies expected summer 2026; scheme recognition now open; **the EU's centralized registry arrives only by December 2028** — a 2.5-year window in which certified-outcome evidence infrastructure is needed but not yet state-supplied ([EC Climate Action](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/news-other-reads/news/commission-adopts-rules-and-launches-initiatives-boost-carbon-removals-and-carbon-farming-eu-2025-12-01_en); [Carbon Gap tracker](https://tracker.carbongap.org/policy/crcf/); [EC May 2026](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/news-other-reads/news/europe-lays-foundations-robust-carbon-removals-and-carbon-farming-market-2026-05-29_en)).

## 9. What this means for the Commons

**1. The tailwind is real, but it blows at the receipt layer, not at the "planetary commons" brand.** Regulators will never mandate a noosphere. They ARE mandating — in four uncoordinated fragments — every stage of the causal action receipt: automatic decision logging (AI Act Art. 12), provenance marking (Art. 50/C2PA), delegated-authority mandates (AP2's model, IMDA Singapore's agent-identity requirement), legally presumed record integrity (eIDAS qualified ledgers), and exculpatory evidence under strict liability (PLD, Dec 2026). The Commons' constitutional identity is the private telos; the public artifact must be the receipt schema. Sell the spine, keep the soul.

**2. The genuinely unowned whitespace, stated precisely**: there is no open, cross-domain standard that binds *identity/delegation → intention → material debit → community authority → evidence → outcome → challenge/reversal* into one verifiable object. AP2 stops at payment. C2PA stops at media. Article 12 logs are private and schema-less. eIDAS ledgers are a legal container with no content standard. Singapore's IMDA framework demands exactly this binding ("verifiable digital identity and an audit trail of which agent acted under whose authorisation") and cites no standard that provides it. Whoever publishes the credible open schema first becomes what C2PA became for media provenance — the thing the codes of practice name "by example."

**3. DPG registration is the cheapest legitimacy the operator can buy, and it is solo-feasible — with one hard prerequisite.** 243 DPGs exist; application is free; "Standard" is an eligible type; the 9 indicators are mostly hygiene a disciplined solo operator already half-satisfies (open license, docs, privacy practice). The blocker is **Indicator 3, Clear Ownership: a legal entity must own the artifact.** The Commons currently has no entity. Precedent says the entity should be a FIDE-shaped nonprofit (3 founders, donations, spec-stewardship mandate), not a company. This is the concrete, dated first institutional move.

**4. The adoption pattern is not replicable solo — so don't replicate it; do the Beckn move instead.** MOSIP/DHIS2/Beckn all needed institutional anchor + $5–30M philanthropy + one first-deployer government. A solo operator cannot manufacture those. But FIDE's actual leverage was authoring the spec and reference implementation with a handful of people and letting Google Cloud, ONDC, and state governments carry deployment. The corresponding play: publish the Causal Action Receipt spec + a signing/verification reference implementation (the dharma_swarm receipt spine, extracted and hardened), register it as a DPG, and submit it into the channels that are open right now — the Global Dialogue on AI Governance (first session was 6–7 July 2026; the process continues to May 2027), the WSIS-GDC joint roadmap, the DPI Safeguards Accelerator, and IMDA's agentic framework consultation orbit. All accept written technical contributions from non-state actors at zero cost.

**5. ONDC is the memento mori.** State power, Beckn protocol, and subsidies could not overcome bad UX and a fragmented dispute layer; retail volume collapsed when subsidies stopped. Two transfers: (a) a receipts commons must attach to flows that already MUST exist (compliance logs, PLD defense evidence, carbon certification, wallet attestations) rather than asking anyone to adopt new behavior; (b) ONDC's named failure — "no clear rules if a network partner defaults," fragmented dispute resolution — is the exact organ the receipt design leads with (challenge/reversal). The Commons' differentiator is that it treats the dispute layer as the product, not the afterthought.

**6. Timing discipline.** 2026-08-02 is a forcing function for GPAI *providers*, not for an outsider's receipt layer — using it as a personal deadline is theater. The load-bearing dates for this strategy are: **Dec 2026** (PLD transposition + EUDI wallets + end of marking grace — the quarter when "evidence infrastructure" becomes a mainstream EU procurement topic), **Dec 2027** (Art. 12 logging binds for Annex III high-risk — the omnibus just gifted 16 months to have the schema and reference implementation credible before logging demand peaks), and **Dec 2028** (CRCF central registry — the interim gap is the nature-restoration wedge, consistent with the prior vote). The omnibus also teaches: dates slip, direction doesn't. Build on the direction.

**7. One honest threat to the deepest identity.** The vocabulary is being enclosed: "Global AI Impact Commons" (New Delhi, Feb 2026) and the UN's commons-flavored language mean the *name* will not differentiate. And the agentic-protocol land grab (Google, OpenAI/Stripe, Linux Foundation) is moving at a cadence a solo operator cannot match on breadth. The only defensible position is depth on the one object nobody else is building — the full-cycle, challengeable, materially-accounted receipt — anchored to the one legal instrument nobody else has noticed (the eIDAS qualified electronic ledger presumption). That is a real, narrow, current opening. It will not stay open past the point where an incumbent notices that "audit trail" and "legal presumption of integrity" can be composed.
