A hundred brilliant people, each holding one true piece — and nothing that connects them.

AI has stopped being abstract. It runs on real electricity, water, and mined silicon — and it is making content, claims, and analysis infinite and nearly free. When anything can be generated, the scarce asset becomes the one thing that cannot be faked at scale: verified truth. That is the thesis under everything here.

Nowhere is the hunger for it sharper than where money tries to heal nature. A credit is supposed to mean a tonne kept out of the sky, or a hectare brought back to life. Investigations found that something like 90% of one major program's rainforest credits were likely phantom (contested in magnitude, robust in direction) — and the tell is that buyers now pay a premium of 10–100× for the credits they can actually believe. The market is already paying, a lot, for proof it cannot reliably get.

The shape of the field

We mapped roughly a hundred of the most serious actors working on this — the AI-energy people, the carbon market, the nature-tech labs, the restoration movement, the standards bodies, the thinkers. They are not failures; each is expert at the slice it holds. The problem is the shape they make together.

The Seam Index · our construct, illustrative not a measurement

A hundred geniuses, no smarter than the best single one.

A proven theorem (Condorcet 1785; Krogh–Vedelsby 1995) says many judges beat any one judge — but only when three conditions hold. The field has the first in abundance and neither of the others.

The offer

A free, decorrelated second opinion on one public restoration or carbon-removal claim — surfacing the counterfactual and the disagreement single-vendor judges miss, with our own estimated footprint printed on the output. No cost, no strings. If it helps, it helps; if it's wrong, we show our work. Earned, not decreed.
