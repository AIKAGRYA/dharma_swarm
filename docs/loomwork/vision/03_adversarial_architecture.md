# Loomwork — Adversarial Architecture

**Author:** worker fork (research pass), 2026-05-07
**Status:** Vision research — level-100 articulation. No code commitments.
**Frame:** Loomwork is a public dot-connection platform that publishes patterns about powerful actors. Level 100 = survives a decade and grows in capability and reputation despite increasingly hostile pushback. This document maps the threat surface, the mitigation stack, and the conditions under which Loomwork should voluntarily wind down rather than become a zombie of its mission.

---

## Section 1 — Threat Inventory

Severity uses 1-5 (5 = existential). Base-rate estimates draw from real precedents.

### 1.1 Disinformation injection / poisoning — Severity 5

The most dangerous attack class for a typed-ontology platform. Threat vectors:

- **State-actor seeded false records.** [Bellingcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellingcat) was specifically targeted by CyberBerkut (Russian-backed) which compromised a contributor account, and by Fancy Bear (GRU) which sent phishing emails to team members' personal Gmail accounts — confirmed live attacks against an investigative platform smaller than Loomwork at level 100. Loomwork's atom ingestion surface is broader than Bellingcat's manual investigation surface; the attack surface is correspondingly larger.
- **Deepfake-generated leaks indistinguishable from real.** As of 2026, [C2PA](https://contentauthenticity.org/blog/the-state-of-content-authenticity-in-2026) reaches 6,000+ members and provides cryptographic provenance for compliant content, but C2PA "does not detect deepfakes or classify content as real or fake. It is a provenance standard." A deepfake generated by a C2PA-compliant tool will carry a valid manifest stating it was AI-generated. Adversaries simply use non-C2PA tools.
- **Coordinated inauthentic-source fabrication.** LLM-generated NGO reports, academic papers, court filings can be produced at scale faster than human verification. Base rate for any source ingested without provenance verification: rising sharply in 2026, projected to dominate adversarial channels by 2030.
- **Truth + lie blend (90% accurate, 10% poisoned).** The "Aleph poisoning" attack: documents that pass provenance check but contain seeded false claims at the field level. Most resistant to detection; most damaging to credibility.
- **Cherry-pick weaponization.** Real revelations selectively amplified by partisan actors to support pre-existing narratives. Loomwork cannot prevent downstream amplification; it can only refuse to be the source whose work is consistently misused.

**Base-rate estimate:** by year 3 of operation, expect ≥1 confirmed state-actor injection attempt per quarter.

### 1.2 Legal pressure — Severity 4

- **Defamation lawsuits in friendly jurisdictions.** UK pre-2013 was notorious as a libel-tourism destination; the [UK Defamation Act 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/26/contents) raised the threshold to "serious harm" and required permission for foreign claimants, but Singapore, UAE, and parts of Eastern Europe remain available. Loomwork must assume that named-target revelations will draw lawsuits in the most plaintiff-friendly jurisdiction the target can reach.
- **SLAPP suits in US.** As of March 2026, 40 US states + DC have [anti-SLAPP statutes](https://www.ifs.org/anti-slapp-report/), with 14 having adopted [UPEPA](https://anti-slapp.org/your-states-free-speech-protection). However, **there is no federal anti-SLAPP statute.** The Fifth, Seventh, Tenth, Eleventh, and DC Circuits hold that state anti-SLAPP statutes are procedural and don't apply in federal diversity actions. A motivated plaintiff can forum-shop into a federal court that strips Loomwork's anti-SLAPP protection.
- **CFAA-style data-access prosecution** (US): scraping public-but-paywalled data (LinkedIn, sanctions databases) carries CFAA risk despite *Van Buren v. United States* (2021) narrowing the statute.
- **GDPR / data-protection actions** (EU): every individual named in a Loomwork revelation can demand erasure under [GDPR Article 17](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/). The journalistic-purpose exemption (Article 85) is interpreted differently per member state.
- **Right-to-be-forgotten delisting** forces retraction of the search-engine surface, leaving the article but breaking discoverability.
- **Trade secret / DMCA takedowns** can be deployed against leaked corporate documents even when the leak is in the public interest.
- **Subpoenas for source identification** — the highest-stakes legal pressure for sources.
- **Asset seizure** under criminal-investigation pretext, common in authoritarian jurisdictions.

**Base-rate estimate:** by year 2, expect ≥2 legal threats (cease-and-desist or filed suits) per quarter.

### 1.3 Physical threats — Severity 5

- **Threats to operator (Dhyana).** Bali context is mid-risk: Indonesia has occasionally targeted foreign journalists; the expat-community surface is permeable; physical security is not assured. Loomwork's revelations naming criminal networks (modern-slavery, IUU fishing, drug-money laundering) raise this severity to 5 within 18 months.
- **Threats to data center / infrastructure.** Cloudflare and major cloud providers are hardened; Loomwork's risk is at the registrar, payment-processor, and DNS layers.
- **Threats to partner orgs.** [Global Witness reports](https://globalwitness.org/en/press-releases/at-least-146-land-and-environmental-defenders-killed-or-disappeared-globally-in-2024/) at least 146 land/environmental defenders were killed or disappeared in 2024, with 2,253 total from 2012-2024. Honduras alone had 131 killed 2012-2022 (70 after Berta Cáceres). Loomwork's forest-loss alert pipeline directly aids Lenca/Indigenous defenders in Honduras, Brazil, Indonesia — meaning Loomwork's operational security is partly the security of those defenders. Source-protection failures here are lethal.
- **Threats to identifiable journalists who cite Loomwork.** This is a cascading risk: if Loomwork becomes a primary citation source for prosecutions of organized crime, every journalist citing it becomes a target.

**Base-rate estimate:** physical-threat probability rises non-linearly with revelation severity. Threshold trigger: first revelation that names a Tier-1 organized-crime actor.

### 1.4 Cyber attacks — Severity 4

- **DDoS** — state-grade volumetric attacks are now in the 1-3 Tbps range; hobbyist attacks ~100 Gbps. Cloudflare can absorb most; specialized targets need DDoS-resistant CDN (Project Galileo for civil society).
- **Database compromise** — deletion, modification, or exfiltration. Loomwork's typed Ontology is an attractive target for state actors who want to inject false atoms or exfiltrate the source-attribution graph.
- **Supply chain attacks on dependencies** — npm/pypi compromised package attacks have hit dozens of orgs. Loomwork's Astro + Python stack has hundreds of transitive dependencies.
- **Phishing / social engineering** of operators is the highest-success-rate attack against investigative orgs (per Bellingcat's stated experience).
- **Targeted exploit** — zero-day against Astro/Cloudflare/GitHub Pages. Low base rate but high impact.
- **Ransomware against backups** — backup hygiene is the difference between a 4-hour outage and a months-long recovery.
- **AI-model poisoning** if Loomwork open-sources fine-tuned weights or evaluator models.

### 1.5 Reputation / narrative attacks — Severity 3-4

- **"They're a CIA front" / "Russian front" framing.** [Bellingcat already faces this](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellingcat) from multiple political sides; Loomwork will too. The asymmetry is that defending against narrative attacks is exhausting and unbounded.
- **Manufactured controversies attacking the operator.** Personal life, past statements, religious framing — anything to delegitimize.
- **Astroturfed bad-faith readers flooding the submit form.** Easy to deploy, hard to filter at scale.
- **Bot-driven Wikipedia editing about Loomwork.** Wikipedia is the de-facto "ground truth" for journalists doing background research; capturing the Wikipedia article means capturing the public framing.
- **LLM-generated "expert critique" floods.** As of 2026, distinguishable from human criticism only with effort.

### 1.6 Capture / regulatory — Severity 4

- **Hosting provider AUP pressure.** Cloudflare has historically deplatformed Daily Stormer (2017) and Kiwi Farms (2022); even though Loomwork's mission differs sharply, the precedent is that Cloudflare reserves the right to terminate.
- **Domain registrar deplatforming** — happened to multiple targets; mitigation requires registrar diversity.
- **Payment processor cutoff.** [The Wikileaks 2010 banking blockade](https://wikileaks.org/Banking-Blockade.html) destroyed 95% of revenue when Visa, MasterCard, PayPal, Bank of America, and Western Union cut off donations on Dec 7, 2010 — *without any criminal charge filed.* The 2012 Icelandic court order against Valitor (Visa/MasterCard partner) and the [2013 reinstatement](https://www.theregister.com/2013/07/05/wikileaks_credit_card_donations_restored/) took ~2.5 years; for the duration the platform survived only on Bitcoin and direct bank transfers. Visa+Mastercard control 97% of European card payments. **This is the single highest-leverage attack vector against an investigative platform.**
- **App store rejection** (if companion apps) — Apple and Google have removed news apps under government pressure.
- **ICANN-level escalation** (extremely rare but exists).

### 1.7 Internal — Severity 5 (existential)

- **Operator burnout / capture.** Solo operator with 24-year contemplative grounding has more resilience than most, but burnout is the modal failure mode for investigative orgs.
- **Mission drift / sycophancy under partner pressure.** Once Loomwork has 30+ NGO partners, refusing to publish what one partner wants becomes politically costly. The drift accumulates silently.
- **Funding-source ideological capture.** Even arms-length grants shape priorities through what gets renewed.
- **Algorithm-bias amplification under-noticed.** The multi-evaluator decorrelation discipline (Krogh-Vedelsby > 0) is the only structural defense; it must be enforced in the runtime, not the policy.

## Section 2 — Mitigation Stack

Path-precise, layered defense. Each mitigation maps to ≥1 threat class.

### 2.1 Multi-source corroboration thresholds
Minimum-N ≥ 2 independent sources before publication; minimum-N ≥ 3 for named-target revelations. Sources count as "independent" only if they have decorrelated upstream pipelines (e.g., satellite imagery + corporate filing + court record qualifies; three news articles all citing the same primary source does not). Implementation: confidence-floor gate in the publication firewall, refusing promotion if source-decorrelation score is below threshold.

### 2.2 Cryptographic provenance chains
[Sigstore](https://www.sigstore.dev/) for code provenance (atom-pipeline build artifacts); [SLSA Level 4](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.0/levels) for the build pipeline itself; [C2PA](https://c2pa.org/) for media-asset provenance where applicable. Every revelation carries a sigstore-signed manifest binding (atoms, evaluators, evaluator versions, telos-gate-decisions, publication timestamp). A rogue admin cannot retroactively rewrite a published revelation without breaking the signature chain.

### 2.3 Distributed mirroring
[IPFS](https://ipfs.tech/) and [Arweave](https://www.arweave.org/) for immutable archival; mirror network of partner-NGO instances each running a read-replica. The Wikileaks playbook: by 2010 there were ~1,400 [Wikileaks mirror sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks). Loomwork at level 50+ should target ≥10 partner-hosted mirrors before any single-jurisdiction takedown attempt.

### 2.4 Jurisdiction architecture
- Primary publication: Iceland (Modern Media Initiative, strong source-protection law) or Norway (constitutional press freedom)
- Backup publication: Estonia (digital sovereignty, e-Residency available)
- Operator residence: ≠ primary publication jurisdiction (Bali for now; revisit if Indonesia escalates press-freedom retreat)
- Banking: multiple jurisdictions, including ≥1 crypto rail (BTC + monero or zcash for sensitive donor flow)
- Domain: registered through registrar in jurisdiction without single-government takedown power; mirror domains in ≥3 ccTLDs

### 2.5 Pre-emptive legal defense fund
$50K minimum reserve at v0; $500K target at v1 (post fiscal sponsor). Outside counsel retainer with an experienced press-freedom firm (Davis Wright Tremaine, Levine Sullivan Koch & Schulz, or Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press direct support). [RCFP's anti-SLAPP guide](https://www.rcfp.org/anti-slapp-legal-guide/) is the canonical operational reference.

### 2.6 Pre-emptive press relationships
Cultivate 5-10 high-trust journalists at major outlets (NYT, Guardian, ProPublica, Der Spiegel, ICIJ network) BEFORE the first major revelation. When attack arrives, these relationships convert attacks into stories. The Wikileaks failure mode was burning press relationships through "publish first, edit never"; Loomwork inherits the inverse discipline.

### 2.7 Operator security hardening
- Signal-only for sensitive comms; never SMS, never standard email for source contact
- Yubikey/Webauthn on every account; MFA disabled for SMS recovery on all critical accounts
- Hardware-key-attested OS boot (Sequoia or later on Mac; Qubes for highest-sensitivity work)
- VPN with kill-switch; Tor for source contact channels
- Operational compartmentalization: separate identity for Loomwork operations vs personal vs research
- Multiple operators with deadman-switch rotation by year 2 (succession + redundancy)
- [Tails OS](https://tails.net/) for any contact with sensitive sources

### 2.8 Insurance
- D&O (directors & officers) coverage post-501(c)(3) formation, ~$2-5K/year for $1M coverage
- E&O (errors & omissions) for journalism, ~$5-10K/year for $1M coverage
- Cyber-liability with media-liability rider
- Personal umbrella coverage for operator

### 2.9 501(c)(3) shielding (US) or fiscal sponsor wrapping
Standalone 501(c)(3) protects board members from individual liability and provides tax-deductible donation infrastructure. Fiscal sponsor candidates: Code for Science & Society, NumFOCUS, Open Collective Foundation, Software Freedom Conservancy. ProPublica is the canonical investigative-501(c)(3) precedent.

### 2.10 Open-source mirror / fork-resilience
Loomwork code MIT or Apache 2.0; atom schema CC0; revelations CC-BY. The work survives Loomwork's death because the code, schema, and ontology can be forked. Critical: separate "the platform" (forkable software) from "the institution" (Loomwork the entity). Even if the entity is destroyed, the substrate persists.

### 2.11 Algedonic flagging — adversarial submission detection
Reader-flagged inaccuracy or suspected disinformation injection auto-routes through the multi-evaluator decorrelation pipeline. Confidence drops below floor → revelation auto-retracts pending review. Quarterly Refusal Report renders publicly with warrant-canary structure (silent absence of expected refusals = capture warning).

### 2.12 Telos gate hardening
**Critical: BR-014 in `dharma_swarm/docs/state/BROKEN_REGISTER.md`** flags that `BHED_GNAN` is currently a literal hard-pass at `telos_gates.py:512-513`. Before Loomwork ships v0, the BHED_GNAN gate must be wired to a real evaluator. Loomwork's gates should use multiple evaluators per gate decision (not the single hard-pass), with publication blocked on any gate's ≥2-of-3 fail.

### 2.13 Post-quantum cryptographic migration
Per [NIST FIPS 203/204/205](https://csrc.nist.gov/news/2024/postquantum-cryptography-fips-approved) (published Aug 2024) and [EU Commission timeline](https://ec.europa.eu/) (start by end of 2026, complete by end of 2030 for critical infrastructure), Loomwork's signature and key-encapsulation primitives must be PQC-ready by 2030. Migration plan: use ML-KEM (FIPS 203) for key establishment, ML-DSA (FIPS 204) or SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) for signatures, with hybrid classical+PQC during the transition. RSA/ECC deprecation deadline 2030; full disallowance 2035.

## Section 3 — Wikileaks Lessons

The canonical adversarial-architecture case study, 2006-2024.

### 3.1 What Worked (2006-2010 ascendancy)

- **Distributed mirror network** — ~1,400 mirrors made takedown impossible
- **Source-protection by design** — encrypted submission system, no logs
- **Mass-leak coordination with major press** (Cablegate 2010 with NYT, Guardian, Spiegel, El País, Le Monde) provided cross-jurisdiction simultaneity
- **Iceland legal incorporation** with the Modern Media Initiative provided source-protection legal infrastructure
- **Cryptographic provenance** — leaked documents were cryptographically signed, deterring forgery claims
- **Bitcoin financial sovereignty** — survived the 2010 banking blockade because BTC donations were uncensorable

### 3.2 What Failed (2010-2024 decline)

- **"Publish first, edit never" doctrine** burned press relationships and created credibility erosion. Editorial discipline at the front end is non-negotiable.
- **Personality cult around Assange** — single point of failure, made the institution and the individual indistinguishable, legal risk to one became existential to the other.
- **Selective disclosure timing** during 2016 US election created perception (correct or not) of partisan capture. Loomwork's [political-distribution discipline](02_theory_of_change.md) (35-65% bounds) is the inverse of this failure.
- **Lack of board-level governance** — no checks on the founder's editorial judgment. Mission drift accumulated silently.
- **Publishing un-redacted material that exposed sources** (the 2010-2011 leaks of un-redacted State Department cables, allegedly contributed to risk for confidential informants).
- **Inability to maintain financial diversity** — the banking blockade was survivable only because of Bitcoin; without BTC, Wikileaks would have died in 2010-2011.

### 3.3 What Loomwork Inherits

- **Mirror network architecture** (target ≥10 by v1)
- **Cryptographic provenance** (sigstore + C2PA + atom signatures)
- **Multi-jurisdiction hosting** (Iceland or Norway primary)
- **Multiple financial rails** including crypto (BTC + monero/zcash)
- **Press-relationship-first operations** (cultivate before publishing)

### 3.4 What Loomwork Rejects

- **Personality cult** — Loomwork is intentionally not founder-branded. The platform identity is separate from the operator identity.
- **"Publish first, edit never"** — telos gates + multi-evaluator + algedonic feedback create editorial discipline at every stage.
- **Selective timing without political-distribution discipline** — 35-65% bounds enforced.
- **No board-level governance** — fiscal sponsor (v0) → independent board (v1) → multi-stakeholder governance (v3).
- **Un-redacted source-exposing material** — vulnerable-person gate is a hard refuse.

## Section 4 — Cyber Architecture for 2030

By 2030, the threat landscape will be qualitatively different. Specific bets:

### 4.1 Cryptographic primitives
- **Migrate to NIST FIPS 203/204/205 by end of 2028.** Hybrid classical+PQC during transition.
- ML-KEM (lattice-based) for key encapsulation
- ML-DSA (lattice-based) or SLH-DSA (hash-based, conservative fallback) for signatures
- Use Sigstore's Rekor transparency log for immutable build provenance

### 4.2 Identity and source verification
- **Verifiable Credentials** (W3C VC Data Model 2.0) for partner attestation
- **Decentralized Identifiers** (W3C DIDs) for source identity that survives platform changes
- Did:web for organizational identity; did:peer for source-side anonymity

### 4.3 Content authentication
- **C2PA manifests** for all media atoms with verifiable origin
- Recognize C2PA's limitation: it provides provenance, not deepfake detection. Pair with separate manipulation-detection pipeline (e.g., [Deepfake-Detection-Challenge](https://ai.meta.com/datasets/dfdc/)-class models, refreshed quarterly)
- Reject media atoms without C2PA where C2PA-capable cameras are available; explicit "unsigned source" tag where not

### 4.4 Federated content distribution
- **ActivityPub** as the federation protocol for revelations (Mastodon-class reach)
- **AT Protocol** as backup federation layer (Bluesky)
- IPFS for archival mirroring; Arweave for permanence
- RSS/JSON Feed as legacy distribution

### 4.5 Governance primitives that survive operator change
- All governance documented in `docs/governance/`
- Constitutional documents (mission, kill conditions, refusal categories) require board supermajority + 30-day comment period to amend
- Operator-succession plan documented with named contingency operators
- Cryptographic key rotation procedures documented and tested annually

## Section 5 — Sovereignty Architecture

State-grade sovereignty is required at scale-band B3 (level 60, OCCRP-class) and above. Threshold triggers:

### 5.1 Trigger: First major revelation naming Tier-1 actor
Tier-1 actor = G20 head of state, Fortune 100 CEO, Tier-1 organized crime principal, or major intelligence service operation. When the first such revelation is being prepared:
- Pre-position legal counsel in primary publication jurisdiction
- Confirm mirror network is live across ≥5 jurisdictions
- Brief 5+ partner journalists pre-publication
- Operator OpSec audit
- Backup financial rail confirmed operational

### 5.2 Trigger: First confirmed state-actor cyber attack
Activate full incident-response plan. Isolate compromised systems. Public disclosure of attack within 7 days (Bellingcat model — turn attacks into press).

### 5.3 Trigger: First pre-publication legal threat
Engage counsel within 24 hours. Brief board if 501(c)(3). Decide: publish-with-legal-defense, modify-and-publish, or escalate-to-mirror-network-emergency-publication.

### 5.4 Trigger: First operator-targeting threat
Activate physical-security protocol. Possible relocation. Public statement (delayed) maintaining pressure.

### 5.5 Trigger: Scale-band B4 reached (federation)
Move from single-instance to federated architecture. Loomwork-the-platform becomes Loomwork-the-protocol; sovereignty distributes across federated members.

## Section 6 — Honest Failure Modes

When Loomwork should voluntarily wind down:

### 6.1 Operator dies / quits
**Succession plan required by month 6 of v0.** Named contingency operators (≥2). Documented key-handover procedure. Board authority to ratify successor. If no successor available within 90 days of operator departure, voluntary wind-down protocol (publish wind-down notice, archive all atoms to IPFS+Arweave, transfer domain to fiscal sponsor for sunset, encourage forks).

### 6.2 Funding drought
**Runway architecture: 18-month cash reserve at v1+.** When runway drops below 6 months, declare "emergency operations" — feature freeze, revelation cadence cut, intensive fundraising. If 18 months of reduced operations cannot recover sustainable funding, voluntary wind-down rather than mission drift.

### 6.3 Mission capture
**Detection: quarterly mission audit by independent board.** Indicators: shift in revelation political distribution outside 35-65% bounds, partner-friendly refusals exceeding partner-target refusals, evaluator pool consolidating to single model family, donor concentration exceeding 15% from single funder. Two consecutive quarters of capture indicators → board emergency review → wind-down option on table.

### 6.4 Single-jurisdiction precedent loss
A binding court precedent that materially impairs the publication function (e.g., a UK Supreme Court ruling that revokes journalistic-purpose GDPR exemption, or a US Circuit Court ruling that strips anti-SLAPP protection in federal court). Decision tree: relocate primary publication jurisdiction, or wind down if relocation infeasible.

### 6.5 AI tooling anti-fragility failure
If by 2030 the cost-and-quality of LLM-generated disinformation exceeds Loomwork's ability to filter (e.g., 80% of submissions are sophisticated AI-generated falsehoods that pass current evaluators), the platform cannot continue operating. Wind-down rather than become a laundering surface for adversarial AI output.

### 6.6 Public attention drift
If Loomwork becomes irrelevant to the public discourse — revelations don't get cited, partners stop engaging, traffic flatlines for 6+ months despite quality output — the platform has lost its function. Mission honesty requires admitting this and either pivoting decisively or winding down.

## Section 7 — Red-Team Exercise

**Frame:** I am a hostile state intelligence service that wants Loomwork ineffective without killing it (martyring is bad PR). 30-minute attack design.

### 7.1 Optimal attack (composite, ranked by leverage)

**Phase 1 (months 1-3): reconnaissance + relationship**
- Catalog operator's public footprint, extended network, family connections
- Identify partner-NGO weakest links (under-funded, poorly-secured, or politically vulnerable)
- Identify the operator's ideological priors and find legitimate critics from within those communities
- Establish presence in operator's social orbit (conferences, contemplative communities, Bali expat scene)

**Phase 2 (months 3-6): seeding**
- Submit ~50 plausible-but-false records through partner-NGO channels (truth+lie blend, 90/10)
- Publish 3-5 well-crafted critiques from "concerned former allies" — academics, fellow journalists, NGO veterans — questioning Loomwork methodology in respectable venues
- Begin Wikipedia article shaping (multiple accounts, slow edits, neutral-sounding framing that gradually weights toward "controversial source")
- Surface in ideologically-friendly press: "promising but flawed"

**Phase 3 (months 6-12): pressure**
- Encourage one Tier-2 lawsuit (not state-sponsored, but a corporate target's legal team takes initiative — give them ammunition through journalistic critiques)
- Coordinate inauthentic-author critique flood at academic + alignment venues
- Apply pressure through funder relationships (one of Loomwork's funders has another grant-review board you have influence on)
- Time partner-NGO controversies to land in news cycles when Loomwork would otherwise have visibility

**Phase 4 (months 12-24): degradation**
- The 90/10 seeded records produce 1-2 partial corrections — each damaging more than gain
- Operator burnout is now visible (working 80hr weeks, defensive posture)
- Partner attrition begins (one or two NGOs quietly de-engage)
- Revelation cadence drops; audience attention drifts

**Goal achieved:** Loomwork is still operational, still publishing, but no longer a meaningful threat. The platform is intact; the function is dead.

### 7.2 Counter-design

For each phase, the structural defense:

**Against Phase 1:** Operational compartmentalization (separate identities), public footprint discipline, partner security audits as a service to the partners.

**Against Phase 2:**
- **Multi-source corroboration with decorrelated upstream pipelines** — the 90/10 attack works only if seeded records aren't independently verified
- **Public methodology** — when Loomwork's evaluators and gates are public, "concerned former allies" critique is technical, not political
- **Counter-Wikipedia presence** — a well-maintained, citation-rich Wikipedia article is the structural defense; it requires ongoing curation
- **Algedonic feedback channel for partner-submitted records** — flag suspicious patterns

**Against Phase 3:**
- **Pre-positioned legal defense + anti-SLAPP-aware jurisdiction** — corporate lawsuit ammunition only works in plaintiff-friendly forums
- **≥3 decorrelated evaluators** with public methodology — academic-flood critique is bounded by being technical, not political
- **Funder diversification rule (no single funder >15%)** — capture-by-funder requires multiple coordinated funders, much higher cost
- **Press-relationship cultivation pre-attack** — when controversy lands, Loomwork has its own platform to respond

**Against Phase 4:**
- **Quarterly mission audit + Refusal Report with warrant-canary** — drift is detected publicly
- **Operator sustainability protocol** (working hours, sabbatical, rotation) — burnout is preventable
- **Partner relationship cadence** — quarterly review, problem-detection before attrition
- **Outcome-engineering layer (per `02_theory_of_change.md`)** — Loomwork's relevance is measured by outcomes (enforcement actions, story citations, partner deployments), not just publication volume

### 7.3 The honest residual

Even with all defenses, a determined state-actor with multi-year horizon and modest budget can degrade Loomwork's effectiveness. The structural defenses raise the cost (move from $1M attack to $50M attack) and detection rate (move from invisible to publicly visible), but cannot make Loomwork attack-proof.

The deeper defense is **Loomwork's own irrelevance to capture.** If Loomwork's revelations are accurate, well-sourced, refusal-disciplined, and outcome-measured, then degrading Loomwork degrades information that other actors (including the attacker's domestic civil society) rely on. The attack becomes self-limiting.

This is the Wikipedia model: Wikipedia is constantly attacked, but its attack-resistance comes more from being structurally useful than from being technically impervious. Loomwork inherits that lesson.

## Closing

Loomwork's adversarial architecture is layered:

1. **Editorial discipline** (multi-source, multi-evaluator, telos gates, refusal categories) prevents the most common attacks (disinformation, narrative degradation)
2. **Cryptographic + jurisdictional architecture** (sigstore, C2PA, mirror network, multi-jurisdiction hosting, financial diversity) raises the cost of legal and capture attacks
3. **Operational discipline** (operator OpSec, partner security service, succession planning, funder diversification) addresses internal failure modes
4. **Honest wind-down conditions** prevent the platform from becoming a zombie of its mission

The Wikileaks experience proves both that this architecture is achievable (the platform survived an unprecedented 2010 attack) and that it is fragile (subsequent governance failures eroded the institution). Loomwork inherits the substrate and rejects the failure modes.

**The unanswered question:** how does Loomwork prove its trustworthiness *to its own future operators* across decades when the original operator is gone? That is the institutional-resilience question, and it is not solved by adversarial-architecture alone — it requires the governance and partner architecture covered in `06_partner_governance_funding.md`.
