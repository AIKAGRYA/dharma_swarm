# Loomwork — Partner Ecosystem, Governance, and Funding Architecture

**Author:** worker fork (research pass), 2026-05-07
**Status:** Vision research — level-100 articulation. Companion to 01-05.
**Frame:** Loomwork at level 100 is not a project — it is an institution that survives a decade and grows through hostile pressure. This document maps the partner network that sustains it, the governance that prevents capture, the funding that buys independence, and the legal architecture that lets it stay open under pressure. Real names, real budgets, real precedents — and where 2026 reality has shifted under prior planning, that shift is named explicitly.

The most consequential 2026 fact for this document: **OCCRP — the closest single comparator to Loomwork — had $22.1M annual budget in 2023, ~50% sourced from USAID, and lost 29% of operational budget ($5M+) in 2025 when US foreign aid was frozen, forcing 20% staff layoffs and pay cuts** ([Drop Site News](https://www.dropsitenews.com/p/occrp-budget-funding-us-government-usaid), [ICIJ on the freeze impact](https://www.icij.org/news/2025/02/foreign-aid-freeze-decimates-investigative-news-outlets-internationally/)). ICIJ, by contrast, derived only 6.2% of expenses from State Dept in 2024 and 8.6% in 2025 — the diversification spread was the difference between crippling and uncomfortable. The funding rule "**no single funder >15% over 5-year window**" is not theoretical discipline. It is the precondition for surviving the next funding-source revocation.

## Section 2 — Anti-Partners

These should not be partnership candidates; rationale is direct.

**Capture-prone funders:**
- **Any funder >15% of multi-year operating budget** — OCCRP's USAID dependency is the cautionary tale.
- **Foundations whose corporate parents are credible investigation targets** — examples (illustrative, not exhaustive): a fossil-fuel-major foundation if Loomwork investigates fossil-fuel methane; a tech-platform foundation whose parent owns advertising data Loomwork investigates; a defense-contractor-affiliated foundation if Loomwork covers conflict supply chains. Funder origin must pass the same telos check as a publication target.
- **Single-state-funded research arms** — even from democracies. State funding is legitimate in principle, capturable in practice. Survive the next election.

**Reputation-laundering candidates:**
- **Sovereign-wealth-funded research institutes (Gulf states, etc.)** that have purchased academic legitimacy without earning editorial independence. Loomwork does not validate their reputation.
- **Tech-billionaire personal foundations** absent demonstrable editorial firewall — being "founder-funded" is not the same as being "founder-controlled," but the burden of demonstrating the difference is on the funder.

**Surveillance-state-fronted research orgs:**
- Front orgs of state intelligence services posing as "open-source research" — pattern recognized in 2014–2024 across Russian, Chinese, and US-affiliated influence ops. Loomwork does not partner with research arms whose ultimate funder is a security service.

**"Investigative" orgs with documented bad-faith:**
- Outlets with sustained track record of fabrication, doxing of innocents, or partisan weaponization while claiming neutrality. Two patterns to filter: (a) sustained politically-asymmetric target distribution; (b) repeated retraction-without-correction.

**AI orgs whose interests would compromise telos gates:**
- AI vendors whose commercial roadmap depends on Loomwork waiving telos-gate rigor (e.g., bulk-revelation-volume incentives that conflict with the Theory-of-Change finding that fewer-better-revelations beats bulk).
- AI orgs that gate model access on data-sharing arrangements that compromise Loomwork's source-protection.

**Direct competitors:**
- Loomwork avoids partnership formats that dilute brand into another OSINT-vendor-pile (Maltego/ShadowDragon/Lampyre etc.) — those are tools, not peers.

The general rule: **if a partnership would require Loomwork to soften a telos gate, decline.** No exceptions.

## Section 3 — Governance Architecture (Bands 0–6)

Per `04_scale_architecture.md` band definitions.

### Band 0 — Solo operator (level 15)
Decision rights: operator (Dhyana) + advisory partners. Conflict resolution: operator. Mission-drift catch: weekly self-review against Arjuna Test (per `OPERATIONAL_DOCTRINE.md`). Funder firewall: no funders yet. Editorial independence: trivially intact (no one to capture). Succession: nothing yet — start writing the will-clause.

### Band 1 — Operator + 1–2 contributors (level 25)
Decision rights: operator decides; contributors have content-veto on their own work. Conflict resolution: operator + advisory committee of 3 (one OSINT veteran, one ethicist, one legal). Mission-drift catch: monthly Arjuna Test review with advisory. Funder firewall: 5-funder minimum before any single-funder >15% threshold becomes structural. Editorial independence: written editorial-firewall policy published. Succession: documented succession plan with named first-call backup.

### Band 2 — Small team (level 40)
Decision rights: editor-in-chief (editorial), executive director (operations), board (mission). Conflict resolution: board majority on mission disputes; EIC final on editorial. Mission-drift catch: quarterly board review + annual external audit. Funder firewall: formal donor-disclosure policy (Wikipedia Foundation model — every donation >$50K disclosed publicly). Editorial independence: chartered. Succession: deputy structure; key-person insurance.

### Band 3 — NGO-tier (level 60, OCCRP-class)
Decision rights: full board governance (5–9 directors), separate editorial board (5 senior journalists), management chain. Conflict resolution: board chair + ombudsperson. Mission-drift catch: independent annual mission audit (precedent: Mozilla Foundation reports). Funder firewall: published funder list; annual transparency report; explicit funder-firewall in editorial process. Succession: documented operator-replacement plan; board recruits from outside core network.

### Band 4 — Federation (level 80)
Decision rights: peer Loomworks govern themselves; federation council coordinates schema/protocol/safety standards. Precedent: ICANN's stakeholder model; Linux Foundation's project governance; W3C's working-group structure. Conflict resolution: federation council with rotating chair; per-instance editorial sovereignty. Mission-drift catch: cross-instance algedonic feedback. Funder firewall: federation members publish funders; cross-funder-overlap audit annually.

### Band 5 — Substrate (level 95, Wikidata-class)
Decision rights: multi-stakeholder governance — partner orgs + funders (capped) + readers + tech advisors. Precedent: Wikipedia Foundation's Board of Trustees (9 members, mixed-elected). Conflict resolution: chartered process; right of independent fork preserved as ultimate constraint. Editorial independence: chartered; can withstand single-board-capture.

### Band 6 — Public utility (level 100)
Decision rights: chartered public-utility status; mission protected by foundational documents that cannot be amended without supermajority. Precedent: Internet Archive's institutional resilience; the model the IETF/W3C aim toward. Succession: built-in; no operator dependency.

**The general principle, lifted from precedents:**
- **ICIJ's consortium model** scales coordination but limits speed — the embargo discipline that produced Panama Papers' impact is impossible without trust-network membership controls.
- **ProPublica's 501(c)(3) board** demonstrates that even foundation-heavy funding can preserve editorial independence given chartered firewalls.
- **Mozilla Foundation/Mozilla Corporation split** is the canonical model for separating mission-protection from commercial activity — Loomwork v3+ may need this structure for paid API tier.
- **Wikipedia Foundation's evolution** (and its crises — Wikimania controversies, board recall procedures) is the most-studied case of mission preservation under scale; Loomwork should adopt its donor-disclosure norms.
- **Linux Foundation's project governance** is the precedent for federation: per-project sovereignty with shared standards layer.

## Section 5 — Legal Architecture

### 501(c)(3) and fiscal sponsor

**Y1 path: fiscal sponsor.** Avoids 6–12 month 501(c)(3) wait; provides immediate grant-eligibility.

Candidates compared:
- **Code for Science & Society** ([codeforsociety.org](https://www.codeforsociety.org/resources/working-with-a-fiscal-sponsor)) — requires board approval + advisory committee + mission alignment. Strong fit for Loomwork's research-adjacent framing.
- **NumFOCUS** — scientific computing focus; weaker mission fit for Loomwork's investigative angle.
- **Open Source Collective** ([opencollective.com/opensource](https://opencollective.com/opensource)) — 501(c)(6) since 2017; lighter governance overhead; transparent ledger model.
- **Open Collective Foundation** — 501(c)(3) sister entity; fits investigative-journalism better than OSC.
- **Software Freedom Conservancy** ([sfconservancy.org](https://sfconservancy.org/)) — focus on FOSS legal protection; strong fit if Loomwork open-sources core stack.

Fees: 8–15% of total project revenue typical.

**Recommendation:** Code for Science & Society as primary candidate (mission fit) + Open Collective Foundation as backup (overhead-light). Apply to both in Y1; pick first acceptance.

**Y3 path: independent 501(c)(3).** Filing time 6–9 months. Triggered when annual revenue exceeds ~$500K (fiscal-sponsor fees become material).

### International incorporation

Per `05_future_inference.md` recommendation (EU-primary hosting):
- **Estonia e-Residency + Estonian non-profit** (MTÜ) — fastest international path; EU jurisdiction; digital-first government. Mirror operations.
- **Netherlands Stichting** — durable for sensitive press work; strong press-freedom protections.
- **Iceland incorporation** — Wikileaks precedent; protective press laws (IMMI). Use for highest-risk investigations only.

US 501(c)(3) remains primary for fundraising scale; EU mirror for jurisdictional resilience. The 2010 Wikileaks banking blockade lesson: **never let one jurisdiction kill the platform**.

### Anti-SLAPP exposure

Per `03_adversarial_architecture.md`: 40 US states + DC have anti-SLAPP statutes; **no federal anti-SLAPP**. Fifth/Seventh/Tenth/Eleventh/DC Circuits strip state anti-SLAPP in federal diversity jurisdiction — motivated plaintiffs forum-shop. Loomwork's US incorporation should be in a strong anti-SLAPP state (California, Texas, New York, DC) and Loomwork should refuse press contracts that waive anti-SLAPP rights.

### IP licensing

- **Code:** Apache 2.0 (patent-grant) for Loomwork engine; AGPL-3.0 for the public-render component (forces fork-publication).
- **Atoms / revelations:** **CC-BY 4.0** for revelations (attribution-required, derivative-allowed); **CC0** for raw atom data (public-good substrate).
- **Brand:** trademark Loomwork USPTO + EUIPO Y1 ($500–$2K).

### Insurance

- **Defamation E&O** (Errors & Omissions): MBO Partners, Hiscox, or Markel. $1M coverage, ~$3K–$10K/yr. Y1 requirement.
- **D&O** (Directors & Officers): once board exists, ~$2K–$5K/yr.
- **Cyber-liability:** $1M coverage, $2K–$5K/yr.
- **Press defense fund:** RCFP membership ($0–$500/yr) for case-by-case representation.

### Precedents
- **Wikileaks** — Iceland incorporation (IMMI), Bitcoin treasury, lesson: financial diversification saved them in 2010 banking blockade.
- **ProPublica** — Newark NJ 501(c)(3); strong board firewall; donor-disclosure model.
- **Bellingcat** — UK incorporation as Stichting initially, then UK CIC; lesson: jurisdictional fit per investigation type.
- **OCCRP** — US 501(c)(3) with Sarajevo operations; lesson: HQ jurisdiction != operations jurisdiction; lost funding crisis 2025 shows single-funder-dependency risk.

## Section 8 — The Telos Audit

Every partner, funder, and contractor relationship passes a telos audit before activation and annually thereafter.

### Five questions before any partnership

1. **Does this partner have any direct or transitive relationship to Loomwork's current investigation targets?** (If yes — defer or refuse.)
2. **Would this partnership require Loomwork to soften any of the 7 publication telos gates?** (If yes — refuse.)
3. **Is this partner's funding from a source that would itself fail this telos audit?** (If yes — escalate to board.)
4. **Does this partnership concentrate single-source dependency above 15% of revenue or 25% of any operational capability?** (If yes — refuse.)
5. **Would this partnership compromise Loomwork's source-protection or vulnerable-person-exposure gates?** (If yes — refuse without exception.)

### Three hard-no lines that override any funding

1. **No funder receives editorial preview** of any revelation, ever.
2. **No partner receives source-identifiable information** about whistleblowers/sources/defenders, ever.
3. **No relationship overrides the 7 publication telos gates** (vulnerable-person, libel, citation-retrievability, disinformation, pramana, confidence-floor, staleness).

### Annual review cadence

- Quarterly: funder concentration audit; conflict-of-interest disclosures from board + senior staff.
- Annually: published transparency report (every funder, every amount, every partnership type, every recusal triggered, every refusal log).
- Quarterly: **Refusal Report** with warrant-canary structure (per `03_adversarial_architecture.md` and `02_theory_of_change.md`).

### Public transparency

Wikipedia Foundation model: every donation >$50K disclosed publicly within 90 days. Mozilla model: published funder lists with amounts. ProPublica model: chartered editorial firewall published.

### Algedonic feedback for partner drift

When a partner relationship causes mission drift detection (algedonic signal from internal observation, external watchdog, or reader flag): immediate review by board chair + ombudsperson; partnership paused pending findings; no quiet drift.
